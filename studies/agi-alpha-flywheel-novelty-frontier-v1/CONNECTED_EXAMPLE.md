# One connected example, with each unpassed gate visible

**Offering considered:** a passive four-event chronology device whose final readout recovers the complete order of four
specified, distinct, once-only events after any one known readout position is erased. The illustrative beneficiary is
an operator handling valuable items whose disposition depends on event order, rather than merely whether an event
occurred. This is a proposed customer use, not an observed buyer requirement.

## 1. Opportunity and origin

The brief asked for an unsupplied productive possibility. The researchers proposed storing useful history in a passive
material and decoding it after partial readout loss. The brief did not name this application. The proposed connection
combined interacting mechanical memory, event-order encodings and readout fault tolerance. The subsequent literature
review found mechanical sequential computation, transition-graph synthesis, autonomous event-order recording and
classical error correction. The origin condition passed; the substantive novelty condition did not.

The full comparison and all seven serious candidates are in
[`discovery_record.json`](discovery/order-memory/discovery_record.json). A later internal-switching-confluence
refinement also matched conventional positive-feedback closure. It did not rescue the discovery claim.

## 2. Transformation witness

| Element | Preserved specification |
|---|---|
| Original useful purpose | Recover complete chronology, not merely event occurrence or one selected precedence relation |
| Binding restriction | A pairwise order code can lose its only distinction between adjacent event swaps when one position is erased |
| Proposed change | Add a parity constraint to the complete pairwise record; investigate a physical event-driven realization |
| Preserved obligations | All four-event orders; every one-erasure position including parity; correct surviving readout bits; fixed event semantics |
| Acceptance | Every required history has exactly one valid reconstruction after the specified erasure |
| Falsifiers | Any ambiguous allowed erasure; silent substitution under an asserted stronger fault claim; no physical realization; cost or buyer participation failure |
| Scope limit | One known erasure with otherwise correct bits; not arbitrary corruption, missing events, repeated events or unknown pulse overlap |

There are six pairwise comparison bits for four events. Their bare code uniquely recovers in 72 of 144 uniform
history/erasure cases. Adding one abstract XOR parity bit gives 168 of 168 correct recoveries, counting the parity
position as erasable. This is a familiar coding construction. Seven abstract stored bits are not seven fabricated
parts: event transduction, parity updates, fan-out, state, energy and reading remain physical engineering obligations.

## 3. Constructed capability and acceptance

The executable construction exhaustively checks the finite encoding. New-size confirmation at five and six events
passes 1,320/1,320 and 11,520/11,520 single-erasure cases. A separately implemented oracle audits 198,840 saved cases,
including two-erasure failures, with zero disagreement. Ordinary-library XOR repair matches the proposed retained
construction on all one-erasure cases. Transfer of executable structure is supported; superior retained competence is
not established.

The secondary hysteron construction distinguishes AB from BA after both inputs return to reference and includes every
allowed internal switching order. It also maps simultaneous C and repeated ABA/BAB to the same readout. This secondary
two-event instrument does not complete the four-event chronology offering. The paper does not sell it as if it did.

Thus a useful **abstract acceptance outcome** exists. Physical device acceptance and customer acceptance remain open.

## 4. Conditional economics for that same offering

The economic simulation assumes a physically qualified four-event device. It does not treat a logical proof, a
two-event latch or a business-plan document as an accepted delivered device. Hypothetical customer value is avoided
loss plus staff time saved, less false-alarm cost and the best modeled outside option. Customers participate only when
the effective price and prepayment burden fit their value threshold. Reachability, finite capacity and acquisition
cost limit volume.

This value mechanism remains a hypothesis. The assumed 70% avoided-loss fraction requires a specific customer action
after the readout, at a time when that action can still reduce downstream loss. Reading history cannot undo damage
that has already occurred. No such action, effect size or buyer willingness to pay was measured.

Ten prices and two payment policies are searched on 128 development worlds per candidate. Selected policies are
frozen before 512 independent-seed confirmation worlds per scenario. All worlds, including physical failure and losses,
are preserved. The model includes fabrication, materials, QA, packaging/shipping, support/recovery, acquisition,
overhead, warranty expense, credit loss and finance. Development includes failed screens once, physical tests and
external prototypes/tooling. Human engineering savings are separate sensitivity assumptions, not measured Alpha.

| Scenario | Frozen policy | Mean annual operating profit after assumed qualification | Expected development-plus-first-operating-year result |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Conservative | $4, net 30 days | −$73,910 | −$160,270 |
| Central | $5 list; 20% deposit with 5% discount | $20,979 | −$131,303 |
| Favorable | $8 list; 20% deposit with 5% discount | $417,868 | $171,609 |

Expected project result equals assumed physical-success probability times conditional annual operating profit, minus
originating development cost. The central assumed probability is 50%; favorable is 75%. They are scenario inputs,
not calibrated reliability estimates. Favorable confirmation still contains 137/512 project-loss worlds. The modeled profit is pre-tax over development plus one operating year. Cost completeness is conditional on broad
allowances: failed-prospect effort, yield/rework, equipment capacity, qualification, rights, insurance and exceptional
recalls remain unscoped. Support/recovery and warranty, and internal versus external testing, need distinct task
allocations to rule out overlap. Arithmetic consistency does not establish complete real cost accounting. Prices are
best among the tested policies under those inputs, not globally optimal real-world prices.

Cash is separate from profit. Deposits remain liabilities until assumed acceptance; loans are not revenue; a 3%
warranty reserve restricts spendable cash while the separate 2% warranty expense is charged once. Monthly timing is
coarse: deposit, production and assumed acceptance share a monthly bucket; a 30-day lead is used in buyer opportunity
cost. The independent Decimal replay checks the resulting cash arithmetic, not actual dated receipts.

**Actual commercial decision: HOLD.** No physical qualification or passed flagship novelty gate exists. Buyers,
deliveries and collections are zero. Total actual session expenditure remains unknown.

## 6. What a successor can inherit

An ordinary research successor can inherit public source, precise semantics, known failures, comparator requirements,
frozen inputs and reproducible outcomes. It must recheck the new mission's meaning and obtain fresh authority for any
production use. The experiment does not grant live `*.agent.agi.eth` access or transfer an independently qualified
specialist. Native validator roles remain unchanged.

The additional retention package is modeled at $2,500 extraction/validation plus $1,200 annual maintenance. Its measured
advantage over an identical-information library is zero in the finite control, so the $3,700 investment is rejected.
At ten future missions, it would need more than $370 of incremental savings per mission to cover that cost. Optional
Gym spending is zero. A positive operating margin would not require training; training would need its own evidence.

The next task is to specify and compare one history property beyond the cited conventional controls under equal
