## Abstract

An enterprise that explores the opportunity frontier needs both constructive imagination and a reliable way to discover when an attractive idea is already known. This study executes that combination. An adaptive search generated seven consequential opportunities absent from the supplied brief, screened them against primary prior art, and constructed two connected finite-model investigations of passive event-history memory. Exhaustive analysis exposes exactly when pairwise order records become ambiguous after an erasure. Classical parity repairs the abstract information loss, while a separate interacting-hysteron construction records two-event precedence with schedule-independent internal switching. Both constructions pass their declared computational checks. Neither establishes the substantive advance required for flagship selection: an ordinary library ties the parity construction, established monotone closure ties the hysteron construction, and the original four-event physical offering remains unqualified.

A conditional economic model makes the development decision concrete. Under favorable assumed demand, cost allowances and a 75% physical-qualification probability, the selected policy yields $417,868 mean annual operating profit conditional on qualification and $171,609 expected pre-tax project profit after central development cost. The customer action producing avoided loss and the completeness of real costs remain unvalidated. Central assumptions produce a $131,303 expected project loss; 137 of 512 favorable project worlds also lose money. Actual collections are zero and actual complete research expenditure is unknown. Native Insight, Nova-Seed, FusionPlan and authority workflows preserve the research while refusing unsupported venture promotion. The result is an executable, inspectable search boundary and a better specified next experiment. No candidate earns the flagship claim in this edition; beyond-human foresight, Operational Alpha and economically positive retained Alpha remain unestablished.

## 1. What becomes possible when capability can construct its own tests?

The productive ambition is to turn an overlooked possibility into a useful capability, an accepted outcome and a viable enterprise. A transformation must survive the strongest relevant existing approach, preserve the customer's purpose and carry its costs. Agents can connect fields, construct executable objects and expose counterexamples; whether they discover opportunities beyond ordinary search is a separate empirical question.

This edition delivers a complete bounded research loop around a particularly revealing possibility: a material that preserves the order of events without a continuously powered logger. The search connected event-history memory, physical hysteresis and information recovery. Those connections were generated during the study; the user did not supply them as the answer. The decisive finding is that the initial information problem has an exact conventional repair, while the later physical abstraction has an exact conventional explanation. A promising story therefore becomes a documented rejection rather than an inflated invention claim. [1, 8–15, 31, 32]

The useful achievement combines executable acceptance boundaries, a same-offering commercial model and native workflows that preserve research while denying unsupported venture promotion. These improve the research record and its reproducibility; they do not constitute the requested headline breakthrough. [31–34]

The economic possibility is meaningful but conditional. In the favorable scenario, approximately 96,034 devices sold during twelve production months support $729,856 gross revenue and $417,868 operating profit after the modeled recurring costs. The same scenario requires physical qualification, sufficient buyer value and roughly $173,548 mean prefunding for development and peak operating credit. The physical product is unbuilt, actionable customer benefit and cost completeness are unvalidated, and the current decision is **HOLD**. Section 7 explains why positive operating economics can coexist with a negative development decision. [33]

## 5. Exact information recovery: a useful construction with conventional parity

### 5.1 Why the original record fails

For $n$ distinct, exactly-once events, encode a permutation using all $m=n(n-1)/2$ pairwise precedence bits. A bit records whether one named event preceded another. All bits together identify the permutation. However, exchanging two adjacent events changes exactly their mutual comparison and leaves every other pairwise comparison unchanged. The resulting code therefore has minimum Hamming distance one. Every history has at least one vulnerable erasure. This is an elementary argument, not a claim of a new coding theorem. [12, 31]

For a fixed history, precisely $n-1$ of its $m$ pairwise comparisons join adjacent events. Erasing one of these comparisons leaves the two neighboring orders indistinguishable. Erasing a nonadjacent comparison leaves a chain of intervening events that implies it. Under a uniform choice of the erased pair, the exact ambiguous fraction is therefore $2/n$, and the uniquely recoverable fraction is $1-2/n$. This distributional statement differs from a worst-case guarantee: the bare code fails that guarantee at every tested size. [31]

A four-cycle pairwise graph produces only 14 distinct readouts for 24 histories. All six pairwise comparisons distinguish all 24 intact histories, but uniquely recover only 72 of 144 history/erasure cases. This abstract graph calculation is not a correction to the mechanics or experimental claims of the published periodic metamaterial. It isolates a declared information model inspired by that literature. [8, 31]

![Exact recovery fractions for bare pairwise readouts and conventional parity](../figures/figure-1-erasure-recovery.pdf)

*Figure 1. Exhaustive abstract-code results. Bare pairwise records recover 50%, 60% and 66.67% of uniformly enumerated single-erasure cases at n = 4, 5 and 6. Standard XOR parity recovers all declared cases. The conventional identical-information library comparator ties on the new-size checks; a separate bitset oracle independently verifies the saved recovery sets. These are finite computations, not physical-device reliability measurements.*

### 5.2 Repair, resource bounds and negative cases

Append one bit equal to the XOR of the $m$ pairwise bits. When one known bit is erased, the surviving parity equation determines it; if parity itself is erased, the original pairwise record remains intact. At four events, the augmented abstract code recovers all 168 history/erasure cases. This is classical parity applied to a new research connection. Its correctness does not make the coding principle novel. [12, 31]

A duplication baseline requires $2m$ stored bits within the restricted class that stores only copies of individual pairwise comparisons. Every pair can be adjacent in some history, so each must have at least two copies to survive an arbitrary single erasure. The parity code uses $m+1$ logical bits. This comparison counts logical readouts; it is not a lower bound for arbitrary encodings or a proof of lower physical cost. A physical parity element could require couplers, ancillas, routing, fabrication tolerances, energy or readout mechanisms absent from the logical count.

![Two orders differ only at their adjacent comparison and are separated by parity](../figures/figure-2-adjacent-swap.pdf)

*Figure 2. ABCD and BACD differ only in the AB comparison. Removing that comparison makes their bare readouts identical. An additional conventional parity bit separates the two abstract codewords. The diagram identifies the information requirement; it does not supply a fabricated parity mechanism.*

The source and configuration were frozen before fresh-size enumeration. At five events, bare recovery is 720/1,200 versus parity recovery of 1,320/1,320. At six events, the corresponding results are 7,200/10,800 and 11,520/11,520. The denominators differ because the augmented code has one additional erasable position. A separate bitset-intersection implementation checked all 198,840 enumerated recovery cases without disagreement; another check verified 24,000 duplication cases. The ordinary library containing the identical coding information ties on all 12,840 single-erasure cases at the new sizes. [31]

The preserved failures delimit the result. With two erasures, 1,560 of 6,600 five-event cases and 13,680 of 86,400 six-event cases remain ambiguous under parity. All 12,840 tested single-bit substitutions are detected, but a single parity check cannot generally correct an unknown flipped bit. Erasing parity while flipping AB can silently change ABCDE(F) into BACDE(F); flipping AB and AC can produce BCADE(F) with parity still satisfied. These cases lie outside the promised one-known-erasure model and are retained so that successors cannot quietly generalize the guarantee.

The new-size checks establish implementation consistency on fresh finite tasks. The general coding argument and the four-event design were already known before their execution. Accordingly, this is technical transfer of a known procedure, not learned independent competence, prospective foresight or numerical retained Alpha.

## 6. A second construction: deterministic internal races, order-sensitive inputs

### 6.1 Exact hysteron model

The next search refinement sought a physically interpretable distinction: internal switching order should not alter the result, while the order of external events should. The constructed model contains binary states $s_i$, a held input vector $h$, and effective fields

$$
H_i(s,h)=h_i+\sum_j C_{ij}s_j.
$$

An inactive bit may switch on when $H_i\geq1.00$; an active bit may switch off when $H_i\leq-0.80$. Every allowed single-bit update choice is explored. No update priority is used to manufacture determinism. Nominal calculations use exact integer-scaled values. [32]

For the four-bit state $(x,y,z,w)$, only $C_{xz}=C_{zx}=0.60$ and $C_{yw}=C_{wy}=0.60$ are nonzero. Pulse A is $(1.20,0,0,0.60)$, and pulse B is $(0,1.20,0.60,0)$. Each is held until settling and followed by zero input. C holds A+B simultaneously; R applies $(-2,-2,-2,-2)$ until settling and then returns to zero. Two- and three-bit projections are evaluated as separate models. An independent Boolean recurrence supplies the semantic oracle:

$$
x'=x\lor a,\quad y'=y\lor b,\quad
z'=z\lor[b\land(x\lor a)],\quad
w'=w\lor[a\land(y\lor b)].
$$

R clears the state. Under the declared exactly-once two-event interpretation, AB produces 1110 and BA produces 1101. C, ABA and BAB all produce 1111. The collisions are central: the model cannot reconstruct full history or uniquely identify simultaneous events. Arbitrary finite-duration overlaps, thermal activation and physical switching dynamics are outside its settled-input abstraction. [32]

![Order-sensitive readouts with explicit simultaneous and repeated-history collisions](../figures/figure-3-confluence-collisions.pdf)

*Figure 3. Four-bit abstract hysteron readouts distinguish AB from BA, while C, ABA and BAB collide at 1111. Every allowed internal update schedule agrees within the declared model. A conventional monotone-closure implementation gives identical results. This partial event-order construction does not satisfy the original four-event single-erasure offering.*

### 6.2 Why it works, and why it does not earn novelty

Under A, B, C and zero, an active bit cannot switch off because fields are nonnegative and the off threshold is negative. Every on-switch can only increase neighboring fields. The process is therefore monotone on a finite Boolean lattice and reaches the same least fixed point under every maximal asynchronous update sequence. R forces all bits off. With symmetric couplings, the coarse energy

$$
E(s;h)=-\sum_i h_i s_i-\sum_{i<j}C_{ij}s_i s_j
$$

strictly decreases at every allowed fixed-input switch. This supports finite-model consistency; it supplies neither a continuous elastic energy landscape nor a manufactured device. Different external vector paths can still yield different final states. Internal confluence does not imply that AB and BA commute, and this result does not overturn scalar return-point-memory theory. [13, 32]

The confirmation includes all 140 nominal initial-state/held-field cases across two, three and four bits; 5,461 words of length zero through six per model over A/B/C/R; and 1,024 fresh seeded words of length one through 64 per model. There are no oracle disagreements or nominal nonconfluent cases. Parameter checks use 512 draws per dimension, perturbing present symmetric edges, thresholds and nonzero drives by at most 0.04, giving 71,680 state/field checks without disagreement. Analytic interval margins also cover the continuous specified perturbation box; the smallest required gated-activation margin is 0.08. These are model checks, not independent physical trials or estimated real failure rates. [32]

Unfavorable controls remain executable. Reducing each coupling from 0.60 to 0.35 destroys order discrimination. An antagonistic pair terminates in either 01 or 10 depending on update order. An asymmetric feedback pair cycles. The checker reports these behaviors. Eight acceptance tests pass, including the negative controls.

The novelty verdict follows from stronger comparisons. Conventional monotone closure matches the proposed dynamics exactly. Mechanical counting, pattern recognition and reset already exist; systematic transition-graph synthesis already exists; vectorial sequential memory and autonomous event-order recorders are identified prior art. The autonomous recorder comparison is especially useful because its first-occurrence semantics are different from, and in some respects stronger than, this recurrent-precedence state. Direct full text was unavailable for the recent vectorial-memory preprint, so no detailed performance comparison to it is asserted. A working threshold motif earns a reference construction, not a new discovery claim. [9, 10, 13–15, 32]

### 7.2 Buyer participation and the best tested arrangement

For each simulated buyer, device value combines assumed avoided losses and staff savings, less false-alarm costs. The model assigns 70% prevented-loss benefit, but a passive record read after an event cannot undo its damage. A downstream decision—such as disposition, future prevention or adjudication—and its timing must be specified before this becomes calibrated value. A manual outside option receives its own assumed loss avoidance, staff saving and $3 per-unit cost. Incremental value is the positive difference between device value and the best modeled outside option. Buyers participate only when effective price plus the financing cost of any deposit is at most 70% of that incremental value. The remaining surplus supplies an explicit participation margin; the rule is an assumption, not an elicited demand curve or a truthful mechanism. [33]

The tested list-price grid is $2, $3, $4, $5, $6, $8, $10, $12, $16 and $20. Payment policies are net 30 or a 20% deposit with a 5% price discount. Deposit financing is charged to buyers at an assumed 10% annual rate for thirty days. The supplier's credit-loss model distinguishes postpaid exposure. A lower effective price can increase participation while a deposit reduces financing pressure; raising prices never holds demand artificially fixed.

Development uses 128 worlds. Policies were saved before a fresh confirmation seed generated 512 worlds for each of three scenarios. Capacity is capped at 120,000 devices annually. Potential buyers number 18, 40 or 70, with central per-buyer volumes of 2,000, 3,000 or 3,500 respectively; reachability, buyer value, demand, volume and unit cost vary under the declared distributions. ### 7.4 Robustness, losses and the development decision

At the favorable base assumptions, the break-even qualification probability is approximately 0.339. This threshold holds the modeled operating distribution and central development cost fixed. Central operating economics would require $q>1$ to repay that development cost within the modeled horizon. Higher R&D costs can overturn the favorable case: the high E0 development estimate produces approximately −$263,655 expected project profit; even an assumed 50% engineering-hour reduction leaves it negative. [33]

An exploratory demand/cost grid keeps the selected policies fixed. Twenty-four of 42 favorable grid cells have both positive expected project profit and a positive fifth-percentile annual operating profit; no central or conservative cell does. At half base demand and unchanged unit cost, favorable annual operating profit remains approximately $160,565, yet expected project profit becomes −$21,368. At 75% demand and 1.5 times unit cost, expected project profit is about $41,179. These bounds clarify which economic assumptions deserve early calibration. They are not a demonstrated robust market region. [33]

A same-team independent Decimal audit checked 16,968 identities or comparisons across all 1,536 confirmation worlds; the saved reconstructed auditor adds 12,543 numeric checks. Residuals remain below $0.000001. The original inline command was not preserved; its reconstructed source and changed rounding residual are explicitly documented. This validates arithmetic, failure preservation and physical-work invariance, not actionable benefit, real cost completeness or disjoint cost scope. The model-review record preserves these material limitations without changing the frozen results. [33] All actual decisions remain **HOLD: unqualified physical offering and failed novelty gate**. Avoiding future expenditure does not erase sunk research cost.
