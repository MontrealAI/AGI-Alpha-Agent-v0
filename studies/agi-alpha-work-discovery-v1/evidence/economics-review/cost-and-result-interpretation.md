# Economic interpretation and cost provenance

## Supported result

The frozen campaign completed 384 paired worlds and 1,536 counterfactual service policies. It produced **no
positive ordinary operating surplus, no recovered project cost, no affordable admitted capability investment and
no modeled self-financing succession**. Available investment funds stayed zero in every week of every world.
The four policies have identical market outcomes because the first investment gate leaves them with retained
conventional A. This is a negative conditional result, not evidence that the broader opportunity is absent.

Actual collections remain **USD 0**. All buyer arrivals, willingness to pay, prices, labor durations and cash
collections in this section are model conditions. The measured inputs are public-data execution resources and
acceptance/source-availability records. Actual complete monetary cost and residual human effort remain unknown.

Primary evidence is in [paper-summary.json](../../outputs/economics-primary/paper-summary.json),
[diagnostics.json](../../outputs/economics-primary/diagnostics.json),
[policy-summaries.ndjson](../../outputs/economics-primary/policy-summaries.ndjson) and
[completion.json](../../outputs/economics-primary/completion.json). The protocol/source freeze SHA-256 is
`420fb1aaa593d37c8c14e2d802f75c5541bdfcf686f6b62238f48580c2e7fc79`; normalized calibration is
`68c4ca13c2671fe77297fbeec09a11425170ae0a43a03158a1d66bae51c54995`.

## Pricing, availability and the limits of the market result

The calibrated expected collected-job probability is
`0.5 × (1 − 0.02²) × 0.98 = 0.489804`. The first factor is the declared source-availability condition, the second
is the assumed eventual technical acceptance after at most two attempts, and the third is collection probability.
Prices are derived before market outcomes at three intended service orders/week. Equal competitors use exactly
the same calculation and grid; equal paid offers are split by a common seeded draw.

| Cost condition | Expected prospective cost/intended order | Cost/expected collected job | Fixed tariff |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Low | $7.58 | $15.48 | **$16.35** |
| Reference | $17.19 | $35.10 | **$65.38** |

These include allocated ordinary setup, but exclude historical research and subsequent investment from the price
allocation; those remain costs in the ledger. Actual realized unit cost rises when service orders fall below the
pricing utilization assumption.

The reference tariff jumps from the $32.69 grid level to $65.38 when cost reaches $35.10. This exceeds even the
maximum modeled buyer willingness to pay. Thus reference-case refusal partly reflects the declared coarse pricing
rule. It is not evidence that no continuous price or other customer segment could support this useful work.
No alternative pricing campaign was run after seeing the outcomes.

The strong competitor dominates the ordinary offering in this model. In low-cost equal-competitor conditions,
some buyers do purchase simulated accepted outputs, but copying and lower prices from week 20, limited eligible
demand, input failures and recurring overhead prevent surplus. Nonzero integration can also discourage switching
for an already onboarded buyer, which explains why some 15-minute cells lose less than their zero-integration
counterparts. That is a consequence of the declared one-job buyer choice model, not measured customer retention.

## Observed resources and investment comparison

| Arm | Conditional available-case service CPU | Standalone CPU/accepted output, including all planned failures and common costs |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| A, retained independent conventional | 0.424909 s | 1.547223 s |
| B, full Workbench | 1.552730 s | 2.675044 s |
| C, incremental plus registry | 1.768245 s | 2.890559 s |
| D, incremental plus graph | 1.621214 s | 2.743529 s |

Per intended job, the original experimental acquisition share is 0.139529 CPU seconds. Standalone accounting
restores four-arm sharing and adds 0.001440 seconds of acquisition-session overhead, for 0.559554 acquisition
seconds, plus 0.001600 driver-overhead seconds. These common costs are charged once; the original and restored
allocations are not both added.

The signed conditional CPU saving is **−1.127821 s for A→B**, **−0.215515 s for B→C**, and **−0.068484 s for B→D**.
At the assumed CPU tariff, these do not produce a positive incremental saving even before construction or
maintenance. They cannot justify either primary investment. The measured D-versus-C difference of +0.147031 s
is one timing diagnostic, not an established general graph advantage. Its conservative CPU valuation is
$0.000040/use, smaller than the assumed graph maintenance increment.

Even with zero graph maintenance, the low graph-only fixed cost requires 1,152,500 uses on the unrounded
calculator, or 1,159,000 with the 26-week rounding provision. The reference figures are 3,655,500 and 3,662,000.
With declared maintenance, no finite break-even exists. No admitted investment has a repayment time because no
investment occurred.

The attention thresholds in the diagnostic are explicitly unmeasured. At 156 hypothetical reuses and default
maintenance, the first asset would need an additional approximately 0.90/3.15 provider minutes saved per use in
the low/reference budgets, besides its CPU result, to cover construction. This is a condition for a future matched
attention experiment, not a credited benefit or a funded trajectory. The threshold table excludes the separate
one-cent-per-week rounding provision, so it is a lower-bound diagnostic, not the strict admission rule.

## Full-cost result and best-case reconciliation

All twelve reference-cost cells make zero enterprise sales and become insolvent in week 20. Nineteen weeks of
accrued operating cost total $509.20; opening cost is $502.63. Total loss is $1,011.83, of which $11.83 remains an
unpaid obligation after the $1,000 seed is exhausted. Cash never goes negative.

Low-cost strong-competitor cells make zero enterprise sales, accrue $448.76 of recurring cost and lose $573.18
including opening costs. Low-cost equal-competitor cells have nonzero simulated delivery, but remain negative.
The accompanying machine-readable file and 24-cell table preserve every cell rather than selecting a favorable
subset.

equal competition, low cost, seed 1. It still fails:

It receives 46 intended service orders, accepts 27 outputs and blocks 19 on unavailable sources. The modeled
customer savings total $774.72 under the human-effort assumptions. Those savings are not enterprise revenue;
the enterprise still loses money. This illustrates why cheap computation and a useful output alone do not close
the productive investment loop.

The concrete missing conditions are sustained demand at a price covering complete operating cost after competing
alternatives and input failures, and a separately demonstrated capability benefit large enough to repay its full
incremental cost. The study establishes useful scoped public-data artifacts and a reproducible negative test of
this particular financial succession. It does not establish paid demand, actual full-cost savings, valuable transfer
to an already capable recipient, graph superiority or a self-financing Flywheel.
