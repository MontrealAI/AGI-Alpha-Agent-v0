## Economics

**arrow1962.** Original chapter downloaded from NBER and locally read, particularly pp. 614--616, section "Information as a Commodity". Official URL: https://www.nber.org/system/files/chapters/c2144/c2144.pdf. The paper distinguishes the value of useful information from the difficulty of selling it under cheap reproduction; distribution of an existing stock and incentives for producing new knowledge are different allocation questions. This paper cannot claim that the idea of valuable knowledge with weak private capture is new. The 1959 RAND version exists, but the bibliography cites the verified 1962 chapter, pp. 609--626.

**teece1986.** Original published paper read through a university-professor-hosted scan: https://www.edegan.com/pdfs/ERBC/Stern,%205.28.11/Teece_1986.pdf. Publisher metadata: https://doi.org/10.1016/0048-7333(86)90027-2. Especially pp. 285--290: appropriability, dominant design, and complementary assets explain why an innovator may lose to a follower; generic, specialized and cospecialized complements are distinct. A simulated instrument is a complementary resource, but merely naming it does not show a durable exclusive asset or profit. Test substitutability, rebuilding and capacity additions explicitly. Do not claim a new economic law or that complements necessarily guarantee rents.

**romer1990.** Published original PDF read at https://web.stanford.edu/~klenow/Romer_1990.pdf; DOI https://doi.org/10.1086/261725. Particularly pp. S73--S74 and S77--S78: rivalry and excludability are distinct, and nonrival instructions can be combined with rival inputs. Use this distinction to separate source-code copyability from instrument occupancy and access restrictions. The experiment is not a test of Romer's aggregate growth model and cannot support macroeconomic growth estimates.

**jonestonetti2020.** Published original read at https://web.stanford.edu/~chadj/JonesTonetti_DataNonrivalry.pdf. Metadata confirmed by https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257/aer.20191330. Existing data can be reused across firms, while ownership incentives and privacy can affect allocation. The relevant contribution boundary is careful separation of public benefits and producer rents. Their modeled welfare magnitudes must not be imported as estimates for Nova-Seeds. Using synthetic inputs here avoids claiming that actual private data can simply be disclosed.

**hagiuwright2023.** Final publication metadata verified at https://doi.org/10.1111/1756-2171.12453. Author-hosted draft read at https://www.wrighteconomics.com/_files/ugd/c6ffe6_1490e06103de41db98494627418c35d8.pdf (dated December 2021; earlier than final publication). Their model separates learning across customers from learning within the same customer and examines how learning functions, accumulation, and beliefs affect competitive advantage. Relevant warning: an initial data lead is not automatically persistent. Our equal-information test deliberately removes this asymmetry; it does not establish that real-world data advantages never persist.

## Verification, authority, and calibration

**bender2013.** Full original author-hosted paper read at https://faculty.knox.edu/dbunde/pubs/ise.pdf; author landing page https://faculty.knox.edu/dbunde/pubs/ise.html. Their unit-time model schedules release/deadline-constrained jobs within calibration validity windows while minimizing calibration count; a single-machine version has an optimal polynomial-time algorithm. Their calibration is instantaneous. Calibration batching is prior art. A new artifact may change the objective, activation time or evidence layer, but must state those differences and include a competent comparator rather than pretend FIFO exhausts the alternatives.

**neculalee1996 / necula1997.** Original OSDI paper available as full HTML at https://www.usenix.org/legacy/publications/library/proceedings/osdi96/full_papers/necula/html/index.html; 1997 POPL publication metadata and author's account verified at https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~necula/papers.html and https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~necula/pcc.html. Formal policy checking of program properties differs from signatures authenticating statements. A Python predicate that checks a simulation trace is a useful executable validator, not automatically a formally proved PCC system. The paper should credit the lineage while using the precise claim "independently checkable execution receipts" or "model-checked trace predicates" as implemented.

**whitehead2004.** Original author-hosted paper read at https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~necula/Papers/sigpcc_csfw04.pdf, especially p. 236. It explicitly combines semantic reasoning with authorization logic and signed statements. This supports separating policy compliance from who has authority to endorse or execute. A signature alone cannot exclude mistakes in signed content. Mission-specific policy and authority checks should be visible and fresh. The bibliography intentionally omits a DOI because retrieved index records disagreed; author-hosted original identifies title, authors, proceedings, year and pages directly.

**nisttraceability2021 / nistinterval2026.** Official text read at https://www.nist.gov/metrology/metrological-traceability and https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/recommended-calibration-interval. The original PDF https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/TechnicalNotes/NIST.TN.2156.pdf was downloaded to verify author order (Possolo, Bruce, Watters; the metadata landing page lists a different order). Calibration traceability does not itself establish fitness for purpose; uncertainty must meet the application need. NIST does not prescribe one universal interval. Therefore synthetic validity windows are model assumptions, not proof of instrument fitness, real calibration certification, or NIST endorsement.

**rfc8032 / dworkin2007.** Official primitive references at https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8032 and https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/38/d/final. Cite only if actually implemented. Ed25519 signatures authenticate messages; AES-GCM provides authenticated encryption under the required key/nonce discipline. Neither makes a falsely measured input true. A known-key demonstration tests implementation behavior, not confidentiality against a reader possessing the disclosed key. The NIST page currently notes a planned revision; cite the actual 2007 specification used, not an invented newer edition.

## Defensible contribution statement

Suggested scope: "We construct a reproducible calibration-aware allocation service, with independently checkable mission-scoped receipts, and subject the complete disclosed service to matched replication, substitution, drift, and registry-removal tests. The contribution is the implemented cross-layer experiment and its measured boundaries: productive benefit relative to named alternatives, cost of rebuilding/verifying, and residual advantage against a fully equipped comparator. We do not introduce a new theory of appropriability, new scheduling complexity result, or new cryptographic primitive."
