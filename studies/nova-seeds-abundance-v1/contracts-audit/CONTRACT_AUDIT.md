## Trust model for the counterfactual

An off-chain Ed25519-signed registry can bind the same disclosed capsule/plan/evidence bytes, parent lineage, policy-scoped authorizations and route receipts while running the exact same productive workload. A public key must be pinned independently. Signature verification establishes authorship and unchanged bytes under that key, not truth or completeness. A single registry can equivocate, censor or omit records unless witnesses or external anchors are added. Hash chaining makes alteration detectable to a holder of a later signed checkpoint, but does not itself prevent a dishonest operator from maintaining divergent histories.

A local EVM receipt demonstrates deterministic execution against that local chain's state. It does not inherit public-chain consensus or independent physical measurement. Actual public-chain anchoring could provide shared ordering and escrow enforcement under its consensus/dependency assumptions, with costs beyond those measured in an in-process EVM. The study must distinguish functional equality of the productive workload and evidence binding from stronger decentralization or custody properties.
