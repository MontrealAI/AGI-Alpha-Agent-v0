## Moving Beta and net Alpha

Beta is the best available competent unrestricted reconstruction baseline, including current frontier reasoning, tools and any capabilities already equivalent at a recipient. Future Beta version times and improvement magnitudes are scenario assumptions, not predictions about an announced future model. A new version can shrink or erase the economic advantage of a retained artifact. New access and compatibility tests must use the current version, not a frozen weak baseline.

Prospective net Alpha compares a fresh deployment/use with and without the retained capability after all remaining distribution, acquisition, integration, validation, maintenance, revalidation, obsolescence and composition costs. Initial graph admission requires positive expected full-cycle net Alpha, including construction and assessment. A previously admitted asset can be retained when its remaining prospective value is positive even if realized historical cost recovery remains negative. Forecast full-cycle admission value is not realized recovery: complete construction recovery remains a separate reported result. Neither graph membership nor agreeing attestations supplies correctness or economic value by itself.

The demo replays serialized outputs of the executable local lifecycle checks. Its selectors choose actual cases and steps; they do not fabricate reviewers, attestations, skill transfers or new evidence. The assignment solver remains a separate useful-artifact demonstration and does not establish live model invention, real network operation or market demand.

## Component assessment units and the economic extrapolation boundary

The $48 reevaluation unit is one batch of **16 paired bounded component-oracle cases and eight recipient-transfer checks**, using 480 critical-workbench minutes, 1,200 billed experimental-container minutes, 800,000 Astra input tokens, 160,000 Astra output tokens, 2,000,000 Terra input tokens and 200,000 Terra output tokens. All quantities are assumptions. It does not pay for 16 pairs of complete buyer projects.

Initial campaigns pay 12 (R), 24 (V) or 36 (bundle) $201.70 component-evaluation units. These finite budgets cover candidate screening, unsuccessful designs, adversarial investigation and the final configured 24 paired component cases plus eight scoped transfer checks. The allocation to these activities is assumed; no live frontier evaluation was observed. Construction and integration charges remain separate.

The economic simulation may observe synthetic component correctness, reconstruction cost and recipient compatibility. Full-project acceptance, semantic accuracy, buyer value, collection and net economic benefit are **conditional model extrapolations**, not observed end-to-end paired outcomes. Passing component tests does not calibrate those unknowns. If a later empirical study treats cases as complete project trials, it must bill both complete workflows and their actual correction, revalidation and failure costs, and consume all corresponding tokens, tools and capacity.
