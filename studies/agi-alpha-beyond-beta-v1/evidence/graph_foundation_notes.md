## Executable local lifecycle

`sim/graph_lifecycle.py` implements candidate → attached paid evidence → incremental Alpha assessment → eligible validator attestation → admission → network discovery/acquisition/use → paid reassessment, supersession, retirement or revocation. Every stored record binds its payload commitment, version, scope, models, dependencies, current Beta and current policy. Evidence and attestations expire. An admitted composition requires its own paid validation evidence, and dependency ids, versions and commitments are checked transitively on reuse.

The graph admits a **new** Alpha candidate only when its evidence-conditioned expected full-cycle net value is positive, including construction and assessment. A record already admitted earlier may be retained after paid reassessment on positive prospective value: sunk historical costs remain visible but are not charged again as a reason to discard a useful residual asset. An initially rejected candidate cannot label itself “retention” to omit those costs; `ever_admitted` is controlled by the graph. Whole-enterprise realized-model profit, investment recovery and incremental policy value must still be computed from the separate finite cash ledger. Admission is neither profit nor proof of eventual repayment.

For component-test assessment, `component_test_passed` means a modeled compiler, checker or compatibility check passed. It is not customer acceptance of a full deployment. Per-project net values carry `MODEL_EXTRAPOLATION_FROM_COMPONENT_TESTS` and remain conditional projections through an uncertain mapping. Raw paired test counts, clustering groups, modeled monetary gains, an explicit zero-centred eight-pseudo-case prior, remaining costs and full-cycle costs are reported separately. Prior pseudo-cases are not performed tests. The descriptive normal uncertainty interval is not a calibrated frontier-performance posterior and is not an extra admission hurdle.

Every eligible shareable admitted asset is discoverable to authenticated members within scope. Private retention is creator-only. Already-equivalent or incompatible recipients do not receive an acquisition gain. A shareable composition cannot expose a private dependency. All these checks are a **simulated access and authority policy**; no real ENS identity is authenticated.

The old `sim/graph_admission.py` is preserved unchanged. The new lifecycle adds authority and economic assessment without changing existing AGI Jobs `*.club.agi.eth` roles, contracts, governance, settlement or execution authority. Access to a capability is separate from permission to perform an external action.

## Identity and permission meanings

Only an exact direct ASCII label of the form `name.agent.agi.eth` is eligible for the synthetic registry. Nested names, suffix matches, `*.club.agi.eth`, trailing characters and an unauthenticated matching name fail. This deliberately restricted profile is not a full ENS normalization implementation.

Creator identity does not grant record-writing authority. Writers need an explicit `write_for` grant. Assessors, validators, admission actors, governance actors and members have distinct permissions. A validator must be eligible for the recorded scope; distinct identities do not establish statistical independence. Policies, policy hashes, Beta versions and authority registries expose read-only copies. Authorized policy/Beta/authority revisions stale existing assessments and require fresh evidence. Terminal records cannot be restored through canonical pointers, old events or reassessment.

Members may retrieve ciphertext and metadata, acquire, invoke an approved executor, or compose within granted scope. Plaintext export is denied by default and requires both an explicit policy grant and member permission. The execution API accepts only a preinstalled trusted executor identifier, not an arbitrary caller callback that could return capsule bytes. A separate action-authorization argument is required. Trusted executor installation and privileged access to the Python process remain outside this research access-policy threat model.

## Actual cryptography and limits

The optional local vault executes AES-256-GCM encryption and decryption with generated synthetic keys and random 96-bit nonces. SHA-256 commits ciphertext; a random salt inside the authenticated envelope binds the plaintext. Associated data binds the supplied context. The installed runtime was `cryptography 50.0.2`, with the established API checked against the [official PyCA documentation](https://cryptography.io/en/latest/hazmat/primitives/aead/). Tests execute a round trip and reject changed ciphertext even after its ordinary hash is recomputed, the wrong key, and the wrong context.

Keys stay in local test-process memory and are never exported in receipts. This is actual local cryptography over synthetic bytes, not proof of production key custody, process isolation, an enclave, real identity authentication, on-chain execution, a ZK proof, or protection from a privileged process owner. There is no real credential, wallet, transaction or security-configuration change. The graph uses SHA-256 content and event commitments, and does not claim the full typed Merkle forest or Ethereum protocol described by the foundation manuscripts.

Revocation blocks later authorized graph access. It cannot delete ciphertext already copied or erase plaintext that a member was explicitly allowed to export. The test suite preserves an exported copy after revocation to demonstrate this limit. Execution outputs may also reveal information inherent in an authorized function; the study does not claim cryptographic prevention of all inference or extraction.

## Test receipts and reproducibility

Run `python sim/test_graph_lifecycle.py`. It writes `qa/graph_lifecycle_audit.json` and `results/graph_lifecycle_cases.json`. These contain actual local test results and the exact stage/event traces, with source hashes. The fifteen adversarial cases include positive admission and retention, redundant/economically negative candidates, uncertainty, direct-name checks, distinct authority, stale evidence, moving Beta, versioned policies, terminal history, paid composition and transitive revocation, supersession, private dependencies, attestation binding, local encryption, and export-copying limits. The bilingual demonstration replays these executed cases; its display does not generate evidence.

These checks establish bounded behavior of the local research implementation. They do not establish that an autonomous frontier agent discovered the capabilities, that paid component tests predict full-project performance, that any sale occurred, that a network has deployed this access policy, or that capability growth exceeds a moving Beta in the real world.
