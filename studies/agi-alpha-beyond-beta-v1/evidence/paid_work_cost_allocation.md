# Paid work, incremental retention and single-charge allocation

The active additive register is `workflow_costs_v3.json`. Versions 1 and 2 remain unchanged. Official model and tool tariffs are the previously verified 26 October 2026 rates. Every workload quantity below is an assumption. No customer payment, frontier production trace or deployed transfer is observed.

## The originating commercial job is charged its full workflow

The base complete first attempt costs **$263.46** at the register’s unit activity level. This includes demanding frontier formulation, construction and experiments, independent semantic validation, fresh tests and reproducible customer handoff. All actual retries, failed attempts, up to two correction/revalidation rounds, intake, recovery, support, storage and business costs remain charged as defined by the simulator. The realized cost of a paid job can therefore exceed the base amount. When a recipient already has an equivalent capability, the declared reconstruction activity can be avoided; fresh reasoning remains charged.

## Only additional work is a retention investment

A validator-approved commercial job need not yield a candidate. Approval, payment authorization and actual collection are recorded separately. A candidate may emerge before collection or after failed authorization; the controller cannot use latent true correctness or future receipts. An invoked frontier emergence scan costs **$3.12 and 20 critical-workbench minutes**, including scans that find nothing useful. That scan identifies a scoped candidate and potential recipient gap; it does not establish admission, successful transfer or positive Alpha.

The resulting additional retention budgets, excluding separately invoked emergence scans, are **R $290.05 / 2,430 minutes; V $307.95 / 2,550 minutes; bundle $569.72 / 4,770 minutes**. The bundle shares extraction and packaging but pays one evaluation per component and an extra composition check. These are assumed metered resource envelopes, not optimized observed costs or fixed admission fees. A rejected, unsuccessful or obsolete candidate retains every cost actually incurred. The simulator must consume its billed tokens, experimental-container time and critical capacity as well as cash.

The evaluation unit remains a **component** unit: the configured 24 paired component cases and eight scoped transfer checks per component do not constitute 24 pairs of complete buyer engagements. Whole-project semantic correctness, acceptance, collections and net economic value are explicitly conditional model extrapolations. A future empirical full-project trial would need complete usage and recovery accounting for both arms.

## Transfer and upkeep remain additional

Per-unit costs are $1.45 for current-version access, $0.73 for distribution, $8.39 for recipient acquisition, and $4.17 for a genuinely new composition. Maintenance is $7.02 per asset-month; regular metadata/scope revalidation is $19.20 per invoked unit; a fresh Beta/component reevaluation batch is $48.00, with 16 paired component cases and eight transfer checks within 480 critical-workbench minutes and 1,200 billed container minutes. Revocation propagation is $1.05 per invoked unit. These tasks may stop at a failed gate; work already performed is paid. An existing-equivalent recipient does not obtain invented improvement credit.

Creator-side initial retained-package integration, a later recipient-specific integration and a later distinct composition can be different activities. Repeating the same named integration or composition record does not authorize another charge. Every version, recipient, scope and attempt must be explicit.

## Receipt-level invariant

Every expense has a globally unique `activity_id` within a simulated world and policy. The implemented record contains `activity_id`, `ledger`, `month`, `cost_key`, `cost_usd` and `cash_paid`, with job/candidate context and resource components where applicable. A reference to a previously paid job receipt has **zero additional charge**. The same activity cannot belong to two charged allocations. Job-level `receipt_id` values are provenance summaries, not additional charges. Attempt and ordinal fields distinguish retries from duplicated records.

Implemented identity patterns and exact fields are in `receipt_allocation_rules`. Expense activity IDs use `{ledger}:{scenario}:{world}:{policy}:{ordinal:06d}`; job provenance uses `job:{scenario}:{world}:{policy}:{project_id}`. The executable audit checks disjoint allocations against cash and resource ledgers. Unique IDs prove consistency of these simulated records; they do not establish real invoices or transactions.

## Alpha and enterprise profitability are different tests

The commercial job’s original construction is already included in the enterprise ledger. The marginal retain-or-discard decision therefore compares fresh eligible recipient benefit against **additional** extraction, generalization, evaluation, integration, distribution and remaining lifecycle costs. It does not recharge the sunk delivery process. It also does not erase that process from full enterprise profitability or from the human-service comparison.

The pre-spend capital decision compares expected benefit with all additional class B investment and future lifecycle costs. After expenditure is sunk, graph admission uses evidence-conditioned prospective gain after remaining costs; source-job A is excluded by matched provenance. Already-spent B remains in the construction-inclusive residual forecast and enterprise profit. That residual forecast is not realized historical repayment. Realized incremental recovery, useful fresh outcomes and enterprise net cash are reported separately. A positive capability increment cannot turn an unprofitable originating business into demonstrated commercial success by accounting convention.

Dedicated research remains an optional alternative with its original complete construction/attempt budgets and a separate ledger. It is not a mandatory per-skill charge in the primary paid-job pathway. No token issuance, appreciation or fictitious transfer revenue contributes operating profit.

## A / B / C classification and payment timing

Class A is delivery and learning already charged to the originating commercial job. Class B is genuinely additional retention and later lifecycle activity. Class C is optional dedicated research. No activity receipt is charged in more than one class. The word “paid” in the venture title is not a substitute for actual collections: the replay shows validator approval, payment authorization and cleared collection separately. Candidate emergence can precede customer payment; extra expenditure still needs cleared cash after protected obligations. Material correctness is an analyst label, not free information supplied to the controller.

The A classification also includes qualification/intake, unsuccessful attempts, correction, revalidation, recovery and evidence storage; it is not limited to the construction rows. B’s version/access/composition checks are separate from A’s per-project semantic validation. The implemented export reconciles **A_direct + A_other + B + C = A + B + C = complete cost**. A_other is the explicitly reported subtotal `baseline_overhead_in_A`, including qualification, fixed/setup operation, payment fees and ordinary Beta-equivalent acquisition; it must not be added again. C contains `startup_discovery_in_C` plus `separate_optional_campaign_cost`. Refunds and unpaid refund obligations reverse receipts outside expense totals. Reserves restrict available cash and are not duplicated expenses. Prepaid budgets and zero-cash resource-execution records have distinct IDs and must not be summed twice.

The metric called operating-funded paid-job feedback excludes **subsequent optional campaigns** (`investment_cost=0`), while retaining the common startup discovery cost in C. It is therefore not a claim that discovery was free. Other recovery metrics combine B and optional C in the paired policy result and should not be attributed to an isolated retention asset.
