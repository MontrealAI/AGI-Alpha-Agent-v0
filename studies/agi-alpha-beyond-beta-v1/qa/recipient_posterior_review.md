# Independent diagnosis of the frozen recipient deployment policy

Date: 27 October 2026. This is a post hoc interpretation of unchanged frozen source and saved synthetic trajectories. It does not replace the commercial-core results, the original stress results or their accounting receipts.

## Finding

The favorable scenario's zero recipient uptake is structurally implied by its frozen deployment rule. It is not evidence that no economically useful retained capability could exist. The candidate is assigned an absolute Beta(2,2) reliability prior, while the competent Beta comparator retains the scenario's declared reliability without the same small-sample shrinkage. Admission and pre-spend forecasts use a different paired-value assessment and do not account for the resulting recipient refusal. A cautious unfamiliar-artifact prior can be a coherent trust preference, but this particular uncalibrated choice and its interaction with the other decisions create an avoidable policy bottleneck.

The frozen posterior is `(2 + passes)/(4 + N)`. Even a perfect 24/24 initial test gives 26/28 = 0.928571. A perfect 16/16 reassessment gives 18/20 = 0.90. Favorable recipients compare this with ordinary reconstruction quality of at least 0.97. The frozen recipient rule converts that gap into a monetary penalty even when every paid component test passes. More than 62 perfect observations would be required merely to raise this posterior mean above 0.97; the declared initial assessment provides 24.

The exact lower-price, maximum-saving bounds below use the frozen favorable tariffs and quantities, no prior local equivalent, the earliest/full reconstruction cost, perfect test results and the minimum allowed price fraction of 0.20. They exclude acquisition/distribution costs, so they favor adoption. Partial equivalence, higher prices and declining reconstruction cost make the bounds worse.

Because every upper bound is negative, the frozen favorable recipient rule cannot adopt an eligible candidate under these supported scopes, even before paying acquisition. More recipients or more eligible jobs cannot remove this arithmetic restriction. This is a result about the implemented policy, not a capability ceiling or a commercially optimal allocation theorem.

## Cost-only-ablation defect

The frozen recipient expression for `expected_quality_gain` omits `alpha_quality_effect_fraction`. Actual deployment and admission valuation apply this factor. Consequently, the prespecified cost-only condition sets actual quality effect to zero while its recipient decision can still subtract the posterior quality gap. That condition is not a clean cost-only deployment ablation. Its recorded result is preserved, with this limitation disclosed; no result is relabeled as evidence of intrinsic cost-only capability failure.

## One separately frozen exploratory sensitivity

The treatment retains four effective prior cases but uses `q0 = p_good*q_good + (1-p_good)*0.65`, where `q_good` is the existing `retained_component_correctness` assumption. For optional C construction, `p_good = asset_build_success`. Actual latent correctness, failures, task quantities, tariffs, paid test counts, transfer draws, demand assumptions, competent Beta, ordinary local learning, cash protection and aggregate quotas remain unchanged. This is a sensitivity to a decision belief, not a measured improvement in model performance, free additional validation or an empirically calibrated prior. ## Completed one-run result and final clearance

The separately frozen diagnostic completed all 100 fresh matched worlds and 300 real policy histories. `qa/posterior_diagnostic_reconciliation.json` passed all 8,100 monthly rows, 512 capital purchases (510 B-retention purchases), 823,438 expense activities and 156,909 source-job records. It also reconciled paid assessments, recipient acquisition, composition, terminal obligations and exact A/B/C partitions. All 19 original frozen hashes and all 28 diagnostic closure hashes remain unchanged.

Changing the decision prior permits useful retention in 36 of 100 worlds. Relative to the frozen network policy, mean total-policy profit improves by $127.54, median improvement is $0, and the 5th/95th percentiles are $0/$543.53; 36 worlds improve, one loses, and 63 tie. Relative to competent Beta, all 100 remain negative: mean minus $1,172.65, with a range from minus $2,281.23 to minus $316.49. Quality-adjusted differences are identical. No world delivers an additional truly accepted project relative to Beta. Useful-retention counts are capability-project pairs, not necessarily distinct projects. One treatment world also incurs optional C research, so the reported differences are total-policy effects rather than isolated B-asset repayment.

The improvement therefore resolves part of the deployment-policy bottleneck without establishing positive additional Alpha or self-financing of the extra retention programme beyond competent Beta. Ordinary paid-work local learning is already retained inside Beta; this diagnostic does not isolate its causal contribution or deny its modeled benefit. The original commercial Beta conclusions remain unchanged. The one approved diagnostic is complete; no further parameter change or experiment was run.
