# Operational economics amendment v1

**Prospective specification; scenario evaluation requires the separate published freeze and authorization record. No scenario results were computed before freeze.**

This is a new prospective computational economic analysis prepared on 25 October 2026, after the original capability-study outcomes were known. It is not a retrospective extension of that experiment's preregistration, a human trial, a reconstruction of an invoice, or a claim that a commercial executor was deployed. The sealed `capability_study`, earlier Economics edition and their publications remain unchanged. An immutable manifest and public hash notice must precede scenario evaluation. The coordinator must explicitly signal that the freeze is published before the analyst implements/runs the scenario model.

## 1. Employer decision and accepted deliverable

**Brief:** “Prepare a source-backed publication-readiness evidence pack for my project coordinator. Using these two approved, pinned source documents and four specified passages, deliver a readable cited brief and a short presentation that make the paper-publication route and approved-executor prerequisites easy to inspect. Preserve the exact passages, source URLs and hashes; flag scope limits. Let a competent reviewer check it before use.”

The concrete anchor is the original `source-synthesis-01` deliverable: two source packets, four selected passages, a finished HTML/JSON brief and slides, traceable source identities, a retained repair module and independent deterministic acceptance. That demonstrated component checks exact passage fidelity, coverage, artifact binding and readable structure. It does not establish open-ended source discovery, semantic research quality, business judgment or independent human acceptance. The original `reviewMinutes: 10` field is a declared input, not an observed ten-minute review.

Both arms receive the same bounded brief, source access, requested passages and acceptance criteria. Traditional work uses competent staff, ordinary non-AI software, legitimate efficient workflows and reusable templates. Source selection, approval and final review remain human responsibilities in both arms and are costed. A five-minute production sensitivity is retained because four specified passages may be quick to prepare. Human productive duration is not inferred from wages.

Acceptance requires all four requested passages without material corruption or addition, all citations and source identities, usable brief/slides, declared limits, and resolution of a competent reviewer's material objections. The scenario's accepted indicator means those standards are assumed met; it does not mean merely that a program returned a file. No new empirical human acceptance is asserted.

## 2. Observation, published price and assumption are separate

Three data classes remain visible in every output:

1. **Observed bounded component:** the original native producer/independent verifier receipt, including its measured elapsed and CPU values and known zero task-time model calls. Its complete project cost and human attention stay null.
2. **Published prices:** dated official model/tool/infrastructure tariffs and existing labor-market observations, with source, currency, geography, benefits coverage, scope and AI-use limits. These are price parameters, not usage observations.
3. **Operational assumptions:** all request-path probabilities, token quantities, calls, employer/provider effort, queue delays, deployment effort, failure correlation, demand, maintenance, overhead reserves and reuse volumes. These are declared scenarios, not calibrated field estimates.

The prospective deployment uses standard first-party Claude Sonnet 5.5 and explicit Claude Opus 5.5 specialist/review calls, a request-billed Google Cloud Run executor in `us-central1`, and standard Cloud Storage in that region. This configuration was not deployed. It does not identify the research host model or its bill. No cache, batch, free-tier or committed-use saving is claimed. Retrieved search content and tool-schema/history tokens are included in declared input totals; reasoning tokens are included in output totals. Failed searches are not charged a successful-search fee, while consumed model tokens and occupied execution time remain charged.

Those hypothetical orchestration calls were neither required nor used by the frozen native job. Their quantities and associated field acceptance assumptions are not performance-validated by its exact-passage acceptance. A separate native-delivery sensitivity removes only delivery model/search calls while retaining positive setup, integration, construction, human review, maintenance and assumed occupied-time budgets. It establishes no new full-pipeline quality result.

The native 0.1-second-scale measurement is a strict included subcomponent of assumed executor occupancy, never a complete service clock or a standalone commercial cost. It is not added again to occupancy. CPU seconds are recorded separately and are not multiplied by a cloud tariff intended for allocated instance time.

## 3. Finite correlated outcome paths

The input file declares six mutually exclusive terminal paths, their event sequences and three joint weight vectors: **base, conservative and stress**. They are not probability estimates from the 42 accepted fixtures.

| Path | Terminal outcome | Work charged |
|---|---|---|
| Direct | Accepted | Intake, initial production/critique, human review, completion |
| Model repair | Accepted | Initial work and review, challenge verification, repair, rereview, completion |
| Infrastructure retry | Accepted | Partially consumed failed attempt, diagnosis, retry delay, complete new attempt, review, completion |
| Combined rescue | Accepted | Failed attempt, retry, initial work/review, specialist/manual rescue, rereview, completion |
| Final rejection | Rejected | Initial attempt, repair, specialist/manual rescue, every review, exhaustion handling; no accepted output |
| Early refusal | Refused | Intake, eligibility check and human closure; no accepted output |

At most three production attempts are permitted within a request. Repeated work, review and resource consumption are linked by their path rather than multiplied as independent averages. A job that is hard for one attempt can remain hard for every retry. The declared weights shift toward expensive/failing paths at the same time that token consumption, employer/provider effort, occupancy and waits increase and usable demand/utilization decline. No independent geometric-success assumption is used.

Events contain separately itemized employer minutes, provider-operator minutes, model input/output totals by route, successful search calls, native invocations, executor occupancy, allocated CPU/memory and external wait. Each event's serial elapsed time is occupancy plus active human time plus external wait. Occupancy already includes model/network/native work; human waiting is outside it. The model assumes sequential event execution and does not subtract speculative parallelism. Model latency and queue components are assumptions, not provider service-level guarantees.

## 4. Complete operational and improvement accounts

Report delivery, improvement, infrastructure and employer attention separately, then combine them for the adoption calculation. The ownership basis is attributed resource cost for a self-operated or cost-transparent service. Do not add a provider fee on top of the same provider costs and call it an employer quote. A future actual fee would be a separate customer-price analysis.

Initial fixed costs explicitly cover requirements/setup, deployment/configuration, approved-executor integration, successful capability construction, unsuccessful candidate construction, independent qualification, employer onboarding, construction model/tool use and occupied compute. Recurring costs cover support, runtime/dependency maintenance, capability revalidation, recovery/security upkeep, successful refresh and unsuccessful improvement work, employer governance, maintenance model/tool/compute use, shared storage, software/connectors, monitoring and an explicit miscellaneous operations reserve. Per-request records add artifact retention and a separately labeled operations/egress allowance. Published tariffs and assumed allowances must not describe the same component twice.

All construction quantities are simulated budgets for a prospective service. Historical research-authoring USD, tokens and human minutes remain unknown. They are neither backfilled with these budgets nor silently set to zero. Labor prices already including benefits are not loaded again; Ontario's wage-only record has a separately assumed 1.30 employer load. No cash saving, revenue, profit or reinvestable money is observed.

The primary labor-price basis for this small administrative pack is U.S. office/administrative total compensation. U.S. professional compensation is a separate higher-skill sensitivity. Ontario and EU examples retain their documented occupation/coverage mismatch. Provider operator/engineering time uses the U.S. professional cost in every price basis, converted to the reporting currency; this assumes a U.S.-based support team and is not a contractor quote. Employer and traditional-worker time use the selected local price basis. There are four price bases across three geographies, not four independent geographic samples.

The scope is a low-consequence administrative draft reviewed before use. High-consequence medical, legal, financial, safety-critical and autonomous-action applications are unsupported. Undetected material error and downstream liability remain unquantified, not expected loss zero. The miscellaneous reserve does not establish liability coverage. Reported totals are modeled operational resource costs; actual field total economic cost, including any material unmodeled consequence, remains unknown. Adoption conclusions are conditional on this limited task and review boundary.

The first-commission display charges the entire initial construction/setup/integration investment plus one operating month and that request's full path costs. It reports its acceptance probability, total cost per requested commission and cost normalized by expected accepted outputs. This is not the expected cost of retrying the same commission indefinitely. Annual finite-volume cases charge the same initial investment once plus twelve operating months and every attempted request, including final failures. A separate positive-demand volume sensitivity changes only the number of requests; it does not erase fixed costs.

## 5. Closed-form calculation

For scenario s and terminal path b, let p_b be its probability, a_b its acceptance indicator, c_b its complete variable cost, h_b its employer minutes, g_b its provider minutes, o_b its occupied executor seconds, and l_b its nonoverlapping elapsed seconds. These come from the declared event list. Let Q = sum_b p_b a_b, E[c] = sum_b p_b c_b, and analogously for other resources. No Monte Carlo is needed.

For N requested jobs and fixed cost F over the declared horizon:

`expected accepted = N Q`

`total cost = F + N E[c]`

`cost per expected accepted output = (F + N E[c]) / (N Q)`

Report the complete cost per requested job as well. All final rejection/refusal costs stay in E[c]. When N=0 or Q=0, accepted-normalized metrics are undefined and reported as null with a reason; fixed losses remain visible. A ratio of expectations is a planning measure, not the expected ratio of a finite random sample.

Employer minutes per expected accepted output are `(fixed employer minutes + N E[h])/(N Q)`. Report provider engineering/operator minutes separately and their sum as total human labor. This prevents low customer attention from hiding provider effort. Conditional successful-request turnaround is `sum p_b a_b l_b / Q`; also report the all-request mean, failed-request mean, discrete median/p95 and worst declared path. Add assumed setup elapsed business time for the first commission. Never present accumulated serial workload per accepted output as a user's latency.

The traditional first commission includes its own declared template/setup delay. Hybrid first-commission and service-calendar calculations conservatively add the Agent and traditional readiness delays sequentially. Subsequent request latency excludes these one-time setup delays but retains the operating queues and review waits.

For currency g, express every USD tariff in that currency using the frozen ECB reference rates; labor rates remain in their native currency. This is nominal accounting conversion, not purchasing-power parity or a geographic productivity comparison.

## 6. Fair traditional-work comparator and break-even

Traditional productive time t takes the preregistered values **5, 15, 30, 60 and 120 minutes**. It covers source inspection, assembling/formatting the brief/slides and the worker's own check; it excludes separately listed client input preparation, independent review, completion and correction. The output must show this t and total human minutes together. Each scenario has explicit common administration/review effort, a correction probability and correction/rereview effort, ordinary software/IT allowance and a modest initial reusable-template/setup cost.

The primary competent-human and hybrid-rescue scenarios assume final human acceptance of 1. This favors the human comparator and makes complete-demand accounting transparent; it is not an observed quality rate. Correction still costs time. The Agent branch acceptance remains below or equal to one as specified by its assumed path weights. No no-AI human duration or quality observation is invented, and wage-source workers' AI usage remains not established.

At fixed N, the human all-in accepted cost is affine in t: `C_H(t) = H0 + (w_H/60) t`. H0 includes its fixed allocation and all listed ancillary costs. The break-even productive human time is `t* = 60 (C_A - H0) / w_H`; also show the corresponding all-in human minutes. A negative threshold means the assumed Agent cost is below human ancillary cost alone, not evidence of negative work time. Dollar comparisons require identical task scope and acceptance standards.

## 7. Complete-demand hybrid

Accepted-normalized Agent cost does not mean all requested jobs were completed. Report accepted and unresolved counts explicitly. The separately labeled hybrid gives each final rejected/refused Agent request to a competent traditional worker, with a declared additional handoff. Charge the **entire failed Agent path**, the handoff and a full traditional variable workflow, including its own review/correction assumptions. No sunk Agent work is credited as free human progress. Pay the human template/ordinary-tool fixed costs as well as the Agent fixed costs when maintaining both routes.

With human final acceptance assumed one, every requested job is completed: `E[c_hybrid] = E[c_A] + sum p_b (1-a_b) (handoff_cost + c_H_variable(t))`. The hybrid acceptance is a scenario assumption, not a rescued job observation. Report its total employer time, all human labor, turnaround and capacity. The same conventional baseline supplies the rescue and the comparator.

Hybrid cost is also affine in productive human time: `C_hybrid(t)=A0+b_A t`, whereas `C_H(t)=H0+b_H t`. Its break-even is `(A0-H0)/(b_H-b_A)` when the slope difference is positive. Equal slopes yield no unique break-even unless costs coincide, and a nonpositive slope difference is reported explicitly rather than divided blindly. The pure Agent case has `b_A=0`. All-human labor for the hybrid includes traditional rescue production/correction as well as employer and provider time.

## 8. Capacity, demand and budget

Capacity is a finite-horizon resource bound, not a service promise. Use one declared annual budget converted consistently from USD, working days, employer review minutes, provider engineering/operator minutes, concurrency, occupied executor time, model input/output-token budgets and demand. Resource utilization applies to available time/token pools. Subtract all fixed construction/maintenance usage from the matching pools before admitting delivery work. Initial setup elapsed days also limit the remaining service calendar; do not count a predeployment day as an operating day.

Token pools are assumed aggregate planning budgets, not observed provider quotas or verified requests-per-minute limits. Traditional production/correction work and hybrid fallback consume an explicit traditional-worker pool in addition to employer review and provider engineering pools. Fixed worker/template effort is deducted from that pool; fallback production is never free capacity.

Executor concurrency means two separately allocated instances with at most one request each, so the model charges each request's full occupied allocation without a shared-instance discount. For each time pool use two ceilings: the annual budget less initial and recurring fixed usage, and the postdeployment calendar budget less recurring usage only. Take the smaller residual. Do not subtract initial setup usage again from the already shortened postdeployment calendar. Apply the same convention to employer, provider and traditional-worker time. Aggregate token budgets have the annual subtraction only because they are not time-rate quotas.

Let residual pools be B (money), H (employer minutes), G (provider minutes), O (executor seconds), I (input tokens) and U (output tokens), after fixed charges. The maximum admissible requested volume is the nonnegative integer floor of the minimum of demand and the ratios of each residual pool to its expected per-request use. Zero residual capacity blocks work; zero use of a resource is an explicit inapplicable constraint, not an unknown cost. Expected accepted capacity is N*Q. Report the binding constraint and all residuals. The hybrid adds human fallback work to those resource vectors. Traditional capacity uses the same budget, working calendar and relevant human pools.

Compute both the cost at declared demand, with an explicit feasibility flag, and the feasible admitted volume/cost. Do not amortize over unattainable demand to report a low feasible unit cost. Cash budget versus economic opportunity cost is clearly distinguished: the primary budget constrains attributed full economic cost, not a bank balance.

## 9. Frozen analyses and stopping rule

Only three joint scenarios are primary. Report four price bases across three geographies, retaining occupation/scope mismatches. Prespecified one-way sensitivities around the U.S. office/administrative base case vary productive human time, requested reuse volume, model token use, employer review effort, unsuccessful-construction labor hours and support/maintenance effort. Do not search combinations for a maximum win. The exact factor/value lists and transformation scopes are in `inputs.json`; they are assumptions, not probability distributions or confidence intervals.

Report adverse cases, cost shares, failure burdens, first-commission cost, finite-volume cost, hybrid cost, attention, turnaround and capacity. Explain where results change sign. Retained capability has no automatic financial value. Stop additional construction when its proposed complete incremental cost exceeds expected eligible-demand savings, or demand/resource feasibility is absent; no self-acceleration is inferred from amortization.

The independent evaluator reviews this document, inputs and sources before freeze and independently expands the paths after evaluation is authorized. It checks probability mass, caps, component accounting, native/occupied-time separation, failed costs, currency conversion, fixed-cost allocation, monotonic sensitivity, finite-capacity limits, undefined denominators and every reported summary. No evaluator or acceptance criterion is optimized against favorable economic outputs.
