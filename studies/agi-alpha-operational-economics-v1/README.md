## What the comparison supports

The primary U.S. office case uses an **assumed** thirty-minute productive traditional task plus its separately charged ancillary work. At the declared annual allocation of 1,000 requests, expected accepted-output costs are USD28.91 for the Agent, USD28.42 for traditional work and USD29.73 for complete-demand human fallback. That declared cohort is **not feasible** within the example USD25,000 aggregate expected-use resource ceiling.

At each route's own admission limit, the Agent admits 829 requests, yielding 787.55 expected accepted outputs at USD31.74 each; traditional work completes 876 at USD28.52; the hybrid completes 703 at USD35.54. Conservative and stress cases cannot fund their fixed commitments within the stated budget and provider-time pools. These are modeled planning quantities, not observed invoices or a hard stochastic budget guarantee.

At the declared base allocation, modeled total human labor falls from 47.10 to 33.20 minutes per accepted output, while employer attention rises from 15.82 to 19.33 minutes. Lower aggregate human labor therefore does not establish lower customer attention. The first commissioned deployment costs about USD5,935.82 before future reuse in this scenario. No historical authoring bill or field supervision time was measured.

Favorable comparisons at longer **assumed** human durations remain visible, alongside expensive and infeasible cases. No matched human duration for this four-passage evidence pack was observed. The post-result reuse diagnostic requires 1,040 Agent requests for parity at the primary thirty-minute case, above its 829-request admission ceiling. The specialist-method losses in the preserved study answer an internal method-selection question; they do not establish the outcome of the separate human-market comparison.

## Scope and preserved limitations

The prior native study accepted 42 bounded jobs across seven authored task families. Its retained planning repair lost all twelve fresh comparisons against the available specialist; source-repair reuse did not show a CPU reduction; no improvement in the rate of improvement was established. Those outcomes and all earlier frozen bytes remain intact.

Capacity and first-commission setup calendars are assumptions. The scheduling diagnostic shows that uniform daily provider allocation would require 45, 160 and 490 setup workdays, rather than the declared 10, 30 and 60. Frontloading was not demonstrated. Downstream losses, actual historical authoring costs and live operational behavior remain unmeasured, not zero.

The later requested pricing, collected-proceeds, reinvestment and token-flow research is a **separate prospective amendment**. No reinvestment or token-holder return is inferred from these operating-cost results. Publication of this completed edition does not depend on that further analysis.
