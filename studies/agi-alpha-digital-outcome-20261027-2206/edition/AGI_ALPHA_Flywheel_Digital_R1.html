A useful answer, with its limits attached

A public numerical failure can now be turned into an inspectable, executable answer within a bounded agent mission. This edition delivers a scalar beta-quantile compatibility kernel, a command-line interface, three frozen implementations, independent numerical checks, a complete replay package and an explicit commercial decision. It changes what a scientific software team can do immediately: reproduce an extreme-tail failure, obtain a checked replacement value within a stated domain, and inspect the evidence before deciding whether to integrate the component.

The concrete example matters. For probability 10−1610^{-16} and beta shape parameters a=0.5a=0.5, b=3b=3, the installed SciPy 1.17.0 environment returned a quantile of 0.5. The independently evaluated value is approximately 2.844444444444444×10−332.844444444444444\times10^{-33}. The public report supplied the failure and a candidate numerical recipe. We did not discover that formulation. We converted it into bounded, failure-aware artifacts and tested the contribution of different ways of operating the available assistant. (chrisb83 2026)

All three workflows passed the 64 fresh confirmation checks: 56 numerical or endpoint cases and eight required refusals. Ordinary competent operation and expert operation also handled a separately disclosed, already-known subnormal rounding trap; the original conventional implementation failed that diagnostic. Its correction is preserved separately. The fresh bank is one mission’s case bank, not 64 independent missions or customers.

Decision: retain and deliver the useful engineering; pursue only a customer-funded qualification pilot. Do not promote this edition as an exclusive numerical breakthrough, a measured general Operational Alpha advantage, or a realized profitable business.

The opportunity and the early challenge

Who benefits, and what changes

The immediate beneficiary is a maintainer or scientific software team evaluating a beta-tail calculation. Such a recipient needs more than a plausible formula: a reproducible failure, a bounded replacement, evidence for extreme and invalid inputs, and clear integration limits. Wrong finite values are particularly troublesome because a program may continue without an exception. Several observed failures emitted warnings; an application that ignores them can still consume an incorrect finite value. Whether a particular recipient’s downstream decision is materially affected remains a customer-specific question; this study does not invent a deployed failure or financial loss.

The useful opportunity is rapid numerical incident qualification: assembling a precise failure report and a tested compatibility component from public inputs before an upstream release or a local migration is available. The changes making this operationally plausible are accessible source and issue histories, current numerical libraries, arbitrary-precision arithmetic and an assistant capable of reading code, compiling kernels, diagnosing failed checks and packaging evidence. This study observes that combination working locally. It does not establish that organizations were previously incapable of doing the same work.

Our shortlist was concentrated in scientific software, not a claim to an exhaustive cross-industry search. The preceding gap sprint had already explored broader opportunities. We narrowed the continuation to deliverable public incidents where the reasoning and execution were real and physical fabrication was unnecessary.

Conventional falsification came first

Before the three agent constructions began, a separate workstream compiled current Boost.Math at commit 70a36632cfbfe6a862cd8a5b88b357ca3a770a3d. A double-precision policy disabling promotion, with safe error handling and 400 root iterations, solved all five public examples. Its maximum relative error against the 80-digit public-recipe reference was approximately 2.008×10−142.008\times10^{-14}. Raising the cap to 2,000 did not change that five-case result. A long-double configuration failed one example at both caps; the failed configuration remains in the record.

This is a meaningful established alternative. SciPy itself documents that betaincinv wraps Boost’s ibeta_inv; the Boost interface already exposes precision and error policies. A successful current-Boost configuration is therefore not an invented numerical method. (SciPy Developers 2026; Boost.Math Developers 2026)

Selection changed because of this evidence. The candidate survived as a useful digital delivery and an operational comparison. Its exclusive technical-breakthrough claim did not survive. Public-source formulation credit stays with the reporter; execution comparators received that formulation in full.

The accepted digital contract

The delivered object is deliberately bounded: a scalar compatibility component, not a drop-in replacement for all SciPy distributions, array broadcasting, accelerators, automatic differentiation or every positive beta shape.

A request contains an identifier and three numbers: probability qq and shapes a,ba,b. The interpretation is the exact binary64 value received by Python. The accepted numerical domain is

0≤q≤1,0.25≤a,b≤106,min(a,b)≤100. 0\le q\le1,\qquad 0.25\le a,b\le10^6,\qquad \min(a,b)\le100.

The result preserves the identifier and returns a status with a value or a refusal. Exact probabilities zero and one require their corresponding endpoint values. An interior true quantile that rounds to binary64 zero requires an explicit underflow status. Unsupported positive shapes are distinguished from invalid inputs. Booleans, strings, nonfinite values, nonpositive shapes and out-of-range probabilities are not silently coerced into numerical success.

For a verified reference value rr strictly between zero and one, the frozen acceptance tolerance is

|x̂−r|≤max{2ulp(r),10−11min(r,1−r)}. |\widehat x-r|\le \max\{2\,\operatorname{ulp}(r),\;10^{-11}\min(r,1-r)\}.

A representable positive reference may not be replaced with zero. Correct endpoint, underflow and refusal behavior is checked explicitly. A computation that cannot establish an answer returns failed; a plausible finite number does not automatically count as success.

How the construction works

The three implementations combine established instruments: a pinned Boost inverse, analytic special cases, incomplete-beta continued fractions, symmetry, log-domain arithmetic and high-precision safeguarded inversion when needed. This composition protects against numerical scale and unsupported inputs. Its scientific ingredients are conventional. Definitions, symmetry, the integer-shape binomial identity and continued fractions appear in NIST’s reference treatment. (National Institute of Standards and Technology 2026)

The ordinary implementation is the recommended research entry point because it passed the primary contract and the disclosed rounding regression with less recorded construction time than the expert arm. That is a local delivery choice, not a universal ranking. All original implementations remain available so a recipient can inspect the tradeoffs rather than inherit a hidden selection.

It deliberately emphasizes adverse numerical regimes. It is not a random sample of customer demand or the prevalence of bugs in software.

What the checks do and do not prove

The bank establishes finite acceptance under a frozen contract. It does not prove correctness throughout the continuous parameter envelope. Two high-precision runs can share an error, and distinct programs can share a mathematical ingredient. The evaluator therefore retains its independent controls and uncertainty rather than treating agreement as a proof of universal correctness.

Every primary call met its observed CPU limits: five seconds per call and 120 seconds per worker. Raw timings are retained, but no numerical speedup is claimed. Many recorded per-call CPU differences were zero; the tiny batch and observed timer behavior do not support a stable ratio. The identical reported peak memory high-water marks are likewise unsuitable for an arm-level memory advantage. Import and build time are recorded separately from solve time, and construction and verification effort cannot be replaced by a microsecond solve-time figure.

The broad result is uncomplicated: competent ordinary operation succeeded, explicit expert operation succeeded, and the strongest identified conventional workflow succeeded on the fresh bank. The expert checklist did not produce a better primary accepted outcome in this trial.

The additional boundary failure stays visible

After primary confirmation, the independent audit ran an already-known diagnostic at the smallest positive binary64 probability, with a=1,b=2a=1,b=2. Ordinary and expert implementations returned the correct positive subnormal value; the original conventional implementation returned zero with underflow. This diagnostic was not fresh and was not added to the primary denominator. A separately labeled conventional correction, using ordinary analytic precision and lattice rounding, passed the same 64-case replay plus the known midpoint diagnostic. These are post hoc replays, not a new blind confirmation; the original failure remains in the correction record. Neither the original failure nor the corrected comparison disappears.

A better measuring instrument

The most instructive failure occurred in the numerical judge. Both the ordinary and expert workstreams independently noticed that converting a high-precision value to a machine float can conceal a subnormal rounding error. Agreement at two precisions did not guarantee correct rounding. The root evaluator initially shared that weakness.

The correction was made before generating the fresh bank. The old evaluator, new evaluator, source hashes, diagnostic and rationale remain in the package. The acceptance requirements did not change. All three primary arms were evaluated by the same corrected instrument. We disclose the feedback from the arm builders because the root evaluator was not sealed from all development feedback.

An exact counterexample

Let δ=2−1074\delta=2^{-1074}, the smallest positive binary64 value. For a Beta(1,2)(1,2) distribution,

F(x)=2x−x2. F(x)=2x-x^2.

At the midpoint between zero and δ\delta,

F(δ/2)=δ−δ2/4<δ. F(\delta/2)=\delta-\delta^2/4<\delta.

The quantile at probability δ\delta is therefore above that midpoint and rounds to δ\delta, not zero. A computation that loses the tiny positive correction can land on the midpoint and apply the wrong rounding decision. This is a numerical instrument failure with an exact rational adjudication, not an invented new special function.

The corrected reference explicitly rounds on the binary64 subnormal lattice. Near a midpoint, it uses an exact integer-shape CDF when available, otherwise a 500-digit adaptive calculation with an unresolved-reference outcome if the decision remains too close. The construction uses conventional mathematical identities; the contribution is the preserved diagnosis and the accountable correction.

Why this belongs in the main narrative

A fluent implementation and a passing high-precision comparison can still be wrong in the same way. A useful agent must be able to question its checker and produce independently inspectable evidence for that challenge. In this study, ordinary competent use already did that. It would be misleading to award the behavior exclusively to the expert arm.

Transformational Intelligence argues that evaluators may themselves impose restrictions, but that revising an evaluator must not become a way for a candidate to award itself victory. Its prescribed discipline is an independent outcome anchor, preserved old evidence and a common corrected comparison. This continuation exercises that discipline on a real numerical instrument. It does not thereby establish a novel theory of evaluator revision. (Boucher 2026c)

The earlier kinetics construction remains supporting engineering. Its conventional parity, thin conditional economics and untested physical assumptions are unchanged. No chemical parameters, catalysts or acceptance criteria were tuned in this continuation to manufacture a second success.

Financing, demand and the next voluntary exchange

Buyer value must survive the free alternative

The central price hypothesis has little room for error. It assumes eight hours of avoided self-qualification at $150/h, $600 of avoided delay, and one hour of buyer participation. The resulting value ceiling is $1,650, leaving only $150 of modeled buyer surplus at the proposed $1,500 fee. The hours and delay value are unmeasured. A competent recipient needing only two hours to reuse the free artifact, with no valuable delay avoided, may rationally buy nothing.

Prices are independent buyer hypotheses, not a fixed discount from an invented market price. The executable sensitivity grid holds volume fixed only to show arithmetic; it does not assume buyers remain when price exceeds their value. Extra participation is not automatically beneficial: replacing a $125 provider hour with a $150 buyer hour increases combined cost unless it brings another justified benefit.

A human team attempting an equivalent new repair from the same public recipe is estimated separately at 14–38 hours, or $1,400–$6,650 at the stated rate endpoints. The central attempt estimate is 24 h at $125/h. This is neither a measured human baseline nor willingness to pay, and it is never added as a second bill for the already produced artifact.

What the result makes possible

Evidence: a small capability that works now

The demonstrated near-term capability is a bounded numerical incident package: locate the relevant public evidence, reproduce the failure, challenge the proposed novelty, construct a usable scalar component, uncover a checker weakness, run fresh confirmation and deliver the complete record. Its usefulness is inspectable without waiting for physical fabrication, private data or a speculative future model.

The external libraries and public report do much of the intellectual work. The live assistant’s contribution is to connect them into an executable, checked outcome under a time limit. That is a real productive capability, even when a competent conventional team could perform the same task. The study does not establish that the expert operating procedure improves that capability over ordinary competent use.

Conditional expansion: a portfolio of qualification missions

A broader service could qualify numerical incidents across scientific packages, supply bounded compatibility components and help recipients decide whether to integrate, upgrade or decline a workaround. That expansion depends on evidence absent from this single mission: multiple prospectively selected incidents, real recipient acceptance, adequate model metering, known licensing and support obligations, and a measured rate of failures that cannot be repaired within the paid scope.

The next economically informative step is one voluntary buyer pilot with a written acceptance contract and a capped integration scope. A valuable customer question might be whether this component resolves a blocked calculation on the buyer’s actual supported stack. It must not be silently replaced by passing our existing numerical bank. The free artifact remains the buyer’s outside option; a buyer who can self-integrate cheaply has no reason to pay a premium merely because an agent was involved.

Long-term ambition: capabilities that open better opportunities

The flywheel’s larger thesis is that a delivered capability can make a later opportunity feasible. This result supplies a possible starting asset, not evidence of compounding returns. A future successor could reuse the source-pinning, numerical-regime analysis or exact-midpoint checks on a genuinely new mission. It would still have to beat its own competent baseline and an ordinary library containing the same asset after retention, validation, integration and maintenance costs.

No fresh transfer trial was performed here. There is no retained-Alpha or Gym-training claim. Adding transfer work would not repair the missing expert advantage, and ordinary reuse is already available. Optional learning investment should follow an expected benefit, not a prescribed number of reinvestments.

Invent the Economy separates technical feasibility, participant value, profitability, liquidity and actual exchange. Transformational Autonomous Enterprises preserves comparisons in which a supplied deterministic specialist matches quality and costs less. Those lessons shape this edition: keep useful assets, let strong baselines win, and require evidence for the next economic claim. (Boucher 2026a, 2026b)

Recommendation: deliver the research artifact, retain the corrected numerical instrument and conventional comparisons, and invite a narrowly scoped customer qualification pilot. Stop the exclusive algorithmic-novelty claim. Leave general Operational Alpha, discovery advantage, retained benefit and realized commercial viability unestablished until their own experiments support them.
