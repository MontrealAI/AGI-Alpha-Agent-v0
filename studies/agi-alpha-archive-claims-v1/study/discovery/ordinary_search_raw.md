# Independent ordinary-prompt discovery arm

Date: 2026-10-27. This is one frontier-agent proxy given a general discovery prompt, not a human participant or an experimental result. No other agents' files, candidate lists, or intended outcomes were inspected. Six web search queries were used. Follow-up page opens and finds were used to inspect those results. No API spend, repository mutations, outreach, physical tests, or publication were performed. This file is temporary research evidence for the parent to preserve.

## Recommendation

**Best candidate: qualify overlooked mine waste as possible mineral feedstock by reconstructing its material history and testing the resulting claims.** The productive need is for reliable evidence that a particular existing waste body contains accessible, recoverable material of commercial interest. The proposed output is a source-linked, independently checked resource dossier and a prospectively validated sampling hypothesis. A polished map alone would not meet the need.

I would advance this to a bounded feasibility study, not straight to a company or extraction project. It has a newly available public starting corpus, a specific buyer, and physical outcomes that can falsify an agent's work. Its biggest weakness is that desk research might be a small part of the total bottleneck: mineral separability, access, ownership, environmental obligations, and capital costs can dominate. I did not establish market size, willingness to pay, freedom to operate, or superiority over existing geological consultants.

Three opportunities survived this short search:

| Priority | Opportunity | Beneficiary | New productive asset | Main unresolved issue |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mine-waste resource qualification | Reprocessors, mine owners, reclamation funders | Verified material-history and assay dossiers for specific waste bodies | Does better evidence lead to economically useful recovery? |
| 2 | Recover missing trait evidence for conserved crop accessions | Public breeders, smaller seed companies, genebanks | Living accessions linked to trustworthy historical and newly tested traits | Are usable records and viable, distributable seed available together? |
| 3 | Restore unsupported non-imaging scientific instruments | University labs and small research facilities | Reproducible replacement control packages with independent measurement checks | Can work repeat across enough instruments to escape bespoke consulting? |

These are proposals inferred from documented constraints, not claims that the markets are vacant or the concepts are unprecedented. The bounded search cannot establish either.

## Capability premise and its limits

The useful change is the ability to run extended tool-using workflows that create code, inspect failures, reconcile evidence, and retain explicit state across sessions. Anthropic's March 23, 2026 account demonstrates scientific software work organized around reference implementations, persistent progress records, and test oracles. Its examples are well-scoped computational tasks; it is vendor-authored evidence, not proof of autonomous geological judgment, biological discovery, or reliable hardware engineering. [Anthropic source](https://www.anthropic.com/research/long-running-claude). genui{"citation":{"ref":"turn12view0"}}

My inference is that this can lower the integration cost of messy evidence and executable checks. It does not create missing measurements. Each opportunity therefore ends in a reference measurement, expert adjudication, or physical test. The common design requirement is to preserve disagreement and unknowns rather than convert plausible reconstruction into fact.

## 1. Mine-waste resource qualification — selected candidate

### Need, beneficiary, and evidence

Owners and potential reprocessors need to determine whether a particular waste body deserves sampling and metallurgical work. Historical documents may describe ore sources, processing dates, recoveries, waste destinations, and past assays without a reliable link to today's stockpile. A wrong identity or unsupported grade estimate can make later work worthless.

The USGS says mineral content and locations of mine wastes remain poorly understood, and identifies potential resource value in material already above ground and partly processed. It has also developed standardized geochemical data-collection protocols. This supports the need for characterization, without establishing commercial viability for any particular site. [USGS Mine Waste](https://www.usgs.gov/special-topics/earth-mri/mine-waste). genui{"citation":{"ref":"turn12view2"}}

The National Mine Waste Inventory's version 2.0 was revised October 1, 2026. It supplies public-source point and polygon records, including location, size, commodity, and mineralogy, for features generally at least about 2,000 square meters. It is marked CC0. This is a timely foundation for a narrow pilot, and also means a basic inventory is already being supplied publicly. [USGS data release](https://www.usgs.gov/data/national-mine-waste-inventory-ver-20-october-2026). genui{"citation":{"ref":"turn10view2"}}

### Existing approach and limitation

State geological surveys, federal researchers, consultants, mine owners, and analytical laboratories already inventory, characterize, and evaluate these materials. Their work is substantive competition and a potential partnership route. The proposed gap is a reproducible bridge from scattered historical evidence to a specific, testable present-day waste body.

Crucially, chemical content alone is insufficient. USGS explicitly identifies both content and extractability as necessary to evaluate resource potential, and describes weathering-induced changes in mineral hosts. Thus an old assay cannot simply be transported forward into a modern recovery estimate. [USGS, Critical minerals in mine waste](https://pubs.usgs.gov/publication/fs20253026/full). genui{"citation":{"ref":"turn8search3"}}

### Why feasible now

The new public inventory reduces the cost of finding and identifying a starting population. Long-running coding agents can potentially build archive parsers, reconcile map coordinates, normalize units, run consistency checks, and maintain a claim-by-claim evidence history. These are hypotheses about economic feasibility, not verified capability on the proposed corpus.

The agent's distinctive job would be to follow chains such as: historical processing plant → operating interval → ore source → treatment method → waste destination → present stockpile. It would mark links as observed, inferred, disputed, or missing. A geologist would approve the chains before they informed sampling.

### Construction and falsification test

1. Select one district and one waste class with a geological partner. Freeze a corpus of public records and 30–50 inventory features. Avoid a nationwide scope.
2. Build a dossier schema containing feature identity, spatial extent, historical aliases, processing intervals, source images or passages, units, dates, contradictory claims, missing measurements, and what sampling could resolve.
3. Produce 15–20 candidate dossiers. Where source records do not support mass, grade, or recovery, leave those fields unknown. Geographic coincidence alone must not establish identity.
4. Have independent reviewers adjudicate identity links and quantitative claims without seeing which workflow produced them. Compare an agent workflow against a competent analyst using ordinary search/OCR and the same source corpus. Record total labor, including verification and correction.
5. For accessible and authorized sites, have a field team sample a small prospective set including some lower-ranked cases. Independent assays and bench extraction determine whether the recovered history was informative. This stage must be designed by a qualified partner; an agent-generated dossier cannot certify a resource or authorize disturbance.

Proposed research gates, not predicted outcomes: materially lower total verified dossier cost; very high precision on identity and numeric claims; additional actionable evidence missed by the baseline; and at least one sampling decision improved under a predeclared decision rule. Stop if checking the output costs as much as producing it conventionally, if candidates are almost always blocked before sampling, or if the archive provides little information beyond the existing inventory. A useful negative result can save a bad sampling campaign, but cannot justify claiming mineral discovery.

### Commercial mechanism

Start with paid, fixed-scope evidence reconstruction for an owner, reprocessor, or survey partner that already has budget for characterization. A deliverable could be a specified number of expert-reviewed dossiers plus a sampling rationale. Charge for verified work; add repeat updates or district coverage only after repeat demand is demonstrated. Do not assume public geological data itself forms a proprietary moat.

The compounding commercial asset would be costly-to-reproduce validated links between historical operations, contemporary features, samples, and results. Customer rights and public-source licenses must travel with the records. Mineral rights or recovery royalties would add substantial complexity and are unnecessary for testing the initial service.

### Successor inheritance

A successor receives source snapshots and hashes, coordinate transformations, unit conversion code, entity-resolution decisions, rejected hypotheses, expert corrections, sample chain of custody, laboratory results, and the frozen comparison set. It must rerun prior cases before expanding scope. Inheritance is useful because later work can avoid repeating failed links and can update a specific claim when new sampling arrives; a narrative summary alone would lose this value.

### Residual uncertainty

Confidence that the characterization need exists: high. Confidence that agents can reduce verified desk-work cost: moderate and untested. Confidence that this becomes a scalable standalone business: low to moderate. No claim is made that a newly identified site would be profitable, easier to permit, or environmentally beneficial overall. Those outcomes require site-specific evidence.

## 2. Recover missing trait evidence for conserved crop accessions

### Need and beneficiary

Breeders need living material with credible evidence for a desired trait and a known accession identity. A collection can contain valuable variation while its old trial books, aliases, provenance, and availability records make the relevant material difficult to find.

A December 2025 study of 20 national genebanks found substantial documentation and availability gaps. Sixteen had critical passport-data completeness gaps; 14 had critical gaps in public characterization/evaluation data. Eight relied to some extent on paper records. Only 21% of accessions in the studied collections were available for distribution after considering viability and seed quantities. These are findings for the study's selected institutions, not global prevalence estimates. [Hanson, Jamora and Bramel study](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/plant-genetic-resources/article/identifying-gaps-and-challenges-for-conserving-crop-diversity-in-genebanks-lessons-from-the-reviews-of-national-genebanks/E2267AB69D2912297F89D60EE04178C3). genui{"citation":{"ref":"turn12view4"}}

### Existing approach and limitation

Genebank curators, passport databases, publications, breeder networks, genotyping, and new phenotyping already support selection. The narrow proposed gap is accession-level evidence recovery: linking an old observation to the correct living accession, its experimental conditions, and current availability. An accession name appearing in two places is insufficient proof of identity. Climate at a collection location is suggestive context, not evidence of a heritable trait.

### Why feasible now

An agent can potentially follow accession aliases across scanned documents and databases, write parsers for irregular trial tables, preserve contradictory observations, and generate a standardized evidence package for expert review. The new contribution would be verified recovered labels and prospective trial results. It would not be a generic chatbot over agricultural papers.

### Construction and test

Choose one genebank, one crop, and a breeder-defined trait. Begin with perhaps 300–500 accessions whose physical availability is checked before extensive reconstruction. Recover a bounded archive, then have a curator adjudicate blinded identity links and phenotype extractions against ordinary database search and manual reading. Measure cost per correct, previously unavailable accession–trait observation and the fraction of candidates that can actually be obtained.

Take a small trait-specific shortlist into an independently run greenhouse assay, followed by field validation if the trait requires it. Include established positive and negative controls and candidates selected using the existing database alone. Prespecify the trait measurement and environment; historical success under another condition is not replication. Stop if the archive mostly lacks observations, identities cannot be resolved, or the viable material is unavailable. An information service cannot compensate for a regeneration backlog.

### Commercial mechanism and inheritance

A seed company, public breeding program, or philanthropic crop program could commission a verified accession portfolio and trial package. Initial payment should cover evidence and testing, with access and benefit-sharing conditions handled with the institution. Do not assume ownership of the underlying germplasm or invent a royalty entitlement.

A successor inherits an accession identity graph, original field-book references, trait definitions and units, environment metadata, availability checks, permissions, negative results, and fresh phenotyping. This supports cumulative biological knowledge instead of repeatedly rediscovering the same paper. The main uncertainty is whether paying breeders value the recovered evidence enough when crossing, multiplication, and field performance may dominate the timetable. This may fit a public-interest service better than a venture-scale business.

## 3. Restore unsupported non-imaging scientific instruments

### Need and beneficiary

Research facilities need to keep functional scientific hardware useful when its control computer, interface card, or software is no longer maintainable. The starting focus should be a narrow non-imaging instrument family, such as a geochronology extraction line, rather than an open-ended laboratory automation platform.

A MethodsX paper documents replacement of obsolete control hardware and software for a noble-gas extraction line using inexpensive single-board computers and open software. This is direct evidence that the problem and a repair route exist. It does not show the size of a commercial market or that agents can perform the conversion safely. [Twinn, MethodsX, 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36593761/). genui{"citation":{"ref":"turn5search1"}}

### Existing approach and limitation

Labs use vendor upgrades, replacement instruments, emulation, isolated old computers, custom engineering, and open instrument drivers. Existing open-hardware projects mean the core idea is established. The possible unmet need is an affordable, maintainable replacement package with enough preserved evidence to demonstrate that measurements and control behavior remain acceptable.

### Why feasible now

This candidate has the strongest correspondence to demonstrated agentic coding: recover a specification from manuals and permitted traces, implement adapters, replay known sequences, and compare results against a reference. Hardware-specific debugging and physical interlocks remain human engineering work. Closed protocols, inaccessible documentation, special acquisition cards, and poor unit economics could make a particular family unsuitable.

### Construction and test

Pick three instruments in one family with partner labs. Create a simulator from documented commands and observed behavior, including timeouts, malformed responses, and safe failure states. Develop the replacement against replayed traces before any live control. An engineer approves the physical interface and retained independent protections. Compare reference materials and repeated measurement runs using old and new systems where the original remains usable.

Compare total engineer time with conventional porting; require another engineer to deploy the package at a second lab from the retained documentation. A first successful one-off port proves little about a product. The discriminating outcome is repeat deployment with acceptable measurement behavior and sharply less new work. Stop if every unit needs essentially fresh reverse engineering or calibration.

### Commercial mechanism and inheritance

Sell installation, verification, and maintenance for a supported instrument family, possibly through repair firms. A replacement package can be open while deployment and maintenance are paid services. Buyers pay to retain useful equipment and reduce an avoidable replacement decision; that willingness to pay remains unmeasured.

The successor receives a protocol specification, simulator, test traces, firmware/software build instructions, hardware schematics, dependency records, calibration comparisons, known limitations, and a working second-site installation recipe. These artifacts directly reduce future reconstruction work. This ranks third because it is closer to an existing integration business and because the bounded search did not establish a sufficiently repetitive neglected niche.

## What this search does and does not establish

This arm produced three evidence-backed hypotheses from a general brief under a six-query budget. It did not run construction tests, interview beneficiaries, compare products comprehensively, or measure discovery quality against any other arm. The selected candidate is the one I would test first under the observed evidence, not a proven frontier-agent invention. The immediate next concrete action would be securing a geological partner's frozen corpus and blind adjudication criteria before building a broad system.
