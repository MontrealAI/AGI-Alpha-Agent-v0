## Result, including its limits

Eight paired six-generation streams yielded 192 workbench epoch packs and 1,152 terminal evaluations. At a fixed cumulative 500,000 structural-work units per stream:

| Workflow | Qualified / 288 | Aggregate work units |
|---|---:|---:|
| Flywheel | 271 | 2,017,944 |
| Equally equipped conventional cache | 271 | 2,017,944 |
| Fixed initial method | 58 | 1,412,176 |
| Authored expert workflow | 288 | 2,134,383 |

The loop ran without manual phase triggers after one primary launch. Prior-generation recipes were used on 138 terminal tasks; 134 qualified. The conventional cache intentionally has the same policy and ties exactly; no unique superiority is inferred.

In the registered whole-stream archive intervention, retention qualifies 36/36 at 316,616 units; clearing history and allowing reconstruction qualifies 30/36 at 279,432. Retention adds six qualified outputs and 37,184 units. A later generation favors rebuilding by 39,662 units at the same 6/6 observed quality. The omitted-annotation counterexample remains a local structural pass and a truth-model failure.

Sixteen bounded fault cases and 24 browser checks passed. Actual 1/2/4-process load increased throughput but kept quality at 66/96. Recovery issues no new accepted result while explicitly charging deterministic verification work. The experiment demonstrates finite source computation and typed capability retention, not a native installed service, production authority, economic growth or settlement. Human/model authoring costs remain unknown.
