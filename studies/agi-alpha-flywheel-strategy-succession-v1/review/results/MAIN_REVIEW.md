## Why no primary capability was retained

The primary flywheel had 12 `NO_COMPLETE_CANDIDATE` acquisition outcomes. Its 96 candidate slots comprised 58 normal valid successive-halving eliminations, 26 unfinished training prefixes and 12 budget- or work-truncated evaluations. A normal racing elimination is a designed selection event; it must not be relabeled a computation failure or budget truncation. Conversely, an unfinished training prefix is not a qualified candidate.

No complete primary flywheel candidate reached its qualification or full-investment admission decision. Therefore the primary flywheel cannot support a causal claim that the admission gate rejected unprofitable tools, or that disabling admission or retention changed productive performance. Those ablations have no admitted capability on which to act. This statement is specific to the flywheel arm: the primary saved-workflow arm completed one candidate and recorded `RETAIN_INCUMBENT`.

## Promotion, adoption and full-investment payback are different decisions

Three questions must remain separate:

1. **Benchmark promotion:** Does an exact candidate satisfy the frozen correctness, scope and comparison rule? A historical benchmark promotion is evidence about that rule and its tested distribution.
2. **Practical adoption after discovery:** Given an already available method, does using it now improve future outcomes compared with the incumbent, after future checking, retrieval, maintenance and execution costs? Discovery costs may already be sunk.
3. **Full-investment payback:** Did acquiring and retaining the method improve the outcome compared with the counterfactual that never paid for its discovery?

The current `admission()` formula implements a forecast of the third question. If `remainingBudget` is the resource balance after acquisition, `A` is acquisition already charged and `Mc` is candidate upkeep, it compares candidate capacity from **`remainingBudget − Mc`** with incumbent counterfactual capacity from **`remainingBudget + A`**. Adding `A` back to the incumbent is an intentional full-investment counterfactual. It is not an optimal rule for marginal adoption after discovery is sunk.

A method can be worth using now while its past R&D remains unrecovered. Conversely, a benchmark win need not justify either use or investment. The recorded `net_benefit` field is a registered model forecast, not realized economic value, revenue or an observed commercial adoption decision. No completed primary flywheel candidate exercised that forecast gate, so the primary outcome cannot establish its effectiveness. Diagnostic correctness-only admission explicitly does not meet the economic test.

## No self-acceleration result

The exploratory relative change statistic is numerically reproduced: +1.878 qualified missions per 100,000 units, with interval [1.575, 2.164]. Its interpretation requires the actual rate trajectories. Mean flywheel window rates are **4.988, 4.298 and 3.580**; portfolio rates are **7.121, 4.710 and 3.835**. Flywheel rates decline from first to last window in 11 of 12 streams; portfolio rates decline in all 12. A positive difference between two declines is not an increasing growth rate.

No primary flywheel capability was retained, and the implementation contains no endogenous reinvestment of earned savings that expands subsequent discovery resources. The changing rates also include initial acquisition charges, partial final windows and growing checkpoint costs. The report must not convert this exploratory relative statistic into a claim of autonomous acceleration.

## Native specialist and cost boundaries

On the 36 selected native-reference tasks, both deadline-feasibility and makespan MILP modes qualified 31 tasks. Deadline mode supplied 31 valid native outputs; makespan mode supplied 36. Preserving the already constructed practical portfolio fallback gave 36 valid practical results in both modes, still with 31 qualified. A deadline model with constant feasibility objective does not prove optimal makespan when its solver reports optimal status.

The solver's opaque internals were not converted into the JavaScript ledger's reference units. The recorded means, response limits, startup, preparation and fallback times therefore remain a separate practical timing comparison, not an assertion of equal total computation. Likewise, declared work-unit accounting includes its specified discovery, checking, recovery and storage charges, but does not price unknown authoring effort or assign a monetary value to synthetic output. Zero manual intervention between stream phases is an operational observation, not a claim of zero human project effort.

The separate development regression-asset probe has its own tariff: one unit per retained byte. The main study uses 64-byte blocks for its declared storage charges. Its diagnostic crossover estimate must not be mixed numerically with the main ledger without an explicit common cost model.

## Independence and reproducibility limits

The scheduling checker and these audits are separately implemented and do not import the producer's solver, interpreter or checker. The original main checker was frozen before confirmation. The post-run audit rechecked stored artifacts and recomputed statistics; it did not rerun candidate search or main confirmation. Reviewers share the same project commission, environment and input records. This is code-level checking independence, not independent institutional replication.

The historical Strategy Laboratory has a separate record: recovered original bytes, an independent stored-artifact audit, and a fresh exact-runtime original-policy confirmation replay. The replay did not rerun historical acquisition or development search. Those layers should be cited separately from the current study and from a maintained production service.
