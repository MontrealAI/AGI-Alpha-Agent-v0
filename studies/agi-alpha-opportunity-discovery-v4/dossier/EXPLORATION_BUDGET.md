# Opportunity exploration budget and decision rule

Declared after the author rejected the premature microgrid flagship, before selection of a replacement or construction of its experiment.

Three parallel researchers investigate two materially different serious candidates each. A fourth prior-art challenge inspects laboratory protocol transfer. Target bound: eight focused search queries and four deep primary sources per candidate. No paid API calls, purchases, contact with prospective customers or hardware access. Extra lookups may resolve a concrete contradiction and must be recorded. This is a research-time budget, not a dollar expenditure claim; hosted tokens and costs are unavailable.

Each candidate must record beneficiary and useful outcome; observed pain versus conjectured demand; current products and research; specific remaining gap; why current intelligence could change feasibility; required construction; smallest decisive experiment; rejection condition; rights, responsibilities, participation and financing hypotheses.

Selection criteria, each rated zero to three as ordinal judgment rather than calibrated probabilities:

1. Independently motivated consequence.
2. A specific gap after prior-art comparison.
3. Ability to falsify the proposal with available evidence.
4. A substantive reusable instrument.
5. Ability to test technical-commercial complementarity.
6. A credible fresh-recipient transfer test.

Hard gates: a useful purpose distinct from a manufactured benchmark; no readily available incumbent already meeting the proposed claim; an inspectable decisive test; and explicit limits for unavailable field evidence.

Stop or change direction if the gap disappears under a strong comparator, the experiment merely measures solver speed on a supplied formulation, or confirmation of the claimed usefulness requires evidence unavailable in this edition. Retain rejected candidates and reasons. The dossier and selection record precede new flagship code.

## Second exploration round

The first six candidates and thermal grounding challenge did not establish a sufficiently convincing flagship. A second portfolio was authorized: three researchers, at most five focused queries and three deep primary sources each. It considers scientific compute-to-artifact handoffs, purpose-preserving scientific evaluation, and manufacturing lifecycle evidence. The criterion is useful added capability relative to the recipient’s real alternatives; neither universal algorithmic novelty nor observed paying customers is required before a bounded hybrid experiment. Existing efficient complete solutions remain controls.

A separate small qualification pilot was authorized for the independently reported Dask/Zarr failure: reported and authoritative current versions, at most four executions per environment including conventional controls. This checks whether a problem remains before selecting or constructing a reusable instrument. Read-only package downloads and isolated local tests incur no purchase; complete hosted research cost remains unavailable.
