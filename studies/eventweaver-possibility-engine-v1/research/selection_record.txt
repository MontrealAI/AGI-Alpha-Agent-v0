SELECTED BEFORE IMPLEMENTATION: 28 October 2026

EventWeaver: verified, independently scoped delayed-veto temporal instruments.

Need: an OEM or experimenter needs multiple precisely specified event predicates to become an executable measurement/feedback kernel. The first acceptance is functional design acceptance. Field timing and hardware integration are separate gates. The current study makes no instrument-performance claim.

Prior-art challenge changed the proposal. Basic coincidence counting, time tagging, and single conditional filtering are already offered by free NIST/BrightEyes and commercial suppliers. The narrower opening is independent multistage predicates with endpoint-veto and simultaneous-event semantics, generated and checked as a complete transferable design pack. Existing custom SDK/FPGA routes remain practical alternatives.

Six candidate families reviewed in opportunity_challenge.json. Privacy-preserving collaboration has consequential value but would leave governance/site access as the binding unexecuted dependency. Assay/protocol generation has strong free tools and unperformed physical validation. Flexible-load contracts overlap neither this paper nor actual technical delivery but require utility/site authority. Generic compute-placement and production-feasibility studies are already actively owned and excluded.

Newly feasible is a hypothesis about cost-adjusted bespoke delivery, not a claim that event logic became mathematically possible in 2026. Current models, formal verification, and reusable contract representations can potentially make small orders economic. The campaign must test the commercial bridge; benchmark announcements alone cannot establish it.

Commercial comparison must include competent engineers using current AI, free HDL tools, existing time-tagger software, waiting and doing nothing. The product is not exclusive intellectual property in coincidence counting. Value could come from specification, evidence, support and nonexclusive reuse arrangements. If these do not cover full costs, stop expansion and preserve the instrument.
