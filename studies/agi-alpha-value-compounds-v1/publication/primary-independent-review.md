# Independent review of the completed first repeat

**Scope:** all 36 first-repeat Workbench packets: twelve registered source transitions and three arms. Later timing repeats were not inspected. This review checks original artifact integrity, source and release identity, inventories, outcome consistency, recorded independent validation and interpretation. It does not rerun the entire comparison campaign or claim a blinded external customer audit. The reviewer authored the conventional comparator and independent binding evaluator; that relationship is explicit.

## Review decision

The first-repeat evidence is internally consistent and source-bound. Every frozen file, retained native file, captured dependency source and consumer source checked in this review matches its declared identity. All 36 packets pass the unchanged native Workbench inspector, which verifies packet/compilation/artifact binding; that acceptance supplies no operational authority.

The strong conventional arm delivers twelve accepted **bounded source assessments**. The custom retained arm delivers nine accepted assessments and three requiring correction under the frozen checker-agreement criterion. Choosing the conventional component for the local offering is supported by this record. Neither result establishes that twelve dependency upgrades are safe, that twelve customer jobs were delivered, or that the investment pays back.

The important product limitation is substantial: **no affected captured consumer call is established in primary confirmation**. Every consumer call remains unresolved, and the separate candidate channel also establishes no affected call. The historical Starlette/HTTPX example therefore remains the concrete end-to-end consumer demonstration; it must not be relabeled fresh confirmation. The primary study supports upstream binding evidence, conservative abstention, reproducible delivery and method selection, with an incomplete consumer-impact layer.

## The three binding losses and the six Griffe alerts

All three independently validated lost-call witnesses occur in **one** transition, FastAPI 0.159.1 → 0.160.0, in `fastapi/dependencies/utils.py`:

| Declaration | Removed keyword implicated by the witness |
| --- | --- |
| `request_body_to_args` | `known_param_names` |
| `request_params_to_args` | `known_param_names` |
| `solve_dependencies` | `_request_param_names` |

Both new methods find these same three local declaration changes. The conventional Griffe output also alerts on all three removals. They are implementation helper declarations with source-bound signature evidence; the study has not established three supported public-API regressions, three independent causes, or actual impact on Alpha's captured calls.

The other three Griffe alerts are retained rather than discarded:

- Requests v2.34.0 → v2.34.1 removes the modeled object `requests._types.HeadersUpdateType`, outside the direct-callable binding endpoint.
- urllib3 2.7.0 → 2.8.0 changes the reported default expression for `BaseHTTPResponse.stream(amt)` and `HTTPResponse.stream(amt)` from `2 ** 16` to `_READ_CHUNK_SIZE`. These are broader expression-change alerts; they do not by themselves prove a changed runtime value or behavior.

All 69 conventional changed-module Griffe captures report `complete` for that static operation. This does not override the narrower evaluator's scope exclusions. Six alerts therefore means six retained review signals, not six independently demonstrated customer defects. The custom method has no demonstrated coverage advantage over this stronger reusable conventional arm on the primary binding endpoint.

## Consumer impact: complete census, unresolved assessment

The 56 captured consumer files contain **10,172 distinct AST call nodes**. An exact per-file multiset comparison of start position, end line and source snippet against parsed `ast.Call` nodes found no omitted or duplicated call nodes in either arm's emitted census. Nested calls sharing a start position remain distinct in this check.

The same captured source is assessed across twelve dependency transitions, giving 122,064 call-node evaluations per new-method arm. Those repetitions are not 122,064 new source sites or jobs. Every evaluation is unresolved in the strict channel. No extra affected-call candidate is produced by the qualified re-export/import-guard channel.

For each FastAPI transition, triage identifies 57 declared dependency-import references; the remaining census entries are other-import references or unattributed calls. In the first FastAPI transition, the 57 split into 19 cases with unavailable producer-module source and 38 cases without an available source-pinned supported signature. The latter includes the captured `FastAPI(...)` construction. Thus a complete call census is not complete impact assessment.

For the Requests and urllib3 transitions, none of the captured calls is triaged as a direct reference to that dependency by this lane. This does not prove that Alpha does not use those packages: wrappers, dynamic imports, transitive calls and paths outside the captured scope can matter. No recall or sensitivity rate can be inferred from this dataset without separately established consumer-impact ground truth.

The useful primary action is therefore to inspect the identified upstream changes and unresolved scope before obtaining project-specific integration evidence. A report that cannot resolve consumer dispatch cannot serve as an application all-clear.

## Retained-candidate rejection: one mechanism, five manifestations

The retained arm fails on `urllib3-2`, `urllib3-3` and `urllib3-4`. Across those packets, five callable rows disagree with the verifier: `Retry.__init__` appears in three successive transitions, and `WrappedSocket.__init__` appears in two.

The same coarse class-attribute target rule explains all five. The candidate's inventory does not count assignment through an attribute as class-name rebinding; the comparator and checker conservatively exclude the parent class whenever such an assignment is present. The actual statements are persistent assignments to `Retry.DEFAULT` and `WrappedSocket.makefile`. They do not directly replace the constructor or rebind the class name.

The frozen rejection stands because checker agreement was required. However, the verifier did not produce a missed-loss witness for any of these rows. The separate postconfirmation signature-only isolation returns no binding loss for all five pairs. These results justify describing a scope-screen disagreement and conservative abstention, not five false all-clears about actual broken calls.

Full exact-source evidence, mechanism, grouping and limitations are in `publication/confirmation-failure-analysis.md`. The earlier partial review is retained as `confirmation-failure-analysis-v1.md`; the original v1 diagnosis and its output are untouched. The completed first-repeat extension is `results/postconfirmation-diagnostics/constructor-scope-v2.json` with its separately named diagnostic script. No postconfirmation repair or outcome was substituted into the primary record.

## Presentation and operating boundaries

The original HTML includes the necessary statement that absence of an established affected call does not prove project compatibility. Nevertheless, its generic action text still says “No supported binding loss found” in rejected candidate packets, while the header says `ASSESSMENT_REQUIRES_CORRECTION`. The publication overlay should lead those examples with the rejection and its reason; it must not recycle the generic action as a clearance decision. Original packets should remain inspectable unchanged.

The original French HTML retains some English action, qualification and limitation text, and neither language has clickable evidence links. The separate fully translated publication overlay can address these shortcomings, provided the additional editorial/integration work is identified and charged. The frozen automatic output must not be presented as having performed that extra work.

All packets retain `HOLD` admission, empty authority, no settlement connection, zero actual customer receipts, and unknown complete monetary costs. `reviewMinutes: 1` and `reviewCapacityMinutes: 1` are template capacity fields, not measured reviewer or customer effort. Runtime model calls are zero for these deterministic analysis runs; construction inference remains unknown. Local integrity and method acceptance do not constitute production qualification, automatic customer authorization or paid demand.

The first-repeat analysis receipts sum to approximately 2.050 CPU seconds for native inventory/diffs, 137.578 for retained assessment, and 139.943 for conventional assessment. These are different delivered scopes and a single declared timing repeat, not a fair stand-alone speed-win claim. They exclude complete research, construction, acquisition, integration and maintenance costs. Later repeated executions may inform timing uncertainty; they cannot increase the count of substantive transitions.
