# Human-Market Advantage: financial results and implementer reconciliation

**Status:** postoutcome analysis of the registered, completed campaigns. All monetary outcomes and buyer choices below are **simulated**. Actual customer collections are **USD 0**. This analysis was written by the economic implementation owner; it is not the independent reviewer's audit.

## Finding

The registered model demonstrates a connected, economically justified two-investment cycle in **270 of 2,808 primary scenario–seed worlds**. Every qualifying primary world uses the **USD 99 advertised human-service reference**, a **10% or 15% discount**, and the **low or base cost schedule**. There are **no qualifying primary cycles at USD 20 or USD 25**, no qualifying cycles at a 5% discount, and none under the high cost schedule. These are counts in a deliberately constructed sensitivity grid, not estimates of market-success probabilities.

The study therefore demonstrates a conditional financial mechanism around an executed bounded service. It does not establish paying demand, a representative human-market price, observed complete dollar costs, or commercially realized self-financing. The model remains particularly sensitive to trust, first-use integration, customer acquisition, review, useful reuse, and capability lifetime. The frozen production tests establish support for specified synthetic work; the assumed field demand and financial terms remain separate.

Authoritative sources: [primary aggregation](primary-final/aggregation.json), [all primary cells](primary-final/cell-summary.csv), [world outcomes](interpretation/primary-world-outcomes.csv), and the registered [primary protocol](../protocol/economics-primary/campaign-primary.json).

## What was executed and what was assumed

The actual bounded offering processes a public or synthetic UTF-8 CSV using explicit customer rules and returns cleaned records, duplicates, exceptions, a quality report, preserved-source evidence, and one scoped automated revision packet. Twenty-four fresh primary inputs contain 800–1,600 rows, split equally among simple cleanup, locale normalization, and versioned-record reconciliation. The measured support profiles are G0: 8 accepted cases; G1: 16; G2: 24. Each supported family passes 8/8 exact acceptance cases. Unsupported cases remain failures; these finite synthetic cases do not estimate field reliability. See [production profiles](../evidence/production-profiles.json) and [acceptance results](../results/primary/acceptance.json).

The financial simulation uses these measured support profiles and measured CPU resources. It does not invent a compute-speed improvement. Economic value comes from enabling additional fresh job classes and spreading shared expenses across more accepted work. Monetary conversion, residual attention, development cost, demand, quality beliefs, collection behavior, maintenance, and useful lifetime are declared scenario inputs. The study did not receive an invoice establishing that a capability actually cost USD 25, 100, or 400 to construct; those are sensitivity allocations, not empirical cost bounds.

The human-price panel is **USD 20 / 25 / 99**, observed as public advertised service prices with differing scope certainty. USD 99 is the most detailed retrieved match for the output packet and row count; USD 20 is a lower-price cleanup/report anchor; the USD 25 source's exact row limit was not retrieved. Versioned reconciliation was not individually quoted by those providers. Posted prices establish neither professional competence nor equivalent accepted human outcomes. Ordinary Python, Excel, or Power Query use by human service providers is allowed; human-only authorship is unverified. No comparison against another AI provider is required or claimed.

The authoritative [pricing methodology](../protocol/PRICING_METHODOLOGY.json) replaced an earlier 20/49/99 discovery panel before confirmation. The evidence file retains that early recommendation as discovery history; USD 49 is excluded from the executed grid because its 500-row tier does not match the primary inputs. The frozen configuration's generic price-evidence path typo is separately documented in [METADATA_ERRATUM.json](../protocol/economics-primary/METADATA_ERRATUM.json). No executed parameter was changed by those reader notes.

## Why low-price first purchases fail in the primary model

The model gives the human option perceived quality 1, the enterprise 0.98, equal five-minute customer review, a buyer value of 1.5 times the human price, a USD 25 error loss, and a USD 3 first-use integration cost. There is no unmeasured turnaround premium. Before any outcome was inspected, the protocol recorded the following first-use enterprise advantage, in dollars:

\[
\Delta U = H d - 0.02(1.5H + 25) - 3.
\]

At a USD 20 reference and the largest primary discount, 15%, this is −USD 1.10. At USD 25 it is −USD 0.50. At USD 99, a 5% discount still loses by USD 1.52, while 10% gains USD 3.43. The primary low-price and 5%-discount failures follow from these explicit assumptions. They are not empirical evidence that real buyers would refuse those prices. Beliefs are fixed; rejection or refund does not update later trust. The separate low-trust condition varies the belief but does not estimate a learning process.

### Cash that can actually fund the next modeled investment

The selected full policy has no closing receivables or unpaid obligations. Its USD 6,803.79 cash includes seed principal. Subtracting USD 500 fixed reserve and USD 600.76 fiscal reserve leaves USD 5,703.03 protected cash availability. The financing rule additionally excludes seed-generated resources. Its eligible operating-generated balance is therefore only **USD 2,203.03**.

The same amount follows from collections of USD 5,167.80 less recurring costs USD 1,864.01, setup USD 300, capability investments USD 200, and fiscal reserve USD 600.76. The USD 1,000 historical allocation was paid from seed; it is excluded only from the operating-generated funding calculation, while remaining included in complete project profit. This separation does not create cash. Full project profit is USD 1,803.79 and both initial capital and reserves remain visible.

The fiscal reserve is a prudential 20% assumption, increased to 35% in stress. It is not an identified statutory tax liability or a claim that actual tax has been paid. Known modeled refund obligations are accrued at collection and protected before investment. Fee charges are retained even on subsequent refunds.

## Break-even workload, repayment, and downside

The supplemental algebra uses the frozen parameters; it does not run new economic worlds or tune a scenario after seeing outcomes. For a newly supported class, expected contribution per accepted job before fiscal reserve is

\[
m = P p_{\mathrm{collect}}(1-p_{\mathrm{refund}}-f)
    - \frac{c_{\mathrm{attempt}}}{q},
\]

where the collection fee is retained on refunded payments and every failed attempt is costed. For a conservative every-attempt-new-buyer case, provider onboarding cost is also divided by acceptance probability \(q\). Fiscal reserve reduces positive contribution; the conservative investment budget includes complete construction plus maintenance for the entire declared lifetime, without crediting maintenance's reserve reduction. Acquisition, screening, and fixed overhead are common to the paired policies and remain in their complete ledgers; the algebra is an incremental capability threshold, not a full-service unit-cost estimate or demand forecast.

For the selected base case, \(P=89.10\), collection probability 0.98, refund probability 0.02, fee 0.10, and per-attempt delivery/review/support USD 11.85 give expected incremental contribution **USD 64.98984** before reserve and **USD 51.991872** after the 20% reserve. A USD 100 build plus all 52 weeks of USD 1 maintenance is conservatively covered by **three accepted useful reuses for existing buyers**, or **four if every attempt needs new-buyer provider onboarding**. Those thresholds are conditional on the prerequisite capability remaining available and on actual acceptance, collection, and buying choices. They do not turn the zero-sale low-price scenarios into viable businesses.

Across funded primary first stages, 292/295 repay by the terminal paired criteria; protected-cash first crossing occurs 3–26 weeks after purchase, with median six weeks among the 292 crossings. Across funded second stages, 272/279 repay; crossing takes 3–19 weeks, median five among the 272 crossings. Two first stages and three second stages have no useful accepted reuse. One first stage and three second stages have a positive accounting gain but a nonpositive protected-cash gain. Complete distributions are in [stage summary](interpretation/primary-stage-summary.json) and [all funded stages](interpretation/primary-stage-outcomes.csv).

For example, `p9900-d1000-base-sparse-expanded`, seed 26001, gains USD 20.68 from its second investment on an accounting basis but **loses USD 3.46 after protected reserves**. It fails the cycle criterion despite positive overall project profit. In `p9900-d1500-base-obsolescence-8`, seed 26005, the second capability has one useful accepted reuse, a USD 40.12 incremental project loss, and USD 52.10 protected-cash loss. In low-demand seed 26006, the second capability receives no useful reuse at either 10% or 15% discount. All nine worlds with a funded second stage but no qualifying connected cycle are retained in [the complete failure list](interpretation/primary-funded-second-stage-failures.json).

## Separately registered buyer-integration diagnostic

The favorable boundary removes only buyer first-use integration disutility. Provider onboarding, review, acquisition, fees, reserves, and every other cost remain. It contains 27 cells, 216 worlds, and 648 histories, reported separately from the primary grid. There are **80 qualifying cycles**: 9/72 at USD 20, 23/72 at USD 25, and 48/72 at USD 99. This demonstrates sensitivity to an unmeasured friction assumption, not observed low-price adoption.

The diagnostic also illustrates why historical recovery must remain separate. `p2000-d1500-low-diagnostic-zero-buyer-integration`, seed 26008, qualifies for a two-stage operating-financed cycle while retaining a **USD 22.27 complete project loss** after the USD 250 historical allocation. Its operating-generated availability is USD 172.18. Profitable deployment, recovery of prior research, and financing new capability development are distinct claims. All 270 qualifying primary cycles happen to recover their declared historical allocation, but that coincidence is not built into the operating-cycle definition.

## Reconciliation, preservation, and reproduction

The implementer reconciliation replays all **8,424 primary and 648 diagnostic histories** and verifies exact cash-event identities, invoices, paid and unpaid bills, complete cost categories, refunds at collection, seed-paid history, closing reserves, actual collections equal to zero, and generated-funds affordability immediately before every investment. There are **zero reconciliation errors**. Separate assertions establish that every qualifying cycle retains positive absolute protected cash in both investment policies. These are implementation-owner checks; independently authored review is documented elsewhere in the companion.

The original primary campaign has 2,808 worlds and the diagnostic 216; neither was rerun for analysis. All frozen economic and production sources remain unchanged. The initial postoutcome analysis encountered a classifier key error on zero-sale histories; its first script and outputs are preserved in `analysis/history/` and `analysis/primary/` / `analysis/boundary/`. The classifier correction did not alter cash calculations or campaigns. The complete corrected reconciliation is in `primary-final/` and `boundary-final/`.

The first analytic break-even table omitted the acceptance divisor on the every-attempt-new-buyer onboarding sensitivity. The simulation itself already charged first-attempt onboarding correctly. The corrected analytic tables are [analytic-break-even-v2.json](interpretation/analytic-break-even-v2.json) and [CSV](interpretation/analytic-break-even-v2.csv); the reason and ten changed integer-threshold rows are retained in [analytic-correction.json](interpretation/analytic-correction.json). The worked example's three/four reuse thresholds are unchanged. Earlier analytic artifacts remain available and should not be treated as the current table.

## Claim boundary

The evidence supports an executable bounded CSV offering, exact acceptance on fresh synthetic work, an internally consistent model of complete costs and protected cash, and conditional two-stage reinvestment under part of the registered human-price panel. The evidence does **not** yet establish actual sales, actual accepted customer outcomes, observed full costs, representative human-market pricing, field reliability, repeated real capability construction paid from earnings, graph superiority, an economic moat, or autonomous self-acceleration. No token issuance, appreciation, internal transfer, wallet action, or live settlement creates any modeled surplus.
