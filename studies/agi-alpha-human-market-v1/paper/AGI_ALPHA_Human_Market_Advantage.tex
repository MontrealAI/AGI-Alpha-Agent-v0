\begin{abstract}
Can an automated service sell a useful outcome slightly below the price of equivalent human-delivered work, retain available operating surplus, and finance capabilities that repay their cost on fresh work? We screen six digital-work families and implement a narrow nonpersonal CSV cleanup service. The customer-facing outcome is a cleaned file, explicit exceptions, duplicate and supersession links, a complete record-disposition ledger, reproducible rules and a verified provenance packet. Separately authored acceptance confirms 8, 16 and 24 of 24 primary jobs across three progressively enabled configurations; all other primary attempts correctly refuse unsupported scope. The final configuration averages 0.176 seconds of inclusive process wall time, which is not a complete commercial cost.

Published human-service advertisements anchor a frozen \usd{20}/\usd{25}/\usd{99} price panel and 5--15\% discounts. Complete monetary costs, buyer behavior and collections remain scenario assumptions. In 2,808 paired simulated worlds, 270 satisfy a strict two-investment cycle: collected operating resources fund both investments, each capability receives useful fresh-model reuse, both stages add positive project value and protected cash, and operating-generated funds remain. Every qualifying world uses the \usd{99} reference; the lower prices fail the declared initial buyer-choice rule. A selected first-seed, middle-cost path charges \usd{89.10}, accepts 59 jobs, incurs \usd{57.02} fully allocated cost per accepted job, and ends with \usd{2,203.03} available operating-generated funds after its declared reserves and investments. These are conditional simulated outcomes, not actual revenue. An independently reconstructed recipient passes all six fresh transfer jobs, so scarcity and an exclusive capability moat are not established. The evidence supports a functioning bounded service and a conditional economic mechanism, not realized commercial success or general intelligence.
\end{abstract}

\section{Question, claim boundary and prior evidence}
The commercial benchmark is the price of equivalent competent human-delivered services. A service can be commercially useful without defeating every other AI system, specialist algorithm or graph selector. Human providers may themselves use ordinary Python, Excel, Power Query or AI assistance. The comparison concerns the delivered service, not manual keystrokes, and does not establish superiority over all available providers.

Production efficiency and price answer different questions. The selling rule here is a modest discount to a substantiated service reference, not a small markup on inference cost. A low execution cost is useful only if the result has the promised scope, someone chooses to buy it, complete operating obligations can be met, and the remaining collected resources can fund a worthwhile next capability. Customer savings do not enter the seller's ledger. Gross margin is not automatically reinvestable cash.

The study is additive to the current AGI ALPHA foundations. It uses exact source from the existing portable SUCCESSOR Workbench, the TRANSFORMATIONAL INTELLIGENCE source-understanding module, the AGI Jobs evidence compiler, and prior canonical cash and funding helpers. It does not replace the installed operator, reopen held native execution, change validator governance, or treat a local research packet as release qualification. All public research changes are confined to a new study namespace, coordinated through issue \#4850\cite{alpha,coordination}.

Earlier negative results remain negative. The Second Investment study found the same six accepted decisions in three branches and no qualifying second capability; its adapter was slower and did not earn its complete cost. The Verified Capability study found equal accepted decisions for registry and graph and no incremental economic graph advantage. The Capability Advantage study accepted all 144 engineering evaluations but found no qualifying later-investment bundle; independent reconstruction succeeded\cite{second,verified,capability}. Those experiments tested their frozen technical and economic questions. They were not retrospectively repriced or rewritten to answer the present human-market question. This study reuses their applicable mechanisms and preserves their conclusions.

\subsection{Legal support remains a serious but gated opportunity}
The existing legal lane owns synthetic Ontario mutual-NDA change-control support for qualified counsel. This study did not duplicate its implementation, inspect confidential client data, give client-specific advice, file or negotiate. A bounded support packet can identify source-linked obligations, playbook deviations and proposed changes, but professional judgment and responsibility cannot be credited unless actually supplied.

The most specific observed Ontario listing charges \$599 plus HST for one NDA up to 15 pages, a written risk summary and recommended changes; redlining and negotiation are excluded, and redlining is separately advertised from \$250. The Canadian denomination is inferred from its Ontario context rather than explicitly labeled on the retrieved page\cite{flatly}. This is not a defensible price for an unreviewed factual packet. Qualified review, material omissions, false positives, source accuracy, inappropriate revisions and escalation must all be assessed and costed before admitting an equivalent service comparison.

The Law Society of Ontario's April 2024 guidance, still linked in current professional resources, requires independent human verification before reliance, competent judgment and supervision, confidentiality, candour and reasonable fees. Its AI-disbursement discussion does not authorize passing off a model charge as professional time. Legal Aid Ontario's December 2025 notice requires its roster lawyers, from January 2026, to confirm compliance with the referenced resources; that roster rule does not apply to every NDA matter\cite{lso,lao}. Ontario's statutory definition also connects legal services to the application of legal principles and judgment\cite{lsa}. These sources support a counsel-support boundary, not a general authorization for autonomous legal practice. Qualified independent legal assessment was unavailable; this lane therefore contributes discovery evidence, not a validated commercial legal offering.

\section{The offering and its human-service price match}
\subsection{What is delivered}
The service accepts one explicitly declared nonpersonal CSV with exactly five columns: \code{record\_id}, \code{category}, \code{date}, \code{amount}, and \code{updated\_at}. The contract fixes UTF-8 handling, explicit delimiters, 1--50,000 records, a 10 MiB input limit and 256 characters per cell. Normalization uses declared category maps and missing markers, exact decimal-to-cent parsing, explicit date grammar and auditable duplicate/version rules. There is no fuzzy entity resolution, enrichment, legal interpretation, Excel workbook editing or inferred missing value.

Each accepted delivery contains the cleaned CSV; exception, duplicate, superseded and disposition files; original bytes; explicit rules and a reusable revision job; a quality report; semantic output; and a manifest binding all promised data files. The actual portable Workbench then packages typed evidence and verifies its native integrity. A source-understanding summary is generated by the existing TRANSFORMATIONAL INTELLIGENCE module. One scoped revision route is implemented and was executed during development, retaining the prior manifest and exact input identity.

The accepted outcome is a complete clean-and-exception packet for the contracted rules. It does not guarantee that every input row is suitable for import. Rule approval, semantic source correction, customer adoption and independent real-world review are outside the executed deterministic service and are modeled or left unresolved explicitly.

\subsection{Buyer-choice model and the initial demand threshold}
The alternatives are the AGI ALPHA supported service, the equivalent human-delivered service, or deferral. The enterprise is not offered when its scope is unsupported, capacity is exhausted, or protected liquidity cannot finance delivery and onboarding. Buyers can choose the human service or decline. No competing AI provider is introduced as an additional success prerequisite.

Baseline buyers value a successful outcome at $1.5H$, believe human quality is 1 and enterprise quality is 0.98, and attach a \usd{25} loss to failure. Both alternatives require five minutes of buyer attention at \usd{35}/hour. First enterprise use imposes an additional \usd{3} buyer integration burden, distinct from the provider's onboarding expense. No monetary speed advantage is credited, even though hypothetical service days are recorded. These are declared structural assumptions, not empirical conversion calibration.

With equal attention and no delay premium, the enterprise's first-use utility advantage is
\begin{equation}
\Delta U = H d-0.02(1.5H+25)-3.
\label{eq:buyer}
\end{equation}
This pre-outcome calculation predicts that \usd{20} and \usd{25} lose the initial deterministic buyer choice even at a 15\% discount, by \usd{1.10} and \usd{0.50}, respectively. At \usd{99}, the required discount exceeds about 6.54\%, so 5\% does not attract first use but 10\% and 15\% can. Confirmation reproducing that pattern is a check on the assumptions and implementation, not newly discovered buyer behavior.

Quality beliefs stay fixed within each condition, even after a failure or refund. The lower-trust condition sets enterprise perceived quality to 0.90; it is a structural stress, not a learned reputation model. The evidence calibrates service scope, advertised prices and measured production profiles. It does not identify demand elasticity, trust or switching behavior. A market probability cannot be estimated from this scenario grid.

\subsection{Available funds and investment admission}
Let $C_t$ be cash, $Q_t$ accrued unpaid obligations, $F$ the fixed reserve, and $R_t$ the prudential fiscal reserve. Protected cash is $L_t=C_t-Q_t-F-R_t$. With cumulative collections $K_t$, all accrued costs $E_t$, and opening historical research $D_0$ actually paid from seed, available operating-generated funds are
\begin{equation}
A_t=\max\{0,\min[L_t,\ K_t-E_t+D_0-R_t]\}.
\label{eq:funds}
\end{equation}
The $D_0$ adjustment separates previously seed-financed research from subsequent operating generation; it does not inject cash. Cash itself still equals initial seed plus collected receipts minus payments. Shared setup, new capability costs, failures and all unpaid obligations remain deducted. Full-project value separately subtracts historical research, so profitable operation and recovery of past research cannot be conflated.

An investment is admitted only if its full cost is affordable from both $A_t$ and protected liquidity, and its conservative projected benefit exceeds construction and maintenance. Forecasts replay only prior simulated opportunities from a trailing eight-week window through the same human/enterprise/defer choice rule. They use a 0.5 benefit haircut, one-week construction lag, the remaining observation horizon and the useful lives of prerequisite capabilities. Future outcomes and uncollected invoices do not finance construction. The first investment cannot precede week four.

The common virtual seed is \usd{5,000}; no further injection occurs. The high schedule pays \usd{4,800} for opening research and setup, leaving only \usd{200}, below the \usd{500} protected reserve. Its failure to begin delivery is therefore modeled undercapitalization, not proof that the technical capability cannot work at high cost.

\section{Frozen economic confirmation}
\subsection{Design and criterion}
The primary protocol contains three references, three discounts, three cost schedules, 13 conditions and eight fixed seeds: 351 cells, 2,808 paired worlds and 8,424 histories. Each world runs the same opportunity and outcome draws for zero investments, at most one investment and at most two. The 52-week service period is followed by settlement runoff; the baseline records balances through week 57. Each campaign has a declared 600-second execution budget and refuses an existing output directory.

Conditions vary arrival probability, buyer integration, provider review, maintenance, useful lifetime, class mix, realized quality and collections, fees and fiscal reserves, prospect acquisition and trust. The base has eight prospective buyers with 0.25 weekly arrival probability and equal class weights. Low/high arrival probabilities are 0.05/0.50; another condition sets arrivals to zero. Other stresses use \usd{10} buyer integration, 45-minute provider review, \usd{5} weekly capability maintenance, eight-week lifetime, 80/10/10\% class mix, 0.9 realized-reliability multiplier with 10\% refunds and eight-week collections, 35\% fiscal reserve with 15\% fees, \usd{20} prospect acquisition, or 0.90 perceived quality.

A separate, predeclared diagnostic sets only buyer integration cost to zero, retaining provider onboarding and all other costs. It has 27 cells, 216 paired worlds and 648 histories. It is not folded into the primary result and cannot rescue unfavorable primary assumptions retroactively.

A full cycle requires two operating-funded investments, accepted uses of the classes actually improved by each capability, positive first-stage incremental project value and protected cash against the zero-investment control, positive second-stage increments against the first-only control, and remaining operating-generated funds. Counts of unchanged jobs after an upgrade do not qualify as capability benefits. A favorable forecast alone does not qualify. Protected-cash repayment is the first positive paired crossing after availability; terminal gains must also remain positive. Full historical-cost recovery is reported separately.

\subsection{Break-even workload, useful lifetime and downside}
Analytic break-even calculations supplement, rather than retune, the frozen paths. For an existing buyer under the middle schedule, expected receipts net of collection failure, refunds and fees at \usd{89.10} are \usd{76.84}. Subtracting direct delivery/support of \usd{11.85} leaves \usd{64.99} per additional accepted job before the fiscal reserve, or approximately \usd{51.99} afterward. A \usd{100} capability plus a full 52 weeks of \usd{1} maintenance therefore needs at least three useful accepted reuses from existing buyers. If every use requires a new buyer's onboarding, the threshold is four. This is an incremental-capability calculation: common prospect acquisition, screening and fixed service overhead remain in the complete service ledger and are not assumed absent.

At \usd{5} weekly maintenance, the corresponding thresholds rise to seven existing-buyer or nine new-buyer reuses. An eight-week lifetime needs three reuses in a much shorter interval: 0.375 useful jobs/week, compared with about 0.058/week for three reuses over 52 weeks. G2's effective life cannot exceed its prerequisite's remaining life. At the \usd{20} reference with a 10\% discount and middle costs, the existing-buyer threshold rises to 52 useful reuses; repeated new-buyer onboarding makes that incremental contribution nonpositive. Even that arithmetic is moot when the initial buyer refuses the offer.

Forecast affordability is necessary but insufficient. Some funded investments fail to repay under subsequent demand and outcome draws. A high upfront research cost can prevent operation before the service gets a chance to earn. Low trust or a modest integration burden can erase the buyer's reason to switch. Expensive acquisition charges resources even for rejected offers. Finite lifetime can end reuse before recovery. The complete output retains all of these downside paths; no extra capital, token appreciation or internal transfer repairs them.

\section{Interpretation and limits}
The positive result is a connected \emph{conditional simulated} mechanism with executed technical foundations. A narrow service produces usable accepted packets, a sourced upper human-service reference supports a price well above the complete cost under some declared allocations, and generated collections can finance two capability stages that add accepted work and repay their incremental costs. This goes beyond showing that an existing asset is cheap to execute. It also goes beyond a large skills archive or a favorable static margin example.

The limits are consequential. First, the commercial price match is provisional. Advertised scope is not demonstrated professional quality, and our five-column contract is narrower than the richest comparison service. Second, the very low human-service references remain relevant; the model does not establish willingness to pay the upper reference when cheaper substitutes are suitable. Third, production acceptance is exact but synthetic and fault-rich, with only eight primary jobs per class. It does not estimate field reliability, real customer remediation burden or business value. Fourth, the study is AI-authored and independently assessed within one session; role separation is not organizational independence.

Fifth, actual complete research and delivery money is unidentified. Nonzero scenario allocations acknowledge real work but cannot replace bills, measured review time or a qualified professional. Sixth, buyer trust, integration, arrivals and willingness to pay are assumed. Fixed beliefs do not respond to failures, and this is not an equilibrium model of a competitive service market. Seventh, both capability configurations were built before the cash simulation. Their retrospective availability supports prospective modeling, not a claim that simulated money literally funded historical development. Eighth, the recipient could reconstruct the capability, so the evidence offers no exclusive moat. Ninth, graph authentication is fixture-only and the frozen graph integration failed. Tenth, the installed native Harness/Fleet and repository-wide release gates were not qualified by this work.

The experiment does not require a paid pilot to study viability, but real commercial success requires more evidence than this study supplies. The next informative work is a same-scope buyer choice with permission to decline, measured onboarding and review, and a costed fresh delivery under that accepted scope. For legal support, qualified independent counsel assessment and an equivalent professional-service or support-only price are additional gates. These are specified next tests, not completed activities or requests for unauthorized outreach.

\section{Conclusion}
The bounded CSV offering works on the registered fresh synthetic tasks, and the declared upper-price scenarios demonstrate that a finite-cash reinvestment loop can repay two capability investments without token income or extra seed. The complete grid also shows substantial failure: lower human-service prices and buyer friction remove initial demand, many costs do not recover, and not every funded investment earns admission afterward.

A useful service can proceed to bounded commercial validation without proving graph superiority or self-acceleration. This evidence supports that next step. It does not establish realized operating profit, a generally autonomous enterprise, rare capability transfer or sustained acceleration. Human-market equivalence, real buyer acceptance and complete observed costs remain the decisive unresolved measurements.
