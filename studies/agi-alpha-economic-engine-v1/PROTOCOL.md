# Collected surplus, bounded reinvestment and distinct settlement routes

**Separate prospective financial amendment v1. No financial scenario results exist at this specification.**

This analysis follows the completed, separately frozen native and operating-cost studies. It does not alter their inputs, endpoints, outcomes or publication. The source-inspected contract register, this protocol, `inputs.json`, `prior_model_anchor.json`, `token_scenarios.json`, and the independent evaluation plan will be hashed and publicly identified before a subsequent recorded authorization to calculate. The current authoring assistant is not a participant, measured commercial service or observed revenue source.

## Questions and evidence classes

Can accepted digital work, under stated demand and complete costs, generate collected resources for further construction before self-acceleration is demonstrated? Which prices help customers relative to competent traditional non-AI work, while also permitting viable provider economics? What does each inspected settlement route actually pay to the modeled worker enterprise?

There are two product profiles. `evidence_pack` imports the exact prior scenario accounts for the bounded four-passage source brief; its native execution is measured, while its field reliability, attention and operating costs remain assumptions. `cost_050_hypothesis` is an explicitly unvalidated offering whose base variable delivery cost is USD0.50 per expected accepted job and whose traditional replacement cost is USD200. It is not a relabeling of the brief or an observed occupation/task. Its whole-business cost includes all imported startup, recurring service/improvement costs and additional commercial overhead. No USD0.50 complete-enterprise cost is claimed.

The USD20-minus-USD0.50 illustration is USD19.50 before other business costs and taxes; half is USD9.75. Customer savings of USD180 are not the enterprise's revenue or reinvestment base. The executable analysis deducts its actual modeled obligations and reserves before allocation. Every cash amount is simulated; actual collected commercial revenue remains unknown, and no collection or transaction is performed.

## Frozen case matrix

The primary matrix is two profiles × three joint scenarios × three prices (USD10/20/40) × four maximum reinvestment fractions (0/25/50/75%) × two capability-outcome worlds = **144 paths**. All paths are reported. The zero-reinvestment reference is a financial policy reference, not a replacement for the human-work comparator. For both profiles, the base USD20 case also uses the predeclared 0.25× and 4× demand sensitivities at all four fractions and both outcome worlds: **32 additional paths**. The 1× cases already belong to the primary matrix and are not additional independent coverage.

Each path has twelve operating months **after** setup. Initial virtual cash is USD25,000, an assumed capital constraint, not new authorization or observed company cash. Setup workdays equal the ceiling of initial provider minutes plus included additional business-setup minutes divided by the utilized daily provider allocation. Setup is paid before service; no revenue is credited during it. This avoids inheriting the preceding paper's unvalidated setup-calendar shorthand. Customer setup and all fixed customer attention remain in its account.

Base, conservative and stress jointly change demand, acceptance/failure paths inherited from the prior model, review burden, support and upkeep, collection, refunds, payment charges and reserves. These ranges are engineering scenarios, not estimated probabilities or confidence intervals. Hypothesis demand is independent of the demonstrated task portfolio; no rate is extrapolated across the labor market.

## Provider, customer and resource accounts

The prior modeled accounts are partitioned by their `.employer.` component names. Every other monetary component belongs to the provider in this explicitly assumed paid-labor operation. All provider hours are paid at the sourced professional compensation proxy. This is neither an actual payroll record nor free founder labor. The partition must sum to the exact prior totals. Recurring service and improvement components, unsuccessful construction and joint unallocated components all stay in the provider account. New acquisition/billing and administration allowances cover additional commercial activities; their included hours are not charged twice.

For the hypothesis base, provider minutes per requested job equal acceptance probability × 0.10 minutes. Nonlabor cost per requested job is the amount needed to make **total variable delivery cost** equal acceptance probability × USD0.50, after pricing that provider attention. In the conservative/stress hypotheses, provider attention is the explicitly frozen one/five minutes per request and nonlabor cost is two/four times the base nonlabor amount. These quantities include rejected work in the denominator construction and remain hypotheses. They do not establish that professional review can actually be compressed to six seconds per accepted job. Occupancy and mandatory fixed accounts carry over as assumptions from the prior service model, not measured behavior of a new USD200 job.

A month's feasible requests are whole numbers bounded by accumulated demand, remaining provider minutes, customer minutes, executor occupied seconds and available cash. Mandatory monthly provider/upkeep minutes are removed before delivery. Initial/refreshed construction and purchased-capacity support use their included provider time. Customer attention cannot be bought away by purchasing another executor. Variable request costs already include all failed, retried and refused paths. Expected accepted units may be fractional and are explicitly expected quantities; no new task diversity or realized acceptances are implied.

## Fixed month order and cash conservation

1. Begin with the previous closing cash. Apply any previously funded candidate's simulated evaluation and any funded executor addition. A failed candidate keeps its cost and produces no benefit. A passed candidate reduces only its frozen cost/time components, from this month onward; quality stays at the inherited assumed rate. Add its cash and time upkeep.
2. Collect only the previous month's accepted invoices, subject to the frozen collection fraction. Deduct refunds on those gross collections, percentage payment charges on gross collections, and fixed charges per collected invoice. The final month's invoices remain uncollected receivables; they never fund current investment. No token sale, collateral return, capital injection or appreciation enters this ordinary-USD dynamic model.
3. Preserve the existing tax reserve, warranty reserve and unspent earmarked research allocation. A one-current-month fixed-cost shutdown reserve is also protected. If cash cannot pay current mandatory operating costs and retain that shutdown reserve, cancel further service, pay one month of shutdown support, retain all obligations/reserves, and collect due invoices without restarting. If even shutdown cannot be funded, record the unpaid obligation and failed plan. Costs never disappear to make cash nonnegative.
4. Otherwise pay mandatory provider/business costs. Demand arrives in integer monthly installments summing to the declared annual demand (remainder assigned to earliest months); backlog carries forward. Admit the largest nonnegative whole request count allowed by demand, time, occupancy and cash after protected balances. Pay all expected request costs now. Accepted jobs alone generate new invoices; rejected/refused jobs still cost money. After cancellation, no further service or demand is admitted.
5. Update cumulative ordinary operating income: collected cash **after** refunds/payment charges, minus startup, all mandatory paid service/business costs and all incurred delivery costs. Discretionary construction is reported separately and deducted in the final combined net result. The tax-reserve target is the frozen fraction of positive cumulative ordinary income; only increases are reserved, with no modeled refund or tax benefit from later losses or discretionary construction. This is a conservative planning reserve, not a jurisdictional tax calculation. Pass-through GST/QST/VAT is outside net prices and must never become provider income.
6. Warranty cash reserve is the frozen fraction of the current and preceding month's gross collections. Working-capital reserve is the specified number of months times current mandatory monthly cost plus current variable cost times the next month's **resource-feasible** demand (including backlog). These are planning buffers, not actuarial guarantees or hard stochastic budget protection.
7. Earned, recovered surplus is the positive cumulative ordinary income after the tax reserve. Initial capital and receivables never enter it. The cumulative allocation ceiling is the chosen fraction of that surplus. Newly earmarked cash is the lesser of the remaining cumulative ceiling and cash remaining after tax, warranty, working-capital and existing earmarks. Allocation is a restriction on cash, not an expenditure. A later loss can reduce the ceiling; there is no new allocation while past cumulative allocation exceeds it. Do not count the same surplus repeatedly each month.
8. Consider at most one funded action at month end. First consider one extra executor only if execution is the binding bottleneck, spare customer/provider capacity can use it, at least two future months remain, and future incremental collection contribution exceeds setup and upkeep. Otherwise consider the next frozen candidate. Spending reduces cash and earmarks once. Provider construction time must fit this month's unused pool. No borrowing against a predicted gain is allowed. Unspent earmarks remain visible and may never be reported as successful investment.

All monthly rows must reconcile opening cash + net cash collections − paid mandatory costs − delivery costs − discretionary spending − any actual payments = closing cash. Reserves are balances, not duplicate expenditures. Net provider result after construction, restricted balances, liquid free cash, receivables and any unpaid obligation are separate outputs.

## Candidate selection, payback and stop rules

The three candidate definitions are frozen in `inputs.json`; no search or tuning against financial results is allowed. The forecast gate values probability-weighted nonlabor/provider-labor savings on remaining feasible demand, minus all remaining upkeep and construction/evaluation cost. It uses current resource constraints and no assumed unobserved quality improvement. A gate cannot spend before enough previously earned earmarked cash exists. Initial capital does not fund discretionary construction.

The `no_gain` world rejects every constructed candidate. In the `assumed_diminishing_gain` world, the first constructed candidate fails and the next two have their explicitly frozen simulated outcomes; later candidates cost more and offer smaller fractional benefits. Outcomes are simulation parameters, **not observed discoveries, independent experimental validations, or proof of a learned improvement process**. Failed construction is expensed and retained. A resource/cash shortfall waits; a negative remaining-horizon payback stops that candidate search. No candidate outside the list is substituted. Quality/acceptance standards and the evaluation outcomes are outside any optimization.

Record construction cost, probability-weighted predicted gain, simulated qualification, subsequent uses, gross savings, upkeep, net return, payback volume, actual spending and idle allocations. Report method gain separately from added execution capacity. More simulated revenue, capital or compute never establishes accelerating improvement.

## Primary customer comparison and break-even outputs

For the actual evidence-pack anchor, compare the same served-request cohort with the prior traditional non-AI workflow at each frozen productive duration. Recompute traditional fixed amortization at this cohort rather than reusing an infeasible thousand-job denominator. Agent-accepted customer cost includes face price plus its own preparation, approval, review, correction and fixed readiness. Complete-demand customer cost additionally funds the entire traditional fallback and handoff after each unresolved request; no partial work receives free credit. Full fallback includes the traditional arm's fixed enablement once whenever unresolved requests are positive. Traditional final acceptance of one remains a favorable assumption, not observed quality.

For the unvalidated USD200 hypothesis, that number is an assumed all-in replacement cost per completed job; fallback pays it in full. The Agent customer's separate modeled attention and fixed costs are still charged. A collected fraction below one is a provider risk; nonpayment must not be described as legitimate customer savings. Customer calculations therefore use full face prices.

Report zero-reinvestment collected-cash break-even price at each actual served cohort, with startup and business costs included; its no-profit tax reserve is zero. Report the maximum customer-beneficial price for complete-demand work, and whether a feasible price interval exists. These are cohort-conditional algebraic diagnostics, not a guarantee the same demand persists after changing price. Report investment reuse thresholds and whether remaining feasible uses meet them. If the slope is nonpositive, capacity is zero, or a threshold exceeds available resources, retain an explicit unavailable/infeasible result rather than zero or infinity disguised as a result.

## Mechanical conventions fixed before calculation

The following definitions resolve implementation choices prospectively; they are not post-result corrections.

- Additional setup and acquisition/billing/administration cash allowances **include** their stated provider hours. Candidate construction/evaluation and monthly upkeep amounts likewise include their stated provider hours. Capacity purchase and upkeep follow the same rule. Hours still consume capacity and are never added a second time as cash.
- If required initial provider plus business cash exceeds initial virtual capital, mark `START_INFEASIBLE`, with the full quoted requirement and shortfall. No partial startup, service or borrowing is invented. A zero incurred amount in a declined simulation is not a zero historical research cost. The actual historical amount remains unknown.
- Working capital is exactly **k × (F + v × Nnext)**: k frozen reserve months, current mandatory monthly cost F, current variable provider cash per requested job v, and next-month resource-feasible requests Nnext including backlog. It restricts **new earmarking**, not service admission. The tax, warranty, existing earmark and one-month shutdown reserves do restrict admission. Recompute/report any reserve-target shortfall; never pretend a reserve is funded when cash is insufficient. Such a shortfall prohibits new investment and is an explicit failed financial constraint.
- New earmark is `min(max(0, fraction*recovered_after_tax_operating_surplus - lifetime_allocation), max(0, cash - tax_reserve - warranty_reserve - working_capital - current_earmarks))`. Lifetime allocation includes money already spent and money still earmarked. Operating surplus here is before this optional appropriation; final combined income and cash deduct all discretionary spending. Mandatory historical construction/upkeep imported from the prior model is already an operating cost.
- Candidate upkeep begins only after simulated promotion. Savings apply multiplicatively to **current** variable nonlabor cash and current variable provider minutes; no reduction applies to fixed costs, customer minutes, acceptance criteria or quality. Subsequent gains therefore diminish in absolute size. The gate subtracts all remaining possible upkeep without a success-probability discount.
- Hypothesis adverse nonlabor is exactly `Q_base * (0.50 - 0.10*provider_hourly_rate/60) * nonlabor_multiplier`, not each adverse scenario's acceptance probability. Base provider minutes are `Q_base*0.10` per request. No new empirical inference follows from this normalization.
- Remaining-demand forecasts are a deterministic resource-only monthly recurrence with the current method, current instances, current fixed/upkeep resource use, carried backlog and frozen future demand. They assume no future discretionary action or promotion and are **resource ceilings, not guaranteed future cash feasibility**. Actual investment must still use recovered, collected, unreserved current cash. Actual monthly service remains cash-constrained, and forecast error is visible in realized simulated payback. Candidate savings in month12 are cash savings; additional-executor payback charges every added request's cost but credits collections only for work served by month11.
- Use the original scenario's `hybrid_handoff_employer_minutes`, copied explicitly into the new anchor. Export both the same-served-cohort comparison and the **annual-demand** fallback requirement; unserved demand also needs full traditional fallback in the latter. Traditional enablement is charged once when any fallback is needed. Report fallback's additional resource requirement and whether the stated traditional/customer pools could meet it. For the USD200 hypothesis, human task duration and fallback human minutes are unknown; full-demand replacement-cost arithmetic is not a claim of verified fallback capacity.
- Provider token quotas, provider rate limits, network tails, daily staffing and live executor concurrency are not newly measured constraints. Their absence from this cash/time model is stated explicitly, so modeled capacity is an upper planning bound subject to those unvalidated dependencies.
- The no-reinvestment price diagnostic holds the actually simulated cohort fixed: let I be the expected number of collected accepted invoices, C all provider startup/business/delivery costs in that horizon, a percentage processing rate, r refund fraction and f fixed processing charge. Break-even face price is `(C/I + f)/(1-r-a)` when I>0 and the denominator is positive; otherwise it is unavailable. It excludes uncollected final-month invoices and does not assume the same demand/cash path after repricing. Customer-beneficial maximum price solves its separately reported full-demand cost equality using all accepted face-price invoices, not only those collected by the provider.
- In a shutdown, cancel and release unused earmarked allocations to meet obligations; record the release and keep lifetime allocation/spending history. Incur the **full** one-month shutdown cost. Pay the amount that cash after protected tax/warranty balances permits and carry the rest as an explicit unpaid obligation. Subsequent due collections repay it before free cash; service/investment never restart. Ordinary/net income and complete costs deduct the entire incurred charge, while cash reconciliation deducts only actual payments. No inability to pay is presented as a cost saving.
- The anchor's traditional comparator pays its initial enablement and all twelve monthly readiness charges for the operating horizon, both in the all-human baseline and once in a fallback plan when any rescue is needed. Agent customer setup is incurred only after a feasible start; customer monthly governance is incurred for operating and shutdown-support months, not later inactive months. Hypothesis USD200 replacement cost is already all-in per completed job and has no additional traditional fixed charge. Handoff applies to **served-but-unaccepted** requests; never-served demand goes directly to the full human workflow without an invented Agent handoff. Separate customer attention, traditional worker time and provider time in all outputs. Where human/fallback duration is unavailable, report it as unknown.
- At month12 there is no next operating month: `Nnext=0` in the working-capital formula, so a still-operating enterprise retains `k*F` as its terminal fixed-cost buffer. Month12 invoices remain uncollected. A stopped enterprise makes no new allocation or investment; only due collections, obligation repayment and reserve reporting continue. For stopped months its forward operating working-capital requirement is zero, without canceling tax, warranty or unpaid obligations.
- The named **zero-reinvestment** break-even price is reported only for allocation-fraction-zero paths. Investing paths instead report a distinctly named **full combined-cost conditional break-even price**, using the same fixed-cohort algebra with every incurred discretionary construction/capacity cost added to C, including failed candidates and all upkeep. They never retain investment gains while treating the construction as free. No retrospective baseline-method rerun is inferred from this price diagnostic.
