# Scientific and experimental-instrument opportunity dossier

**Exploration date:** 27 October 2026. **Status:** opportunity assessment before flagship selection; no implementation, customer interview, sale, or physical experiment was performed in this track.

## Decision for the cross-track selection meeting

Neither broad “AI designs experiments” nor “AI translates laboratory protocols” is a defensible discovery claim. Both have substantial current prior art. **This track returns a negative flagship selection: neither candidate clears the worth-building gate within the evidence budget.** S1 has accessible public computational ingredients but lacks the new observations and laboratory commitment that would establish its scientific value. S2 is consequential but requires laboratory collaboration for its decisive end-to-end claim. Both dossiers remain useful rejected or deferred candidates, not admitted opportunities.

The most useful discovery in this exploration was an exclusion: a protocol firewall, device-independent semantic representation, learned troubleshooting memory, capability routing, and hybrid human/robot execution already appear together in LabCompiler. Constructing that combination again would not establish discovery. Its authors also distinguish execution-grounding metrics from wet-lab outcomes. [S6]

### Declared and used research budget

The assigned ceiling was 12 targeted search queries, 16 opened primary-source URLs, and two serious candidate dossiers, with no large simulator or solution implementation. All 12 query slots and 16 source-open attempts were used. Several URL opens failed or returned a CAPTCHA; these are recorded in `sources.json`. Follow-up `find` operations inspected already opened sources. No monetary research budget was observed, and search counts are not a substitute for token or billing telemetry.

This was bounded investigator-directed exploration. Candidate generation, the proposed instruments, commercial arrangements, and rankings are researcher constructions. Source evidence supports specific problems and existing capabilities; it does not establish demand for our proposed services.

## S1 — An experiment-or-stop instrument for distinguishing competing mechanisms

### Beneficiary and useful outcome

A small experimental research group has two or more plausible mechanistic explanations for its existing time-course data and a limited menu of measurements or interventions it can actually perform. The useful outcome is a defensible next action: a feasible measurement plan that can distinguish the explanations at the stated error tolerance, or an explicit finding that the current instrument and budget cannot answer the question. The beneficiary is the principal investigator or experimental lead deciding whether to commit a new assay campaign, not a model-fitting benchmark owner.

### What is already possible

Structural-identifiability packages, PEtab, PEtab Select, calibration systems, profile likelihood, Bayesian design, and optimal-control methods are available building blocks. The PEtab benchmark repository contains mathematical models paired with experimental data. PEtab Select already standardizes model-selection problems and interoperates with established calibration tools. These should be available to every comparison arm. [S1–S4]

Liu, Maini and Baker distinguish parameter estimation from model discrimination and construct experimental controls for both. Their model-discrimination treatment assumes determined parameters and, for simplicity, noiseless continuous measurements, while acknowledging that actual observations are noisy and discrete. This identifies a limitation of that particular formulation, **not** proof that uncertainty-aware experimental design is absent elsewhere. [S2]

### Specific remaining gap to investigate

The concrete scientific anchor found in the surveyed public material is **histone H4 acetylation mechanism discrimination**. The PEtab Select example uses a 16-state reaction model and relative concentrations measured at a later time treated as steady state. Competing hypotheses concern which acetylation reactions require distinct rate constants. Established search/calibration tools already recover the published best model, and several candidate rankings differ at numerical precision. Repeating that fit would add no meaningful discovery. [S3]

A possible new question is whether a feasible transient measurement can distinguish biologically different mechanisms that remain plausible under those steady-state observations. The new asset would have to connect a real assay's measurable quantities and uncertainty to a defensible discrimination experiment. The search did **not** establish a laboratory currently blocked on this decision, an available transient dataset providing independent acceptance, or a willingness to commission the experiment. Those missing facts are why this candidate is deferred. Fabricating compatible buyers and simulated transient data would not resolve them.

The candidate gap is the mission-specific bridge from an actual scientific decision and a laboratory's finite assay menu to a trustworthy *experiment-or-stop* decision. It requires preserving what the observable means, accounting for confounded mechanisms and uncertain parameters, expressing practical constraints, and providing an independently checkable explanation of why the proposed experiment can or cannot answer the question.

The strongest novelty claim currently supported is **none**. The product hypothesis is that preparing this bridge across unfamiliar models and laboratory contexts is costly enough that expert frontier-model operation plus a reusable instrument can improve the beneficiary's complete delivery cost or turnaround. No source in this search establishes a price, backlog, or unmet willingness to pay for that package. A targeted buyer interview and comparison with a competent systems-biology consultant are prerequisites to a commercial flagship.

### Why frontier intelligence could help now

The plausible role is to interpret the research question, map existing models and data into standard interfaces, identify hidden assumptions, propose feasible measurement changes, and produce a reviewable experimental plan with executable checks. Numerical integration, optimization, and identifiability calculations should remain with established tools. Any benefit may therefore be Operational Alpha in using existing science effectively. A model that merely rephrases an optimizer's answer adds little.

### Binding restriction and transformation witness

| Witness item | Proposed record |
|---|---|
| Original purpose | Distinguish specified competing mechanisms sufficiently to justify a particular research decision. |
| Restriction | Existing data and the default observable may make those mechanisms indistinguishable; a better fit alone does not remove that restriction. |
| Removable part | The group may change a measurement, sampling time, or permitted intervention within its actual capabilities. |
| Alternative | Search jointly over the approved experiment menu and uncertainty-aware decision rule; return an experiment, an information shortfall, or a stop decision. |
| Executable realization | A standards-based model/data adapter, observable-meaning contract, assay-budget constraints, simulator calls, and fresh predictive-discrimination evaluation. |
| Falsifier | The proposed experiment fails the frozen error criterion, relies on an unavailable assay, or offers no accepted-quality cost advantage over a competent existing workflow. |

The transformation changes information acquisition while preserving the scientific decision. Selecting an easier mechanistic distinction after seeing failure would violate the purpose.

### Smallest decisive computational experiment

1. Before any design search, select a public study with accessible model/data files and a consequential, explicit mechanistic question. Verify that its measurement semantics and permitted interventions are described well enough to reproduce. Exclude a case whose only difficulty is a missing dependency.
2. Have an independent reviewer freeze the scientific decision, acceptable discrimination errors, real instrument menu, noise assumptions, budget, and time limit. Use held-out experimental conditions if available; otherwise label generated future observations as simulated and refrain from a physical-discovery claim.
3. Give H, U, E and E+ the same source evidence and access to PEtab-compatible calibration and existing design methods. U is a competent ordinary frontier-model user with tools. E receives an established model-to-decision workflow. E+ additionally receives a capability developed on a different source study, with no target-specific plan or hidden outcomes.
4. Evaluate the delivered experimental plan and its stop decisions independently on protected observations. Measure complete construction and verification effort, accepted-quality cost, false assurance, and useful abstention. Report source-model families as the independent units, not thousands of simulated trajectories as independent scientific discoveries.
5. Give the selected formulation to the original ordinary/expert solver. If its advantage disappears, attribute the value to discovered structure or transfer, not to an intrinsically better solver or a general invention policy.

A first pilot may use two model families for development and two protected families for confirmation. That is an economical scoping test, not proof of cross-domain superiority. Reusing a familiar logistic example alone should not qualify as this edition's flagship.

### Commercial mechanism and uncertainties

The initial offer would be a bounded “next experiment or justified stop” dossier plus an executable instrument, with acceptance attached to the frozen decision contract. H should be a matched specialist-service quotation or explicitly estimated hours; there is no sourced market price in this track.

A possible arrangement is a small group of laboratories funding a shared, rights-cleared adapter and observable-validation layer while paying separately for private mission adaptation. Coordination could help amortize the common instrument, but no coalition is assumed to form. Each participant may decline, withdraw, use existing tools, or keep its data private. A common model-error event can defeat all buyers simultaneously. Initial cash must cover construction, failed adaptation, and validation before final payment; refundable deposits cannot be treated as earned surplus.

The causal comparison is a two-by-two: conventional mission-specific construction versus a reusable instrument, crossed with independent purchasing versus a real or modeled coordinated commissioning arrangement. Permit both baselines to buy existing tools and propose alternatives. Count coordinated-demand benefits only after incremental contracting, support, refund and maintenance costs.

### Successor asset and stopping boundary

The reusable asset would be an executable interface and qualification method connecting a scientific decision to observable semantics, uncertainty and permitted experiments. Generic algebraic checks may transfer. Scientific meaning, plausible mechanisms, noise, allowed interventions, acceptance and authority must be rechecked for each mission. If existing specialist software plus competent prompting already meets the same envelope, retain only demonstrably useful adaptation components and stop further exploration.

**Assessment:** plausible scientific decision; substantial conventional competition; the specific proposed new observation and participation are unverified. **Rank within this track: 1, deferred at the worth-building gate.** A generic experiment-design engine is insufficient, and the existing detector or successor Harness should be reused if a later real mission supplies the missing evidence.

## S2 — A laboratory relocation qualification service with explicit evidence boundaries

### Beneficiary and useful outcome

A laboratory or shared core facility wants to move a protocol from one instrument configuration to another without silently changing the experiment. The useful outcome is an auditable acceptance, rejection, or bounded adaptation of that specific transfer, including which operations still require measurement or expert confirmation. A syntactically valid robot script is insufficient.

### What prior art already accomplishes

LabOP provides an open laboratory protocol representation and platform specializations, including manual and automated execution. ACRA extracts literature procedures, translates them to XDL, simulates hardware-specific execution, and reports six physical syntheses across two robotic platforms. Therefore neither protocol standardization nor literature-to-robot translation can be claimed as a new mechanism here. [S5, S7]

LabCompiler is especially close. Its October 2026 preprint separates protocol diagnosis/repair, semantic representation and device-specific execution; it uses retained troubleshooting knowledge and capability-based routing. The study reports both controlled and naturally occurring protocol errors, with finite device/action coverage and wet-lab evaluation left for later work. The broad candidate was consequently rejected during exploration. [S6]

### Narrower gap that may remain

The candidate service would qualify a *specific relocation* using measured instrument capability and calibration evidence, including incompatible calibration state, changed units, dead-volume assumptions, mixing or timing assumptions, and any required human handoff. It would preserve a trace from the original useful assay purpose to the target realization and distinguish computational consistency from empirical assay equivalence.

This is a hypothesis about an inadequately served operational boundary, not a claim that LabOP, device vendors or laboratory experts cannot address it. A qualified laboratory partner must confirm that a real transfer repeatedly fails or costs too much after ordinary vendor support and current software are considered. Without that evidence, this is an attractive but unproven service concept.

### Why frontier intelligence could change feasibility

An expert agent might reconcile protocol prose, device manuals, calibration records and platform APIs, identify unsupported assumptions, construct executable checks, and prepare a focused validation experiment. Existing compilers and simulators should remain in the workflow. The agent should abstain from inventing missing physical parameters. Its residual human obligation is laboratory authorization and empirical validation where genuinely required, not an arbitrary fixed review surcharge.

### Transformation witness

| Witness item | Proposed record |
|---|---|
| Original purpose | Obtain an assay outcome meeting its original acceptance and uncertainty requirements on the target configuration. |
| Restriction | A valid source protocol and compatible API are often treated as sufficient transfer evidence. |
| Removable part | Separate stable protocol structure from target-specific physical assumptions and evidence obligations. |
| Alternative | A relocation packet that makes unmet evidence visible and orders the smallest informative qualification measurements. |
| Executable realization | Compile through existing representations; check device limits and source-to-target semantic obligations; schedule empirical tests where computation cannot close the obligation. |
| Falsifier | The system passes a transfer that fails the original assay, cannot detect a harmful change caught by ordinary vendor tooling, or costs more without improving accepted outcomes. |

### Smallest decisive experiment

Begin with benign calibration or measurement protocols for which public specifications and real target-device evidence exist. Freeze source and target identities, instrument limits, acceptance criteria, and changes before the agent sees the protected outcomes. Compare established vendor/standard tooling plus competent AI assistance against the expert method, and then a fresh recipient with a capability learned on another relocation. Use the original protocol author or an independent domain specialist to assess semantic preservation; do not rely solely on an LLM judge.

The computational part can test detection of unsupported transfers, reproducibility of compilation and preservation of explicit constraints. The decisive end-to-end test needs physical measurements from the target platform. This environment cannot produce that evidence. Simulated device success cannot be sold as validated assay portability.

### Commercial mechanism and uncertainties

A core facility, instrument vendor or laboratory network might commission common qualification adapters and maintain shared calibration schemas. Individual missions would still pay for private adaptation and physical testing. A milestone arrangement could fund generic construction first, with final payment contingent on mission acceptance. The seller must reserve for failed qualification, refunds and support; buyer deposits impose obligations.

Participants have meaningful outside options: vendor application scientists, internal automation engineers, existing LabOP/XDL workflows, manual execution, or abandoning the relocation. The shared adapter and coordinated demand may be complementary, but common device errors or calibration drift can invalidate several missions simultaneously. No price, procurement commitment or customer demand has been observed here.

### Successor asset and stopping boundary

The retained asset would be a versioned device-evidence adapter and qualification procedure, not a universal laboratory intelligence. Reuse requires matching device firmware, calibration status, relevant settings, material class, protocol scope and rights. Each new assay must earn fresh semantic and empirical acceptance; prior execution must never grant authority to operate laboratory hardware.

**Assessment:** consequential and potentially reusable; broad construction substantially preempted by current prior art; decisive evidence requires physical partners; no buyer evidence. **Rank within this track: 2, defer as flagship; pursue only with a concrete laboratory transfer and agreed measurements.**

## Other broad directions screened out

Generic AI-assisted computational reproduction is not a fresh gap by itself. Xu and Yang's latest June 2026 revision reports full-paper replication across 384 studies and 3,523 models, with 92.1% fully or largely reproducible conditional on accessible packages. Earlier search snippets described the initial, smaller version. We used the current primary version, and did not infer that all scientific reproducibility is solved. Any new service would need a more specific unresolved decision boundary and direct comparison. [S8]
