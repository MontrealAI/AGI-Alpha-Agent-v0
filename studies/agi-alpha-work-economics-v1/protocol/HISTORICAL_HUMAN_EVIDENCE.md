# Published human evidence for market-grounded economics

Retrieved 25 October 2026. These are external primary studies, not new participants in AGI ALPHA's experiment. No retrieved study supplies a matched, fully costed no-AI human completion time for the frozen 36-job reconciliation portfolio. The commercial model can therefore use official wage evidence and explicit sensitivity/break-even calculations; it must not label an adjacent study's duration as the observed human time for our job. Recruitment is not required for this modeled comparison.

## Evidence matrix

| Source | Population and task | Published observations | Comparability and permitted use |
|---|---|---|---|
| WREX, CHI 2020, pp. 7–8 | 12 professional data scientists from a software company; mean 4 years' data-science experience; six string/date/coordinate transformation tasks; manual Jupyter coding with internet access | Manual condition passed 12/36 attempts within five-minute limits. Per-task manual entries: 2.5, 5.0, 3.6, 5.0, 4.4, 4.2 minutes; the 5.0 entries include all failures on those tasks. | Strong evidence that qualified workers perform ordinary data operations with non-generative software. Censored microtask timing; no full reconciliation, delivery review, employer overhead or per-accepted-job cost. Do not transfer these times or its tool advantage to Alpha. |
| TASHEEH, EDBT 2024, §5.5 | Five outside computer scientists with data-cleaning expertise; same sample of ten real malformed CSV files; any preferred tool/programming environment allowed | Authors report 67 ±18 minutes manual cleaning across experts, 83 ±17% accuracy; sampled files average 904 ±842 rows and 64 ±47 malformed rows. | Closely related data work, but structural repair is a different contract from exact reconciliation. Public text does not explicitly prohibit AI assistance. Keep authors' batch-level description; do not infer a universal per-file rate or treat ± as a confidence interval without raw data. |
| SpreadsheetBench, 2024 v2, §3, Table 2 | Four Excel experts; 50 instructions with three cases each; formula or VBA solutions required | 71.33% soft/per-case and 62.00% hard/all-cases accuracy; entire human evaluation completed within one day. | Good evidence of professional spreadsheet task demand, realistic formats and multi-case acceptance. No task-level durations or explicit AI prohibition are supplied. Never divide one day by 50 to manufacture a task time. |
| OSWorld, 2024 v2, §3.4 | CS-major students with basic software skills and no previous exposure to samples/software; 369 mixed desktop tasks | 111.94-second median completion time; 72.36% success. Same paper reports 35.38 seconds and 88% on 100 WebArena tasks under its comparison setup. | Measured broad computer-use timing, but unfamiliar students are not the stipulated competent professional reconciler. Whole benchmark median mixes tasks and successes/failures. It is not a costed employer job or transferable latency denominator. |
| OSWorld-Human, 2026 v2, §4.1 | Authors manually construct and verify minimal action trajectories for 369 tasks using source instructions or ground truth | LibreOffice Calc average trajectory: 13.2 single actions, 4.5 grouped action units. | Action counts, not an observed workforce timing distribution. Never assign seconds per action or claim human completion cost from these annotations. Useful for explaining why reusable tools and batching can reduce interface overhead. |
| OSWorld 2.0, July 2026 v2, §2.2 and Appendix G.2 | 108 long workflows; two annotators supply task-time ranges | Median human-annotated expected time approximately 1.6 hours. Scalar times derive from midpoint/geometric-mean conversion of the ranges. | Treat as annotated expected times, not exact stopwatch measurements. Much broader tasks and different acceptance; cannot substitute these durations for Alpha's bounded CSV jobs. |
| How Wrangling Tools Shape Wrangling, July 2026, §III and discussion | 40 freelancers with data-wrangling experience, tool-specific conditions spanning Excel, Jupyter, OpenRefine and ChatGPT | Overall session mean 38 ±27 minutes, including two tasks and follow-up. | Excluded as a no-AI baseline: authors explicitly describe AI use across nominally non-AI tool conditions. Session time also includes surveys. Helpful methodological warning against equating a conventional tool label with no AI. |

## Recommended paper wording

“The comparison is complete as a market-grounded decision model: observed system execution is combined with documented labor-price benchmarks and explicit assumptions about human work and review. Published studies demonstrate that competent people perform related spreadsheet and data-cleaning tasks, but their task conditions and timing measures do not match this portfolio. We therefore present break-even attention and cost thresholds rather than relabeling external durations as matched human observations. The new human trial remains NOT RUN.”

## Economic modeling implication

Use employer cost per accepted delivery as the unit. If an illustrative traditional workflow requires h minutes of worker plus review time at loaded rate w per hour, its modeled labor cost is wh/60. For Alpha, use measured operational resource quantities where priced, plus explicit review minutes, investment allocation and maintenance assumptions. Keeping h as an assumption is more defensible than importing a number from a distinct task. Solve the adoption condition for required time saved or maximum review attention. These thresholds let a real employer compare with their own workflow without pretending the literature observed this exact job.

Never use the 67-minute TASHEEH report, the 111.94-second OSWorld median, the five-minute WREX deadline, or OSWorld 2.0's 1.6-hour annotation as a headline human denominator against Alpha's subsecond local kernel. They refer to different output contracts and omit different categories of cost.
