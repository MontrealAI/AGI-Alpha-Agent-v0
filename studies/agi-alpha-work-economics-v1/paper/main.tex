\begin{abstract}
A person is paid to deliver useful work through a computer. Replacing that transaction requires an acceptable deliverable, a credible total cost and a manageable burden of supervision. This paper evaluates an actual supported \Alpha\ product path against traditional human-performed digital work using a market-data-grounded comparison. The executed job reconciles two tabular exports and delivers exact joined records, unmatched records, field differences, a source-linked action queue and a portable reusable tool. A public website source-inventory case identifies 16 changed records among 135 matched paths. A prospectively frozen confirmation accepts all 36 fresh controlled jobs across four contexts and three complexity levels; median process turnaround, including artifact generation, independent machine checking and packaging, is \MedianWall\,s, with range \MinWall--\MaxWall\,s. The retained executable is invoked on all 36 fresh jobs. A distinct reporting adapter composes their evidence with the first delivery into a source-linked successor report. Three repeated batches at each of one, two and four local processes accept 108 additional attempts, with median throughput \ThroughputOne, \ThroughputTwo\ and \ThroughputFour\ deliverables/minute, respectively. These finite local results do not establish an unattended enterprise.

Official compensation data and historical human studies ground the commercial model without inventing worker observations. Where a matched no-AI human duration is unavailable, the model derives break-even time, maximum affordable attention and accepted-capacity conditions. Complete monetary cost remains unobserved because human review, setup, maintenance and investment allocation are not metered. At the documented U.S.\ office-support compensation rate, an explicitly modeled 30-minute traditional workflow versus five minutes of residual attention plus USD1 of allocated nonlabor cost yields approximately USD17.98 versus USD4.00 per accepted delivery, conditional on equal full acceptance. That is a scenario, not an observed 4.5-fold gain. The contribution is an inspectable bridge from real useful artifacts and executable reuse to adoption economics, with unfavorable scenarios, historical negative studies and unproved acceleration preserved.
\end{abstract}

\section{Start with the work an employer needs}

Consider a release owner with two exports of a website's asset inventory. Before approving a release, the owner needs to know which paths were added, removed or changed, where each difference came from and what remains to be decided. An employee might use a spreadsheet, a database join, a script or a native version-control diff. The business output is the checked exception package. The employer still decides whether each proposed change belongs in the release.

Our first job uses public source metadata from the actual \Alpha\ repository: the 135 tracked files beneath \code{docs/assets/} at the v1.42 source and at the pinned current main. The delivered package finds 135 exact matched paths, no unmatched paths and 16 changed Git-blob identities. It provides each old and new value, its source row, a grouped review action, exact JSON and spreadsheet-safe CSV. The machine acceptance checker independently reconstructs the expected answer from the two input files. This useful artifact exists independently of any claim about future learning.

The same bounded workflow is relevant to product catalogs, stock exports and publication metadata. O*NET describes record verification, discrepancy reporting and reconciliation as paid duties of data-entry and bookkeeping workers; business-intelligence work includes report preparation and reusable reporting tools \citep{onet_dataentry2026,onet_bookkeeping2026,onet_bi2026}. These occupational descriptions establish task relevance. They do not establish how long this particular job takes or that every responsibility of those roles is automatable.

We define \emph{computer-mediated work} as work performed through digital interfaces, using a keyboard, mouse or equivalent input, with results delivered or actions completed through software. A salaried role can contain computer work, physical work, interpersonal obligations and accountable judgment. This study measures one explicit portion: exact reconciliation and descriptive reporting. It does not substitute a successful spreadsheet operation for an entire occupation.

\section{A finished job, then fresh useful missions}

\subsection{The delivered artifact contract}

The original catalog CLI writes joined records, unmatched records for each side, conflict records, exact result JSON, a local comparison record, an HTML report, project history and a standalone successor tool \citep{alpha_catalog_cli}. The original browser guide adds the complete field-level difference list and groups related occurrences without dropping evidence. Our thin study wrapper adds a plain-language summary, a manifest, a delivery archive and an independent acceptance record. Later jobs invoke the exact emitted tool and receive the same employer-facing summary and exception artifacts.

Keys are exact strings: case, whitespace, leading zeroes and Unicode are not silently normalized. Duplicate keys retain every matching pair. Common non-key columns are compared; unshared columns are explicitly listed. A conflict means unequal source text, not proof that either source is wrong. Formula-like CSV cells receive a protective leading apostrophe, while exact JSON retains originals. The supported limits are 200,000 bytes and 1,500 rows per input, 32 columns, and 10,000 matching pairs. Exceeding a limit produces refusal rather than truncation.

\subsection{Prospective boundary and development record}

Before confirmation generation, 69 files covering the protocol, producer glue, independent oracle, source inventories, upstream code and analysis were hashed into a frozen boundary. The checkpoint was saved and the boundary recorded in the existing coordination issue before any confirmation result \citep{study_freeze}. The SHA-256 begins \code{41b8b56a39993e29}; the companion contains the complete value and all member hashes.

Development retained a genuine generator failure on a tiny test fixture. Independent pre-freeze review also identified two accounting defects: an outer timeout could lose a failed attempt, and missing CPU measurements could be treated as zero. Both were corrected and checked before freezing. The shared employer deadline was clarified as one eight-hour business day; the system's separate 30-second execution cap is a resource control, not a 30-second limit imposed on the human comparator.

\subsection{Portfolio, acceptance and sequence}

Confirmation consists of 36 fresh jobs: four contexts, three complexity levels and three instances. Base sizes are 80, 360 and 1,200 rows per input; the high level adds 24 duplicates per side. Changed keys, column order, unmatched records, conflicting text, quoted commas, multiline values, Unicode and formula-like strings create meaningful variation. The randomized order mixes difficulty. These are controlled source-generated tasks, not 36 paying customer engagements or a representative sample of worldwide work.

Acceptance is conjunctive. Every pair, unmatched row, conflicting field and source value must be correct; coverage and traceability must be complete; downloadable artifacts and summary links must be valid. Material error tolerance is zero. An independently authored Python CSV/SQLite checker reconstructs the expected semantic records without importing producer modules. Independent role separation reduces implementation coupling, but is not a blinded human assessor or an outside organization. Technical usability is checked; human usability and willingness to pay are not measured.

The public showcase is explicitly development-selected, separate from the fresh 36-job confirmation. It produces the retained executable. All 36 fresh jobs invoke those exact bytes. A final successor task maps the 37 accepted outputs into a grouped workload report through the released public-data workbench. That task combines reconciliation evidence with a second known capability; its totals, counts and extrema are checked independently in Python.

\section{The economics of an accepted deliverable}

\subsection{Complete cost, including attention and failure}

Let $t_H$ be traditional human minutes per attempted job, including attributable preparation, correction, review and supervision. Let $h_A$ be the corresponding residual human minutes when using \Alpha. Hourly employer valuations are $w_H$ and $w_A$. Let $q_H,q_A$ be accepted outputs divided by attempted jobs at the same accounting boundary as time and resource use, and $u$ measured system CPU-seconds per attempt. Retries must be included consistently in those totals. Then:
\begin{align}
C_H &= \frac{w_Ht_H/60+v_H}{q_H},\\
C_A &= \frac{w_Ah_A/60+p_{\rm CPU}u/3600+k+A/N}{q_A}.
\label{eq:cost}
\end{align}
$v_H$ is attributable nonlabor human-workflow cost; $k$ covers per-attempt maintenance and operating overhead; $A/N$ allocates construction, installation and verification investment across the declared number of attempts. Model usage is included in $k$ when present. Our task-time model calls are zero, but authoring cost is not zero merely because the session does not supply a bill. The sum of all incurred costs, including failed and abandoned attempts, divided by accepted outputs is the empirical version of Equation~\ref{eq:cost}.

The study reports marginal \emph{resource quantities}, not an observed marginal price. Fully allocated monetary cost is also unknown. The companion's ledgers record the missing categories explicitly. A numerical all-in cost in the following tables is therefore conditional. An employer can replace each input with its own documented work, review and infrastructure costs.

\subsection{Break-even time and the attention frontier}

Solving $C_H=C_A$ gives:
\begin{equation}
t_H^\star=\frac{60}{w_H}\left[
\frac{q_H}{q_A}\left(\frac{w_Ah_A}{60}+\frac{p_{\rm CPU}u}{3600}+k+\frac{A}{N}\right)-v_H
\right].
\end{equation}
For equal acceptance, equal labor rates and no separate human-workflow nonlabor charge, this simplifies to residual attention plus the labor-time equivalent of the system's other cost. The threshold is often more useful than an asserted 100-fold speedup: an employer can ask whether its actual current workflow exceeds it.

For a specified all-in cost per accepted delivery of five local-currency units, with $q_H=1$ and $v_H=0$, the traditional-work thresholds are 12.50 minutes at the Quebec data-entry wage, 10.71 at the Quebec bookkeeping wage, 8.34 at U.S.\ office/admin compensation, 6.26 for the Netherlands employer-cost scenario and 25.00 for Bulgaria. Wage-only rows omit employer loading unless explicitly selected. These are independent CAD5/USD5/EUR5 scenarios, not an FX comparison or an observed five-dollar \Alpha\ price.

A 30-to-5-minute attention substitution with USD1 of other allocated cost gives approximately a 4.5-fold cost ratio and sixfold attention-limited capacity. A 60-to-1-minute case can produce a much larger conditional advantage. An efficient five-minute incumbent can instead beat an attention-heavy system. The commercial region is consequently defined by the task's real human burden, the system's exception burden and the repeated-use allocation, not by branding or a compulsory positive result.

For a fixed attention budget $H$ minutes, budget $B$, operating time $T$ and measured accepted machine throughput $\lambda$, usable capacity is bounded by
\begin{equation}
Q_A\leq \min\!\left(\frac{B}{C_A},\frac{Hq_A}{h_A},\lambda T,D\right),
\end{equation}
where $D$ is available qualifying demand. Accepted throughput $\lambda$ must correspond to the same operating regime as $q_A$; if $\rho$ is attempt throughput, $\lambda=\rho q_A$. An acceptance sensitivity is not a new measured throughput. The repeated local throughput measurement is not evidence that employer attention, data access or demand scale at the same rate. Saved worker minutes create capacity. Cash savings require an actual change in overtime, contractor expenditure, hiring or another expense; an unchanged salary bill is not reduced by arithmetic.

Mean CPU per attempted delivery rises from 0.523 to 0.621 seconds, about 18.7\%. Initial queued backlog is 11, 10 and 8 jobs. No unplanned rescue intervention occurs within these scripted batches; this observation does not measure an employer's preparation or review time. Physical CPU allocation, infrastructure price, peak host memory and sustained multi-day behavior are not measured.

All eight separate controls pass: pre-aborted STOP; checkpoint interruption followed by historical restore and explicit new execution; input and time boundaries; source mismatch; corrupted saved claims; preserved local artifact restoration; wrong-key refusal with explicit fallback; and exact-string ambiguity preservation. The time-boundary control injects clock readings, rather than spending sixty seconds of real work. Recovery restarts from committed inputs; it does not continue from an internal program counter. Rollback restores local saved bytes; it is not production database or contract rollback.

The browser performs local computation and stops when its execution context closes. A separately started native command can continue only while its process remains alive. This finite study does not install a background service, provision an approved remote executor or establish a continuously operating enterprise. Monetary-spend enforcement, remote-worker rescue and transactional production rollback are not tested.

\section{The \$40 trillion thesis and the commercial opportunity}

The relevant global quantity is not GDP, total salaries, the number of computer users or a provider's revenue. A defensible task-expenditure estimate would combine countries and occupations:
\begin{equation}
L_{\rm digital}=\sum_{c,o}
\bigl(\text{hours worked}_{c,o}\bigr)
\bigl(\text{loaded cost/hour worked}_{c,o}\bigr)
\bigl(\text{digital-task time share}_{c,o}\bigr).
\end{equation}
Task allocations must avoid overlap, use compatible years and currencies, match worked-hour or paid-hour denominators consistently, and distinguish employee compensation from imputed self-employment income. ILOSTAT definitions separate earnings from employer labor costs \citep{ilostat_wagedefinitions,ilo2026labourmetadata}.

World Bank displays 2025 world GDP of USD118.35 trillion, while ILO reports a 52.6\% global labor-income share \citep{worldbank2025gdp,ilo2026sdgreview}. Their mechanical product, USD62.25 trillion, is an arithmetic scale check, not an official harmonized employer-cost estimate. Country coverage, aggregation and price bases were not reconciled here. ILO's recent labor-share estimates include modeling \citep{ilo2026modelmethods}. A USD40 trillion digital-work figure would be roughly 64\% of that mechanical envelope, a substantial allocation requiring its own evidence.

Digital-device adoption does not resolve that allocation. Eurostat reports nearly 30\% of EU workers using devices all or most of their work time in 2022; JRC's 2024--2025 survey uses a broader any-use concept exceeding 90\% \citep{eurostat2022digital,jrc2025digital}. Neither is a worldwide wage-weighted share of tasks a system can deliver. The proposed USD40 trillion annual opportunity is therefore an \textbf{illustrative scenario}, worldwide, motivated by 2025-scale economic quantities.

That is a concrete economic route toward accumulating productive capacity; its scale must be earned in actual deployments.

\section{Limits, preserved evidence and the next commercial test}

This study executes a narrow useful path, not a universal autonomous employee. Its inputs are bounded public or synthetic tables. Its correctness contract is exact and executable. Real organizations also face missing authority, semantic ambiguity, inconsistent business rules, private data, access permissions, evolving systems and adverse incentives. No result here establishes performance on those untested conditions.

The independent checker is separately authored but within the same research effort. No blinded human assessment, user-study satisfaction or paying customer's acceptance is claimed. The chosen portfolio can establish finite correctness, not a calibrated population reliability. The local process host is shared and short-lived; throughput measurements are not a capacity contract. Current models are not benchmarked, and a future provider upgrade requires fresh evaluation.

Previous research remains unchanged. Strategy Succession's 517/864 qualified outcomes tied its equally funded learner and fell below its stronger fixed portfolio's 591; its primary retained capability was not admitted. The subsequent Software Capability Foundry recorded a whole-instance CPU ratio of 1.0574 against its direct specialist, failing its predeclared superiority gate \citep{strategy_succession,software_foundry}. These software-only findings are not reinterpreted as human-work results. The present market-data comparison answers a different question and inherits none of their unproved claims.

The next commercial observation should fill the largest practical unknowns: actual task intake and review minutes, exception-resolution effort, installation and maintenance costs, accepted output under a buyer's own contract, and willingness to pay. Existing ordinary software remains part of the competent human alternative. An employer with a fast established script may find little incremental value; one repeatedly constructing and checking the same deliverable may find substantial value. The economic model makes that difference explicit without requiring new participant recruitment to complete this edition.

\section{Source method and scope notes}

All market rates retain their original nominal currency. No currency conversion or purchasing-power substitution is used. Quebec low/high wage figures are distributional wage estimates, not confidence bounds. Statistics Canada's wage guide and Job Bank's method distinguish survey and regional estimation approaches; the selected Quebec provincial rows are labeled Labour Force Survey, 2023--2024. Eurostat country figures concern broad covered sectors and enterprise-size coverage; they are never presented as exact clerk rates.

Published human-work evidence retains its original denominator. SpreadsheetBench's expert subset is 50 instructions, not its full 912-instruction benchmark. WREX's five-minute failures remain censored failures. The TASHEEH batch and OSWorld's mixed-task median remain external observations. AI assistance is explicitly unestablished where the publication does not say. These sources ground task relevance and economic sensitivity rather than fabricate a matched trial.

Global market scenarios use a hypothetical common USD60T labor envelope. Technical eligibility, adoption, retained-cost fraction, provider price and market share are assumptions. The task experiment does not estimate any of those fractions. A provider's attainable revenue must be measured from actual offered services, price, accepted volume and realized market access.
