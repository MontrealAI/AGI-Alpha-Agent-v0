# Research finding

**A reusable instrument can make a conditional shared service explicit and testable; its commercial value still has to survive fresh work.** This study constructs and executes a finite observation-and-contingency compiler. Its originating example asks whether three satellite operators could share the cost of learning more about a common uncertain object while preserving each operator's risk criterion, action deadline and fallback funding. The software produces inspectable branch plans, transfers through the existing AGI ALPHA lifecycle, and runs in a separate recipient process.

The originating example supports a narrow positive result. Under frozen, explicitly hypothetical prices and behavior, individual observation purchases attract no orders, while the joint offer has expected service collections of **$157.30**, complete cash operating costs of **$134.47**, and operating contribution of **$22.83** before optional retention or extra research. The conventional expert, given the same information and coordination rights, ties this result. A fully successful branch earns $105; it is distinct from the expectation. All monetary amounts are USD.

Fresh evaluation sharply bounds that positive illustration. An independently written exact reference agrees with the frozen compiler on **48 of 48** synthetic missions: 36 valid plans and 12 correct refusals. Of the valid plans, 20 have positive aggregate information value, four have zero value and twelve have negative value before acquisition fees. Applying the unchanged commercial policy produces **no acquisition order in any of the 48 cases**. Their modeled preliminary assessment costs total $346. Origin plus confirmation invitations therefore contribute **−$323.17**, before optional B/C investment and any unmeasured research opportunity cost.

The retained executable transfers correctly. Its fresh recipient has positive aggregate information value, but one operator is worse off before fees and the proposed order is economically unjustified. The useful successor decision is to stop that offer. An ordinary directory with identical contents and permissions produces byte-identical results. This establishes executable reuse and fresh semantic checking; additional retained financial advantage remains unmeasured.

The resulting contribution is a complete conditional study: an opportunity hypothesis, a purpose-preserving transformation witness, a usable instrument, independent confirmation, disjoint accounting, actual byte transfer and a justified commercial stop. The constructive lesson is precise: **information quality, buyer participation and available cash must be checked together, branch by branch, before a capability is treated as earned Alpha.**

# Opportunity discovery before the benchmark

The initial question was not how to accelerate an existing numerical solver. It was whether several parties could purchase an uncertainty-reducing observation that none would find worthwhile alone, without transferring the observation's adverse consequences to someone else. The beneficiary would be an operator buying a small, inspectable decision-support package: what can be learned, which actions remain available after each possible result, what must be reserved, and when the proposed acquisition should be refused.

This is a consequential setting with extensive prior art. NASA documents free USSPACECOM screening and validated commercial alternatives, recommends retaining suitable data/actionability checks, and describes a widely used per-event mitigation threshold of $10^{-4}$. That threshold informs the illustrative scale here; our finite inputs do not implement operational NASA conjunction assessment. [@nasa2026] ESA's collision-avoidance challenge concerns evolving risk estimates in CDMs; the final reported risk is not a realized collision label. We did not download or reanalyze that dataset. [@uriot2020]

# Minimal executable experiment

## A finite world with explicit limits

A mission supplies a finite common state $s\in S$, prior $p(s)$, an observation likelihood $L(y\mid s)$, and a delivery time for each possible outcome. Each operator $i$ supplies actions $a\in A_i$, a risk vector $r_i(a,s)$, deterministic action expense $c_i(a)$, an action start limit and an original risk ceiling $\tau_i$. A fixed fallback action applies if observation times out. The implementation permits at most twelve states, twelve outcomes, six operators and six actions per operator.

The inputs are synthetic probability tables. They are not learned sensor calibration, orbital covariances or validated maneuver predictions. Actions have no physical cross-effects in this bounded world. Adding maneuver interactions or a different measurement regime would require a new model and fresh qualification. This narrow scope keeps the causal question inspectable rather than hiding it inside an oversized simulator.

For a timely result $y$, the compiler calculates

$$
p(s\mid y)=\frac{p(s)L(y\mid s)}{\sum_{s'}p(s')L(y\mid s')}.
$$

It chooses the least expensive action that is still available and satisfies

$$
\sum_s p(s\mid y)r_i(a,s)\leq\tau_i.
$$

Each operator may have a different observation deadline. Its timeout is the union of no-result outcomes and results arriving after that deadline. Their likelihoods are summed **before** applying Bayes. The declared fallback must satisfy the conditional risk bound and remain available at timeout. Unreachable events remain explicitly unreachable; they are not counted as deliveries.

Observation permission is refused for unusable inputs, uncleared fixture rights or mismatched calibration scope. The output carries the input hash and `execution_authorized=false`; the compiler identity is bound by the frozen source, bundle and transfer receipts. An admissible plan and a valuable purchase are distinct decisions.

## The first decisive counterexample

A reviewer proposed the design challenge that missingness can be informative. We executed a two-state example with dangerous-state prior 0.02, risk 0.002 in that state, and timeout likelihoods 0.29 in the dangerous state versus 0.04 in the safe state. A prior-based timeout check gives $0.02\times0.002=0.00004$, below the illustrative limit. Conditioning on timeout gives

$$
P(\text{danger}\mid\text{timeout})=
\frac{0.02\times0.29}{0.02\times0.29+0.98\times0.04}=0.128888\ldots,
$$

and conditional risk **0.00025778**, above the same limit. A previously acceptable coast action would therefore be wrongly accepted by a prior-only check. The corrected compiler aggregates operator-specific timeout evidence and preserves a safe fallback. This was a researcher/reviewer design revision, not an autonomous discovery claim or a measured U-versus-E result. Its original executable counterexample is preserved.

## Why information can have negative cost value

The familiar argument that additional information cannot hurt assumes that the original decision remains available after observing. Physical action availability alone is insufficient under a branchwise posterior risk requirement. Adverse evidence can make a prior-safe action fail that same threshold; a forced timeout fallback can also be more expensive.

Let $a_i^0$ be the prior-optimal action and $A_i(y)$ the admissible action set after result $y$, including timing, posterior risk and the fixed timeout rule. If $a_i^0\in A_i(y)$ for every reachable branch, then

$$
\min_{a\in A_i(y)} c_i(a)\leq c_i(a_i^0)
\quad\Rightarrow\quad
\mathbb E_y[c_i(a_i^*(y))]\leq c_i(a_i^0).
$$

The condition is sufficient, not necessary. Its failure permits a negative value but does not force one. A post-confirmation diagnostic found that 90 of 130 accepted operator plans satisfy this inclusion condition, with zero violations of the bound. Four negative-total cases arise from the forced timeout rule alone; eight also remove baseline coast through posterior safety. Those diagnostic classifications explain the result; they are not new independent confirmation.

# Commercial construction and complete accounting

## A specified conditional order

The package fee is an illustrative **$80 per accepted operator package**, not a fixed discount from a human price. Shared acquisition costs $600 in the primary scenario, with a predeclared $100–$2,000 range. Customer adaptation costs $20. An operator participates only if its own modeled surplus is nonnegative and, when specified, its cash can cover the full fee, acquisition share, adaptation and its fallback reserve.

For a proposed set $S$ with equal acquisition shares, the declared buyer rule is

$$
u_i(S)=(1-c)\left[q(V_i-p)-\frac{K}{|S|}\right]-a_i\geq0,
$$

where $V_i$ is gross information value, $q=0.90$ is conditional semantic acceptance, $c=0.05$ is common package failure, $p=80$, $K=600$ and $a_i=20$. These probabilities are assumptions, not measured model performance. The roster is recomputed after withdrawals and after participants decline. The common package failure is distinct from the sensor's no-result branch, already included in $V_i$.

Before commitment, each invited operator withdraws with assumed probability 0.10. On acceptance, a remaining fee balance defaults with assumed probability 0.03; a 30% service deposit has already been paid. Common package failure refunds both service deposits and acquisition pass-through, leaving a sunk acquisition loss with the provider. Individual nonacceptance or postcommit withdrawal refunds the service deposit but does not refund the already authorized acquisition share. This is a modeled contract design, whose enforceability and actual attractiveness have not been tested.

An exact finite event tree enumerates these events. It uses 44 positive-probability paths for the originating joint offer. There is no Monte Carlo sampling error in its arithmetic. Uncertainty about likelihoods, service effort and buyer behavior remains undiminished by exact enumeration.

$$
\pi_k=Q_kPh-a_k,\qquad

$$

# What the simulation usefully establishes

The conditional source example works only when information benefit, voluntary participation, supplier price, package effort and funding jointly fit. At the frozen $80 fee, the strongest source coalition's acquisition ceiling is **$706.34**. At that exact boundary, the event tree still has positive expected contribution of $19.08. The ceiling is an inverse-price diagnostic derived after confirmation, not an observed supplier quote or a new primary result.

![The source offer has a bounded commercial region. Larger fees can eliminate participation; inexpensive acquisition does not make a zero-fee service profitable. All cells are exact arithmetic under declared assumptions.](figures/feasibility.pdf)

For equal shares, a voluntary subset $S$ has acquisition ceiling

$$
K_{\max}(S)=|S|\left[q\left(\min_{i\in S}V_i-p\right)-\frac{a}{1-c}\right].
$$

This gives the smallest useful next question: can an appropriate provider deliver timely, actionable information below the relevant ceiling, and do actual operators agree with the modeled values and obligations? More simulated draws cannot answer that question. The current fresh cohort's zero orders and negative total invitation contribution make broad commercialization or compulsory training unsupported.

There are two kinds of uncertainty. Within a specified world, the exact event tree determines expected collections and losses. Across worlds, the probabilities, effort, buyer values, sensor behavior and contractual terms are uncertain assumptions. The latter dominate the result. No narrow numerical confidence band can convert them into empirical demand or physical assurance.

# Practical operating blueprint

The general operating path extends the existing system without a competing registry:

1. **Discover and qualify the need.** Record beneficiary, original useful outcome, outside options, overlap with active work and the cheapest falsifier. Reject generic novelty claims when a conventional method already supplies the claimed capability.
2. **Freeze the commercial and technical contract.** Record quality, deadline, customer effort, permissions, price and failure handling. Separate supplied assumptions from observed evidence and preserve a source identity for each.
3. **Construct the smallest instrument.** Charge learning necessary for the paid outcome to A. Keep every adverse-result and timeout branch. Use an independent conventional implementation with equal information and rights.
4. **Gate each proposed offer.** Check mission-specific semantics, each participant's utility, original fallback resources, funding prefixes and all obligations. Refuse zero-value information and unaffordable or incompatible offers.
5. **Accept and account.** Bind artifact, acceptance, source, permissions and cost records. Separate pass-through funds from earned fees; retain refunds, defaults and support. Treat actual missing billing as unknown.
6. **Retain selectively.** Assess incremental value against the recipient's competent baseline before paying further admission cost. Rights-clear and version the actual executable asset. Withhold protected evaluation material and secrets.
7. **Transfer and revalidate.** Reuse stable identity and scope qualifications only when unchanged. Recheck the new mission's inputs, outputs and meaning. Grant authority through its existing separate process. Preserve Jobs validator and settlement boundaries.
8. **Choose the next opportunity or stop.** A correct refusal is useful operating information. It is not a sale, new productive-frontier crossing or earned reinvestment capital.

The public research artifact is inspectable, while the fixture lifecycle exercises sharing among authorized `*.agent.agi.eth` identities. Those fixture checks do not establish live ENS authentication or production scope. The source-qualified journal and cost ledger remain authoritative for their existing boundaries. No token minting, wallet action or `$AGIALPHA` utility is counted as operating profit.
