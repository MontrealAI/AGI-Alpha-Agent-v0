\section{Question, contribution and claim boundaries}

The network studied here sells a useful computational asset: a policy module for a formally specified, non-safety-critical scheduling, batching or planning problem, accompanied by an original-constraint checker, a held-out performance report and a bounded adapter. Its economic opportunity can change when it learns a reusable representation, a compatible validation mechanism, or a more effective invention procedure. The relevant outcome is a buyer's supported result at complete lifecycle cost. More completed calls or a growing archive is not itself productive growth.

The study separates five questions. Can competent Beta delivery earn a surplus at a price buyers prefer to their alternatives? Does useful paid work leave a capability or complementary bundle whose future advantage exceeds the additional cost of retaining it? Does making admitted shareable Alpha available throughout the authenticated network benefit other agents on fresh work? How much advantage survives when Beta improves? Finally, does a retained experimental protocol improve later independent invention campaigns, rather than merely increasing descendant-job volume? The results report these quantities separately. There is no required investment count, compulsory reinvestment percentage, or single combined victory criterion.

The accounting distinction is central. A customer's job pays for its own formulation, learning, search, tests and delivery (ledger A). Retention pays only the additional extraction, generalization, fresh Alpha assessment and lifecycle work still required (ledger B). Separate research beyond the paid scope is optional (ledger C). Every expense remains in consolidated economics once. A retained skill is not required to repay the same paid-job development expense a second time.

Our contributions are a task-specific commercial model, a finite cash and capacity simulation, a tested capability lifecycle and access layer, paired Beta/local-Alpha/network-Alpha experiments under a moving baseline, and a policy-search and fresh-confirmation protocol with a bilingual explanation. The catalogue of possible transformations is supplied to the simulator. The execution does not discover novel algorithms in the real world; it tests the commercial consequences of possible discoveries under exposed assumptions. This distinction permits a serious modeled viability claim while preserving the need for later empirical validation.

\section{Transformational intelligence as the object of enterprise growth}

\subsection{Purpose, formulation and instrument}

The motivating manuscript separates the authorized purpose from the means and from the instrument used to judge success. A formulation includes representations, assumptions, information procedures, executable policies and intermediate objectives. A useful transformation changes that setup while preserving the applicable purpose and resource boundary. The manuscript also separates successful reuse from improved discovery across fresh campaigns. Its exact illustrative information result is not evidence of commercial demand or frontier-model invention.\cite{TI}

For the foundry, the intent is a policy or solution that obeys the buyer's supplied constraints and improves the declared outcome within the agreed resource budget. The formulation may change the encoding, decomposition, experiment selection or intermediate search objective. The evaluation instrument independently recomputes the original constraints and objective. Replacing a hard constraint with a softer score is not a purpose-preserving transformation.

Write the intent charter as $I=(D,U,C,A,B)$: task distribution, useful outcome, material constraints, authorized actions and budget. Let $F$ be a formulation and $L_F$ its realization in the original environment. A transformation $T$ contains a new formulation, its construction procedure, its realization and a falsifiable witness. Its accepted output $z$ must satisfy a bridge of the form
\begin{equation}
  G_{F'}(\phi_T(x),z)\ \Longrightarrow\ G_I(x,L_T(x,z)),
\end{equation}
or a separately justified empirical counterpart. Feasibility under this bridge does not establish optimality, economic benefit or omitted physical requirements. Those are separate claims with separate tests.

\subsection{A mission Gym, fresh proof and a retained institution}

The related \emph{Gym, Specialist ASI \& Successor $\Omega$} manuscript treats a mission environment as more than a prompt: it specifies state, observations, bounded actions, constraints, scenario families, verification and receipts. Formation and teaching are distinct from protected fresh proof. A complete successor also includes tools, evidence, operations, memory and authority, and it must survive changes to models or providers. These are institutional design requirements, not measured performance rates.\cite{Gym}

We use that separation to keep paid experiments, reusable content, protected evaluation cases, failure memory and admission decisions distinct. Identify the restriction; out-learn through informative work; out-think and out-design a candidate formulation; out-strategise through pricing and optional retention; out-execute the supported outcome; out-evolve only when fresh evidence establishes a retained advantage. This sequence supplies no numerical expertise bonus. A failed approach can become useful failure memory without becoming positive admitted Alpha.

The manuscript's Specialist ASI designation requires fresh comparative evidence for a complete mission institution. Its operational Successor $\Omega$ admission requires separate accountable authority. Our fully automated, bounded research simulation claims neither realized designation. Its synthetic Aurelia example and illustrative enterprise price bands are excluded from calibration. The source's empirical completion gate describes a later real-world study; it does not convert the current modeled results into observed commercial success.\cite{Gym}

\subsection{Why complementary capabilities can create an opportunity}

A representation can be expensive and useless without a compatible producer and checker. Conversely, a checker can be unable to validate the artifacts that a better representation would make possible. The enterprise is therefore allowed to consider a bundle rather than requiring every component to repay independently. For component sets $S_1$ and $S_2$, the interaction in a common net outcome $V$ is
\begin{equation}
  \Delta_{12}=V(S_1+S_2)-V(S_1)-V(S_2)+V(\varnothing).
\end{equation}
A positive interaction is a hypothesis to be tested with paired ablations, not a free benefit granted by calling an asset a bundle. Failed construction, unsuccessful transfer and misleading validation can make the interaction negative.

The economically relevant structural changes include avoiding repeated reconstruction, making work serviceable within its resource budget, reducing the cost of informative experiments, and allowing independent agents to use a purpose-preserving interface. An Alpha claim exists only when the changed setup supplies additional net mission advantage over versioned competent Beta and the relevant already-available capabilities, at the same complete budget. The absence of a saved file does not imply that a frontier agent lacks the underlying method.

\subsection{Strong baselines and restraint}

All arms may use current appropriate models, established optimization methods, adequate context, decomposition, correction and the same authorized information. OR-Tools already provides executable constraint programming for scheduling. The baseline may be optimal for a small instance, commercially adequate for a large one, or cheaper than any new method after research cost.\cite{ORTools} A negative-control environment with no useful transformation is therefore necessary. Retaining cash or abandoning a search is sometimes the correct policy.

This study compares commercial service prices with a competent human multidisciplinary team. Competitive pricing against other AI providers is outside that commercial comparison. Within the technical work, comparison with strong existing algorithms remains essential: human-market value does not excuse a deliberately weak solver.

\section{The offering, its buyer and the human-team comparison}

\subsection{A fresh screen against the network criterion}

The primary venture is selected because its Alpha claims can be made falsifiable at both creator and recipient levels. Formal scheduling and batching tasks have explicit objectives, executable constraint checks, public strong baselines and related but disjoint instances for transfer. Verified software is a credible contrast, but usefulness depends strongly on specification overlap. Simulation-native engineering adds an intended-use bridge to physical reality. Coordination mechanisms require more information about preferences and strategic behavior. This is a bounded qualitative choice, not a measured ranking of entire industries.

\subsection{A complete bounded outcome}

The primary buyer is a technical owner with a complete structured specification, a supplied formal simulator or a public instance family. The module package includes scope and schema checks, a competent baseline, bounded method search, an executable policy, an independent original-constraint checker, held-out tests, a pinned environment and a narrow I/O adapter. One bounded correction cycle is included in the reference scope. Routine use of the resulting optimizer is distinct from commissioning another invention campaign.

The offering excludes manual discovery of unstated requirements, production-wide integration, physical certification, open-ended revisions and ongoing enterprise support. Automated intake can request missing structured information; unresolved semantics cause abstention or deferral. The primary workflow contains no routine human provider review and no manual provider onboarding. This makes the claim more ambitious and the supported scope narrower: the required evidence must be executable in the declared environment.

\subsection{Buyer value is independent of production and replacement cost}

A high human-team estimate does not make an output valuable. The buyer may already have a satisfactory optimizer, may lack an integration budget, or may value the improvement below either service price. The simulator therefore treats customer value as an independent uncertain quantity. A buyer can choose the foundry, commission the matched human team, retain the existing method, postpone or decline.

For example, an official cloud pricing page gives a list-price unit of approximately \usd{0.0404784} per Linux/x86 vCPU-hour in US East (Northern Virginia). An assumed ten million \emph{reducible billed} CPU-hours per year and an assumed five-percent reduction would imply only \usd{20,239.20} gross CPU savings before migration, running cost and realization limits. These workload and gain assumptions are not observed. A shorter schedule does not save this money if it leaves billed resource duration unchanged.\cite{AWS} The primary commercial value scenarios are consequently not obtained by multiplying makespan gains by an invented cloud bill.

\subsection{Contrasting ventures}

A simulation-native engineering lab could sell a candidate design, parameterization and reproducible uncertainty package. Its opportunity is credible within a formal simulator, but a physically useful design needs evidence that the model is valid for its intended use. Simulation consistency alone does not establish that bridge.\cite{NASA} A verified-software foundry can sell a narrow module with executable contracts or a proof, but verification establishes a relationship to a specification; it does not recover missing customer intent.\cite{Dafny} A coordination-mechanism lab is also promising, but unobserved preferences and strategic responses make its present market calibration weaker. These cases bound the portfolio rather than expanding the simulator into several incomparable industries.

\section{Autonomous production, independent evidence and finite capacity}

\subsection{What external capability evidence does and does not establish}

Program-search research provides serious precedents for reusable algorithm invention under executable evaluation. FunSearch studies program evolution for mathematical problems and bin-packing heuristics. AlphaEvolve reports whole-program evolution, evaluation cascades and scheduling applications. An independent Evolution of Heuristics study uses ideas and executable programs to search packing, routing and flow-shop methods. These studies support the workflow and possibility of useful transformations; they do not calibrate the acceptance rate of Astra on this commercial scope.\cite{FunSearch,AlphaEvolve,EoH}

The distinction matters because research applications differ in supplied formulations, evaluator maturity, human participation, search budgets and selection of reported runs. AlphaEvolve's compiler-IR application included final human correctness confirmation; it cannot by itself support our no-human workflow assumption. Expensive evaluation can overwhelm cheap generation. Evaluator exploitation and mixed benefits from added verification in narrower studies also motivate negative controls. Agreement among model roles is not an independent correctness proof.\cite{AlphaEvolve,Harness}

\subsection{Current frontier configuration and billing}

The demanding roles use the strongest appropriate documented Astra reasoning configuration for production, critique, strategy, construction and investment judgment. Terra is allocated to visual or computer-use work where the supported workflow calls for it. Access, reasoning settings, usage classes and evidence dates are recorded in the source-linked register. The configuration is not assigned an arbitrary expertise multiplier. Its relevant uncertainty is exposed through task components and stresses.\cite{OpenAIModels,OpenAIPrices}

Every role's input, cached usage and total billed output are charged. Output includes the reasoning work specified by the applicable pricing model; it is not charged again as a separate fictitious resource. Long-context rules apply to the individual requests to which they actually pertain. Tool execution, sandbox sessions, solver compute, storage and recovery add to model usage. A ChatGPT subscription is a separate allocation and conveys neither included API use nor an assumed unlimited production quota.

\subsection{Error, rejection and recovery}

The modeled chain includes structured intake, semantic/formulation checks, production or search, independent executable evaluation, critique, correction, packaging and post-delivery recovery. A useful result must pass the stated acceptance contract; fluency or an optimistic self-assessment is insufficient. The model distinguishes a useful valid asset, an executable but unimproved asset, an invalid result, a false pass, a false rejection and an unresolved case.

The executed program simulates this workflow's events and resource use. It does not run newly invented scheduling algorithms or call the frontier models. Putative validation outcomes are generated by the declared error and detection process. ``Independent'' identifies the proposed evaluation role and its modeled failure structure; it does not mean that an actual customer module was independently checked in this study. The same distinction applies to the fresh-campaign experiment.

Shared interpretation errors are an explicit risk. If the producer and checker both implement an incorrectly understood requirement, repeated agreement does not eliminate the error. Latent common conditions can affect many candidates or jobs. A separate checker must read original inputs and recompute the original outcome, while mutation tests and small exact cases can test its scope. Even then, unrepresented customer requirements remain outside what it proves.

Retries consume cash, tokens, slots and time, including when they end in abstention. Residual defects can produce refunds, default, support work and revalidation. The simulation does not silently insert a human professional when automated evidence fails. It stops, defers or rejects according to the frozen scope and recovery policy.

\subsection{Capacity is shared by production and improvement}

Finite concurrent slots and token/tool quotas constrain the entire enterprise. Validators, retries, construction campaigns, integration, maintenance and recovery compete with ordinary delivery. A build can therefore delay a sale even if the model API itself is cheap. Queued work has a deadline and a reserved cost; refusal or expiry is recorded. Elapsed delivery, occupied execution time and collection time are distinct quantities. The conservative booking mechanism uses the blind maximum reservation duration and retains unused retry capacity until the promised completion. Quota-utilization summaries measure booked reservations; actual billed job resources are separately retained. Quoted delivery includes waiting in the queue. None of these modeled latencies is a measured API service guarantee.

Automation is a property of the modeled workflow, not an observed operational achievement. Report autonomous delivered outcomes, abstention and recovery together with capacity refusals and queue tails. A high automatic-pass fraction is not a quality metric when false passes remain. Results on many simulated jobs establish simulator behavior, not empirically demonstrated real-world throughput.

\section{From a demanding job to validated network Alpha}

\subsection{Beta is a competent, versioned counterfactual}

Beta includes the strongest appropriate documented frontier configuration, adequate context, ordinary tools, current established solvers, reasonable decomposition, source checks, critique, correction and the recipient's already-available capabilities. The same baseline competence and authorization apply to producers, critics, validators and investment evaluators. A recipient does not lack a method simply because it lacks a particular file. Reconstructing an available method is a legitimate baseline action, with its complete cost and latency charged.

Let $B_{a,t}$ denote this baseline for agent $a$ at time $t$. A demanding supported job may leave a candidate linked to its formal purpose, development record and evidence. Candidate emergence, rights clearance and reusable content are uncertain. It can be an encoding, search adapter, verifier, experiment protocol or a tested composition. Being novel or valid is insufficient. A useful customer result can be delivered entirely by Beta; it earns revenue under the commercial contract without becoming Alpha. Selection uses observable accepted and collected status, not hidden simulated truth; an undiscovered false pass can enter the candidate pool and must face fresh checks.

For a candidate bundle $S$, a monetary assessment can be written
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_{a,t}(S)=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\Pi(B_{a,t}\oplus S)-\Pi(B_{a,t})\right]-K_{a,t}(S),
\end{equation}
where $\Pi$ is future net operating contribution, including recurring operation, risk, upkeep, revalidation and erosion, and $K$ is the remaining incremental B/C expense. Development already charged to the customer job in A is absent from this prospective hurdle and remains in the enterprise's historical expense. A cost is not counted again in $K$ if it is included in $\Pi$. Quality and purpose constraints remain separate requirements; they cannot be traded away by a high revenue forecast. Nonmonetary improvements and newly serviceable work are reported separately rather than hidden in an arbitrary composite success score.

The enterprise charges shared retention or separate construction once in its consolidated ledger. Recipient adoption adds retrieval, adaptation, independent testing and composition cost. Internal fees may redistribute cash among agents but cannot create consolidated earnings. The human-service price difference belongs to the commercial comparison; it is not inserted as an Alpha gain in this paired counterfactual.

\subsection{Evidence, admission and mandatory network availability}

The lifecycle is candidate creation, bounded evidence, net-Alpha assessment, eligible attestation, admission, network discovery, authorized reuse, revalidation and supersession or retirement. Admission is bound to a capability version, baseline version, task scope, dependencies, evidence freshness and policy. The evaluation must charge false passes, false rejections and correlated mistakes. A simulated admission is a result of that model's assessment process, not empirical proof that an actual algorithm outperforms a frontier model.

The primary network makes every admitted shareable capability discoverable and eligible for scoped use by all authenticated members. Availability is mandatory after admission; use is optional. A member can decline because its Beta is already equivalent, its workload differs, adaptation is expensive, or its current budget is better spent elsewhere. The local-only arm is an experimental counterfactual isolating the effect of this network rule, not an alternative primary distribution policy.

Only sanitized reusable knowledge is eligible for sharing. Private capsules and verifiable commitments may protect content while metadata records scope, version, prerequisites and status. Retrieval, execution, export and composition are separate rights. Permission to inspect a capsule does not authorize an external action. Any authorized plaintext disclosure can be copied, including retrieval, execution or composition with export disabled. Revocation blocks future mediated access; it cannot recall existing copies. Cryptography establishes integrity or possession under its assumptions; it does not establish truth, correctness, economic value or Alpha.

The additional admission role belongs exclusively to eligible authenticated direct-label \texttt{*.agent.agi.eth} identities. Nested names cannot obtain authority by suffix matching. Creator/write, validator, admission, root-publication and execution authority remain distinct. Existing AGI Jobs \texttt{*.club.agi.eth} contracts, governance and settlement remain unchanged. The research tests use explicitly simulated identity and authority; they do not deploy new permissions or production settlement.

Canonical promotion cannot bypass admission. Every retrieval and reuse checks the current authoritative state, so stale history cannot reactivate a revoked or retired asset. Composition checks current compatible scope/environment bindings and pinned dependencies. Semantic purpose-preservation remains a separate evidence obligation; matching digests does not prove it. Rejecting a stale, unauthorized or unsupported request is a correct lifecycle outcome. These implementation properties are testable without claiming that the tests prove capability value.

The economic gate uses $n=6$--24 fresh paired mission clusters. Repeated observations from one mission contribute one cluster mean. For observed candidate-minus-Beta net mission differences $d_k$, it computes
\begin{equation}
 \underline d=\bar d-t_{0.95,n-1}\frac{s_d}{\sqrt n}-r_{\rm systematic},\qquad
 \underline\Delta=\widehat N\,\underline d-K^{B/C}_{\rm non\text{-}evaluation}-K_{\rm paired\ evidence}.
\end{equation}
The forecast $\widehat N$ uses prior observable workload; it is not future demand truth. Both test arms receive the same common held-out constraint-check coverage, and all probe costs are metered. Admission requires positive per-use and lifecycle lower bounds, independent-check acceptance, nonredundancy and the separate identity/version/scope gates. The one-sided Student-$t$ bound is a small-sample decision approximation conditional on the assumed observations. It does not cover shared semantic bias or uncertainty in demand and useful life, and it is not an empirical 95\% guarantee of commercial Alpha. An apparently positive admission can still be false in the simulated latent ground truth.

\subsection{Recipient evidence and a moving frontier}

The network comparison holds agent count and aggregate compute constant. Heterogeneous histories create overlap and genuine differences in reconstruction cost; they do not supply arbitrary talent multipliers. Recipient tests use fresh disjoint jobs and explicitly identify the baseline, adaptation cost, rejected acquisitions, useful outcomes and net difference. Downloads, active nodes and transferred bytes are not value metrics.

Beta is versioned and advances during the experiment. An improvement in ordinary models, public algorithms or generally available tooling can absorb a retained method's advantage. Revalidation may preserve a narrower integration or reliability gain, require new evidence, supersede the asset or retire it. Earlier admission history remains auditable but does not grant permanent influence. The experiment reports Alpha generation and erosion together; it does not assume lasting scarcity or exclusivity.

The future Beta path is hypothetical: it changes specified resource requirements and absorbs a declared fraction of retained advantage. It is not an announced frontier-model release, a measured 2027 capability or a new official tariff. The current documented model configuration anchors the starting baseline only.

Finally, more work using the same retained asset is reuse. A stronger self-acceleration claim requires improved effectiveness on fresh independent invention campaigns, with construction, failed searches, validation and the retained experiment protocol's full cost charged. That endpoint is separate from ordinary service profitability and network dissemination.

\section{Prices, buyer decisions and cash-constrained investment}

\subsection{A game with outside options}

Let $s_A,s_H$ be perceived probabilities of obtaining an accepted improvement, and $q_A,q_H$ the corresponding conditional correctness beliefs. Both probabilities are assumptions; neither is a benchmark score. For buyer $i$, the contingent-payment utilities are
\begin{align}
 U_i(A)&=s_A(q_A V_i-P_i)-I_i-T_i-(1-s_Aq_A)D_i-\ell_i(A),\\
 U_i(H)&=s_H(q_H V_i-H_i)-(1-s_Hq_H)D_i-\ell_i(H).
\end{align}
Here $V$ is useful outcome value, $P$ the foundry bid, $H$ the matched human fee, $I$ integration/switching burden, $T$ trust burden, and $\ell$ delay cost. The additional business loss $D$ excludes lost $V$, preventing double counting. An unsuccessful improvement attempt has no contingent fee but can still impose lost time or business harm. The human success probability and assumed contract terms are particularly weak empirical links in this comparison.

Completion belief is updated from all completed work, including no-improvement and abstention outcomes. Conditional correctness belief uses observable acceptance checks and later discovered failures rather than hidden truth. Decline has an outside value, and postponement need not be the same as choosing a human supplier. Quality beliefs can differ from realized quality; this is a risk to be modeled rather than a license to sell misleading claims.

The enterprise selects a price policy from a declared feasible set, considering current capacity and available information. It cannot observe a buyer's hidden draw or the latent success of a capability construction before deciding. The human-team reference informs an outside option and the relative price grid. It does not determine willingness to pay, and a low production cost does not mechanically set the selling price.

The policy-search objective and risk constraints are frozen before optimization. Selection is conducted on development worlds; the chosen policy is then evaluated on fresh worlds without choosing again. A policy described as best is best within that declared catalogue, simulation and objective. It is not an estimate of a universal market equilibrium or a recommendation to charge that price to actual customers.

The searched policies all enter the venture and incur setup plus a historical-development allowance. A prelaunch no-entry outside option is not optimized. Thus a loss-making selected policy means the least unfavorable searched entry policy, not that launching is economically justified. The no-separate-research arm is a diagnostic intervention on the chosen network controller; it is not a separately optimized retention-only business policy.

\subsection{Complete expense and a finite ledger}

Let total expense contain all input, output, tool and compute resources, intake and packaging, failed attempts, validation, correction, support and recovery, fixed operations, setup, and invention lifecycle costs. The three ledgers partition productive work by what it pays for, rather than placing all learning in a compulsory research project.

Consolidated pre-tax profit deducts A+B+C and common expenses from net collections. Failed work, rejected candidates and unsuccessful optional projects remain charged. Fully allocated results also retain a \usd{5,000} historical-development allowance. That allowance is an explicit scenario input, not a measurement of actual past research expenditure. Tax reserves protect available cash; the reported profit does not deduct a modeled final tax liability. Recovery and refunds are charged under the scoped prototype contract, but consequential-damage indemnity is not estimated. An inexpensive B can be economically worthwhile after an expensive profitable A; this is not a subsidy created by deleting A. Conversely, an accepted job can be unprofitable, leave no reusable content or fail to collect.

The cash recurrence is
\begin{equation}
 X_{t+1}=X_t+\mathrm{collections}_t-\mathrm{refunds}_t-\mathrm{cash\ expenses}_t.
\end{equation}
Uncollected invoices are not spendable cash. Customer savings are not enterprise revenue. A refund reverses a receipt; it must not also be counted as a duplicated resource expense in an already net-revenue calculation. The report distinguishes gross offered prices, net modeled collections, operating expenses, outstanding commitments and available cash.

Applicable \texttt{\$AGIALPHA} utility is kept separate from operating earnings. Token issuance, assumed appreciation and transfers among the network's own agents do not create consolidated profit or satisfy an investment's cash requirement.

Before accepting or starting work and before investment, the enterprise must respect its obligations and reserve:
\begin{equation}
 F_t=X_t-O_t-R_t,\qquad 0\leq K_t\leq F_t,
\end{equation}
where $X_t$ is cash, $O_t$ protected outstanding commitments, $R_t$ the liquidity reserve and $F_t$ available funds. Initial seed is shown separately from generated surplus. No current retention or research is financed with a future customer's uncollected payment. Payment delays, bad debt, refunds and bounded runoff can change solvency even when expected unit margins are positive.

\subsection{Operate, retain, wait, build or abandon}

The enterprise may preserve cash, continue delivery, wait for more evidence, retain a candidate or complementary bundle left by paid work, fund additional research, adapt an admitted asset to a recipient, or abandon an unpromising opportunity. The full incremental benefit is evaluated against its counterfactual on the same workload and uncertainty conditions. A completed job's learning is not rebilled as retention. The fully allocated report nevertheless preserves its original cost.

A simple forward condition for an asset is
\begin{equation}
\sum_{u=1}^{L}\frac{\mathbb{E}[\Delta m_u+\Delta v_u-\Delta o_u]}{(1+r)^u}>K^{B}_{\rm remaining}+K^{C}_{\rm optional},
\end{equation}
subject to cash, quality and capacity constraints. Here $\Delta m$ is changed contribution from supported work, $\Delta v$ contribution from newly feasible work, and $\Delta o$ upkeep, revalidation and opportunity cost. Recurring costs already in $\Delta o$ are excluded from the right-hand side. The model must not insert the true latent gains into this forecast. Copying, overlap, expiry, transfer failure and low reuse can defeat an otherwise attractive retention decision.

Break-even reuse is not necessarily a job count. A retained invention asset may change the success, cost or validation burden of a fresh campaign. Descendant executions and fresh campaigns therefore have different identifiers and denominators. Increased profit from using an existing solver is not evidence that the enterprise learned to invent more effectively.

The implemented opportunity measure asks whether the retained assets allow a useful job to be booked within its deadline and the current capacity state when the competent baseline cannot. This is newly serviceable work under the AI enterprise’s declared budget and promised turnaround. The human alternative has a separately assumed lead time and delay utility; it is not excluded merely for exceeding the AI offering’s window. Newly serviceable does not mean universally impossible for a human, a new physical domain or recovery of unspecified requirements. Candidate mechanisms without an implemented effect receive no demonstrated-value claim.

\section{Interpretation and limits}

\ResultConclusion

The positive case, when present, is conditional on a specific buyer population, formal task scope, outcome value, quality and search-cost distribution. Public service rates provide an outside-option anchor; they are not demand data. The fully automated workflow is a hypothesis with explicit error and abstention behavior. Neither a high benchmark score nor a large Monte Carlo sample closes that empirical gap.

The admission rule can make false positive or false negative economic judgments; it does not turn a modeled forecast into empirical Alpha. A node count, attestation, download, encrypted capsule or root commitment is not a value measure. A moving Beta can erase a previously useful advantage; no permanent rarity or moat is assumed.

The catalogue and effect distributions restrict what can be discovered. Optimizing a supplied set of attractive opportunities is different from discovering those opportunities in the real world. Future empirical work should test complete campaigns, including unsuccessful candidates and evaluator construction, then update the uncertain parameters with task-matched observations. A paid pilot is a later validation stage, not a prerequisite for reporting the present modeled result.

An empirical validation path should first verify the structured contract and independent checker on public instances; then test current frontier workflows on held-out campaigns with recorded tokens and failure costs; then obtain comparable human-team quotes for the same bounded artifact; and finally measure buyer choice, integration, collections and useful recurrence. No stage should be inferred from success at an earlier one. Physical or regulated offerings need their own evidence and professional boundaries.

\section{Break-even relationships}

For a fixed mix and unchanged conversion/cost ratios, accepted useful workload $N$ must satisfy $N(\bar p_{\rm net}-\bar c_{\rm variable})\geq C_{\rm fixed}+K$. This conditional arithmetic is not a fresh demand simulation: changing price or workload can alter queueing, buyer choice, failure mix and acceptance. The maximum affordable construction outlay at a decision is the smaller of protected available cash and the justified forward benefit after upkeep, risk and opportunity cost. A positive mean does not guarantee solvency along every path.

For a proportional price change holding realized transactions fixed, let $G$ be gross collections, $R$ refunds, $F$ percentage processing fees and $K$ all other resource expense. The accounting price factor is $f^*=K/(G-R-F)$ when the denominator is positive. Repricing fees avoids treating the current transaction fee as a fixed cost. The independently reconciled thresholds below use apparent acceptance that generated invoices, with refunds and failed delivery retained; the main outcome table instead pools expense over truly useful outputs. Per-world threshold medians and pooled useful-outcome cost are different estimands. Zero-acceptance worlds cannot be rescued by an algebraic price increase.

The minimum acceptable price must cover complete cost at the modeled workload while remaining below the buyer's willingness threshold. This interval can be empty even when AI execution is cheap. The useful lifetime and reuse condition must include copying and obsolescence. For a constant reliable per-use saving $s$ with upkeep $M$ and full incremental investment $K$, a simple threshold is $Ns>K+M$. For a capability opening a new offering, use expected contribution of the added useful outcomes, not gross revenue or hypothetical human-cost savings.
