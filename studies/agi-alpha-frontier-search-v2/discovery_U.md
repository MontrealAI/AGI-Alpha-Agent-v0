# Ordinary frontier-search arm: four candidates, no flagship selected

**Result.** None meets all four frozen gates. These are serious useful-outcome hypotheses, not positive discoveries. Recent primary work already supplies much of the proposed fabrication and recycling capability. Two inexpensive mathematical probes expose unidentifiability in the proposed measurement constructions. This is a bounded rejection result, not evidence that the opportunity space is exhausted.

**Scope.** Read only the shared initial brief and discovery freeze before searching. Used 12 search queries and eight page-open attempts; three opens returned substantive content, five failed or challenged access. Primary-paper search excerpts therefore support several comparisons. No wet work, customer evidence, spending, contact, private data, or remote mutation occurred. Monetary figures below are explicit scenario assumptions, not prices or willingness-to-pay observations.

The shared amendment arrived after search/open budgets were exhausted and before writing these records. It additionally excludes previously tested passive event-history/hysteron repair, dynamic catalytic selectivity, temporal impedance, sequence-storing fluidic computers, intervention-class generation, sample privacy, and unlearning release contracts. Candidate 1 uses active genomic writing, but its history-recording overlap is an additional caution; candidate 2 is a fabrication process, not a sequence computer.

## 1. Absolute-time genomic recorder despite changing cellular write speed

**Beneficiary and missing output.** Developmental-biology laboratories could reconstruct how long individual inaccessible cells experienced a signal, separately from signal strength, after one terminal sequencing readout. The restrictive case is cell-state-dependent editing speed without repeated imaging or externally delivered time pulses. Event order alone does not provide elapsed physical time.

**Strongest comparators.** ENGRAM explicitly combines signal-responsive recording with DNA Typewriter; its standalone intensity/duration ambiguity is not a gap left entirely untouched by prior art [S1]. Typewriter already describes combining stochastic and signal-specific symbols [S2]. The 2025 peCHYRON work adds durable, extensible, ordered recording [S3]. These are the changed capabilities that made the candidate seem plausible, but they also defeat generic “add a clock” novelty.

**Proposed connection and required advance.** Interleave constitutive reference symbols with signal symbols, treating the former as an internal time coordinate. The substantive target would be an independently calibrated physical clock that remains valid through heterogeneous transcription, cell division, and editor expression. Sharing the same variable editor does not provide that clock.

**Construction and falsifier.** First model event intensity as g(t)s(t), reference intensity as g(t), and observe only the ordered labels. A numerical time-change probe constructed a two-hour pulse and a four-hour pulse over the same ten-hour window. Both generated expected reference count 10 and signal count 4, with the signal occupying recording-clock interval [2,4]. The complete ordered-label point-process distributions coincide after time reparameterization. This rejects absolute-duration recovery under the stated nuisance model, although it does not reject amplitude discrimination or all possible independent clocks.

A successor would need a physically independent time standard, then blinded variable-expression cultures with known pulses; proposed acceptance is median duration error below 20% and no systematic state-dependent bias, outperforming ENGRAM/Typewriter under identical sequencing budgets. Failure under time-warped histories is decisive. No such biological construction was demonstrated here.

**Commercial mechanism.** Sell calibrated research recording kits and readout per experiment. Assumed $8,000 revenue per 10,000-cell batch versus $5,000 for reagents, sequencing, labour, calibration, and support leaves $3,000 before fixed R&D/licensing. Laboratories participate only if inaccessible histories are worth that premium; IP access and assay portability remain unknown. A future capability package would include reporter designs, calibration standards, identifiability counterexamples, and blinded decoding benchmarks. **Decision: reject present construction and generic novelty; independent-clock research remains unsupported.**

## 2. Seam-free microreactor with interwoven phases and selective internal membranes

**Beneficiary and missing output.** Chemical-process development laboratories need a single-piece reactor containing curved, interwoven gas/liquid channels separated by chemically selective membranes, without bonded seams or sacrificial material trapped inside. The useful output is a manufacturable reactor topology with specified transport and pressure performance, not a print-quality checker.

**Strongest comparators.** Embedded extrusion–volumetric printing already makes multimaterial and hollow structures using sacrificial inks, including roughly 228–248 micrometre channels [S4]. More damaging, March 2026 thin-film DLP demonstrates multimaterial parts and closed cavities down to 750 micrometres with less than 1 weight-percent trapped resin [S5]. Its authors explicitly include functional devices and integrated electronics. Hence “print clean sealed multimaterial cavities” is already supplied.

**Proposed connection and required advance.** Alternate a structural resin with a membrane-forming material during vat-free film delivery, so an interwoven separator is fabricated as part of its housing. The proposed advance would have to be reproducible selective transport across arbitrary curved internal interfaces while keeping those interfaces pressure-tight. Merely placing a membrane inside the established process is ordinary integration; no new compatible membrane chemistry was constructed.

**Construction and falsifier.** Fabricate a coupon containing two crossing channels with a shared membrane and an otherwise identical impermeable control. Proposed gates: no bulk bypass at 100 kPa, at least tenfold transport selectivity for a specified benign test pair, and stable performance for 100 hours. Compare with assembled commercial membranes and a direct application of thin-film DLP. A prior-art build meeting these gates without a new material/process principle defeats novelty. The paper-level probe already rejects the broad cavity claim; the narrower membrane outcome remains an unsupported materials hypothesis, not evidence of discovery.

**Commercial mechanism.** Contract-fabricate validated reactors. Assumed $600 per accepted unit; $250 variable build cost divided by 80% yield plus $150 testing/support/amortization gives $462.50 cost and $137.50 contribution. Customers must value geometry and seam elimination above assembly alternatives; printer time, material compatibility, leak/transport testing, solvent use, and yield bind capacity. Licensing, durability, and repeatability are unpriced risks. A future capability package would include CAD coupons, resin/interface recipes, printing parameters, and transport/pressure fixtures. **Decision: reject broad novelty; do not promote the unbuilt narrow formulation.**

## 3. Single-shot passive longwave infrared spectral capture of a nonrepeatable event

**Beneficiary and missing output.** Combustion and transient-physics laboratories could obtain a quantitative spatial spectrum of one event without active sample illumination or repeated wavelength scans. The ambitious target is a simultaneous 8–12 micrometre spectral cube at useful spatial and temporal resolution using silicon detection.

**Strongest comparators.** A 2025 primary preprint already demonstrates incoherent thermal-target upconversion around 10 micrometres [S6]. A February 2026 preprint reports passive 3–5 micrometre imaging at 100 kHz [S7]. A June 2026 paper reports nonlinear-dispersion snapshot spectral imaging at 2.5–4 micrometres [S8]. They change the feasible instrument components. The retrieved evidence does not establish the entire proposed longwave, passive, transient spectral combination, but absence from these abstracts is insufficient novelty evidence.

**Proposed connection and required advance.** Translate incoherent longwave radiation through a pumped conversion stage, encode wavelength into spatial camera channels, and calibrate the forward optical operator. The substantive advance would require preserving enough independent spectral measurements and photon throughput for nonrepeatable events. A learned reconstruction cannot itself manufacture the missing measurements. Straightforward multiple-channel image splitting may be feasible but appears an ordinary spectrometer extension until a stronger principle is shown.

**Construction and falsifier.** A synthetic 64-measurement/192-radiance-bin operator had rank 64 and nullity 128. Two everywhere-positive cubes differing by 4.08% in relative Euclidean norm produced detector values differing by at most 3.33e-16. Thus unrestricted three-band recovery at the original spatial pixel count fails even before noise. This is a deliberately generic linear measurement counterexample, not a calibrated simulation of the cited instruments.

The next construction must allocate enough independent detector channels or state an independently justified low-dimensional scene class. Proposed bench gates are less than 10% radiometric error on blind mixed-spectrum transients, measured photon budget, and one exposure without repeated-scene assumptions; comparator is a calibrated conventional spectrometer plus the strongest upconversion architecture. Failure of rank or photon accounting rejects the instrument claim before procurement.

**Commercial mechanism.** Sell a calibrated instrument to spectroscopy cores. Assumed $45,000 sale against $35,000 components/assembly/calibration and $5,000 support leaves $5,000 before R&D. Utility requires an experimentally demonstrated information advantage worth ownership and pump alignment costs. Crystals, pump, detector, conversion efficiency, calibration drift, and service bind delivery; demand and pricing are unobserved. A future capability package would include the optical operator, calibration target set, nullspace tests, and measured photon budget. **Decision: reject unconstrained construction; restricted version lacks demonstrated substantive novelty.**

## 4. Preserve all polymer streams while separating stretch-textile blends

**Beneficiary and missing output.** Textile recyclers could recover reusable elastane and host fibres instead of destroying the stretch component or downgrading the whole garment. A narrower unresolved target is dyed cotton/PET/elastane blends with all three polymer streams reusable and a closed solvent cycle.

**Strongest comparators.** The 2023 tert-amyl-alcohol work selectively disassembles elastane, particularly in polyamide blends; it notes that selectivity with cotton/viscose requires improvement [S9]. More decisively, September 2026 primary work reports deep eutectic solvents that dissolve elastane selectively, recover it by centrifugation, preserve PET/PA6/PA66 fibre properties, and allow recovered host fibres to be re-extruded [S10]. January 2025 work also describes sequential cotton/polyester chemical recycling [S11]. The original intact-elastane-plus-host-fibre outcome is therefore already supplied for synthetic hosts.

**Proposed connection and required advance.** Couple selective elastane extraction to a second, orthogonal cotton/PET separation while preserving molecular weight and recovering solvent. Extending an existing separation train to another blend is ordinary process development unless a new selectivity window or material transformation is demonstrated. The retrieved DES abstract does not test cotton; that omission is not proof of a new valuable capability.

**Construction and falsifier.** On a small synthetic ternary blend, pre-register mass balance, polymer molecular-weight distributions, tensile retention, dye carryover, and solvent recovery. Proposed gates: at least 90% recovery of each polymer, at least 90% molecular-weight retention, and repeat performance across ten solvent cycles. Run the published DES approach and sequential separation as comparators. If those already meet the output, novelty fails; if solvent recycling degrades selectivity, feasibility fails. No chemical experiment occurred.

**Commercial mechanism.** Offer toll processing; customer retains recovered polymers and pays only when specified properties pass. Assumed $500 per tonne fee and $450 all-in processing/quality/waste/capital cost gives $50 contribution; customers participate only when recovered-stream value minus transport exceeds that fee. At an assumed 5 kg solvent/kg feed and $2/kg solvent, increasing loss from 1% to 3% raises makeup cost from $100 to $300 per tonne, overturning the assumed margin. This sensitivity is arithmetic, not measured process economics. A future capability package would include solvent recipes, ternary mass balances, reuse-loop data, and qualification specifications. **Decision: reject baseline novelty; ternary preservation remains unevidenced, not a discovery.**
