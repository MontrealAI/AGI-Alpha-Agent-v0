Abstract

AGI ALPHA constructed a confidential planning instrument: three parties can jointly determine whether their declared reserve pool covers every agreed simultaneous-demand scenario while keeping raw eligibility, availability and withheld-asset inputs at their respective computation roles. The opportunity emerged from a bounded search across twelve possibilities and a subsequent, explicitly attributed synthesis. The instrument changes what the participants can learn under a nondisclosure restriction; its purpose is a pre-emergency funding decision, not faster matching or an equipment dispatch guarantee.

After source and policy freezing, the instrument matched an independent allocation oracle on 240/240 fresh synthetic worlds, including a separately executed successor with 41 scenarios per world. An ordinary identical MPC library tied exactly. The commercial model found $9,324 contribution per accepted-equivalent assessment at a $12,000 fee, but its complete 180-day venture lost $30,147 on average across 1,000 fresh modeled worlds. No launch was the selected policy. A prospective follow-up of all nine conditional-commitment policies also launched no ventures. The practical contribution is an executed private-planning capability and a measured within-model admission boundary: this pipeline does not finance its commercialization.

The technical result is an application of established multiparty computation and matching theory. The experiment uses public synthetic inputs and actual local processes, not independently operated utilities. The market segment, customer values and deployment readiness are modeled. Exact collections are zero; complete actual research expenditure is unknown. Ordinary identical-library execution is a competent control, and the study does not establish superior foresight, numerical Operational Alpha, incremental retained Alpha or a cryptographic novelty. It establishes a reusable, inspectable capability and a concrete economic condition for its next investment.

1. A new planning answer

Before a group shares sensitive inventories or commissions a full reserve agreement, it can ask a useful question: could our declared resources jointly cover the incidents we agree matter? A bilateral answer does not suffice. The same available asset may appear to solve two owners' problems until both need it simultaneously. Yet a centralized inventory can be unacceptable to a participant even when a narrowly specified collective answer is acceptable.

We construct that answer as a bounded information product. Three owners each supply two distinct asset slots. Six public demand locations define the scenario family. Eligibility, scenario availability and asset-specific withholding remain private inputs to the computation. The original mission covers all six single-demand and fifteen double-demand incidents. A successor extends the question to all twenty triple-demand incidents as well. For each scenario, every demand must receive a different eligible, usable asset. The released answer is the conjunction over the entire family.

This creates an instrument for deciding whether to fund more detailed cooperation. A positive answer means the supplied constraints permit a complete allocation for each listed incident. A negative answer means those inputs do not certify the pool; it can still be a valuable reason to defer a larger project. Neither answer establishes physical compatibility, truthful ownership, transport access, future availability or a binding reserve right.

2. Why this possibility matters—and why the gap is narrower than the ambition

GAO identifies reserve-transformer availability as a consequential electricity-grid issue, including purchasing, transport, supply-chain and smaller-utility participation barriers. Its evidence motivates looking for better cooperation; it does not identify confidentiality as the dominant barrier or estimate demand for this product. [1]

Existing organizations already address substantial parts of the problem. SPAREConnect provides decentralized contacts, has no central asset database and supports voluntary direct arrangements during emergencies or nonroutine failures. Its documented service does not supply the pre-emergency, jointly constrained answer tested here. Grid Assurance offers dedicated reserve access, modeling and logistics through an existing subscription arrangement. Those approaches are real outside options; their wider physical responsibilities are not equivalent to selling one assessment bit. [2,3]

The opportunity hypothesis concerns a narrower group: owners with a funded cooperative-planning decision who will authorize a complete aggregate query but will not centralize its raw inputs. A sponsor can purchase the assessment while each owner retains its data and its authority over any subsequent reserve commitment. If a trusted broker can receive the inputs, ordinary cleartext analysis should usually be cheaper. If the bit cannot change a funded decision, its value may be zero. Both cases appear in the economic model.

The relevant change is the ability to assemble a working instrument, its tests, its authority boundary and its commercial arrangement in one agent-assisted research cycle. Frontier reasoning, tool use and source-grounded construction make that path plausible and were used in the actual study. Secure computation itself predates this work. We do not claim the application was historically impossible, that existing providers lack competent people, or that an AI advantage over a human engineering team has been measured.

The closest prior art matters. Beaude and colleagues already study privacy-preserving distributed allocation under individual constraints, including secure computation and energy applications. Prime Match demonstrates production private financial-inventory matching with malicious security, a stronger adversarial setting than our passive MPyC fixture. Private inventory coordination also has earlier research precedents. The novelty claim is therefore limited to the disclosed application hypothesis, scoped construction and connected evidence package. The cryptographic primitive and the general idea of confidential allocation are established. [4–6]

3. Discovery before demonstration

The initial brief supplied the ambition and existing ecosystem, not this application. A frozen common packet described consequential needs, stranded capacity, coordination costs and missing representations. It excluded the available battery, microscopy and manufacturing demonstrations from flagship selection. Ordinary frontier search (U) and expert AGI ALPHA search (E) each received the same query cap and evaluation criteria.

In round one, each arm could make six primary-source queries. U proposed conditional transformer reserves, thermal-network commitments and joint prospective rare-disease evidence. E proposed contingent relocation, fleet guarantees on non-firm connections and a generic-medicine restart option. A label-masked independent review rejected all six as flagships in their proposed form. Prior-art checks found substantial existing capabilities in reserve services, synchronized relocation, fleet guarantees and long-term medicine procurement. The failures and the probes remain part of the study, including a relocation construction that depended on explicit bridge funding and full principal-loss coverage.

Round two gave both fresh contexts the same corrective feedback and eight queries. The six proposals concerned genomic ambiguity, tactile diagrams, legacy provenance, zero-knowledge meaning, nonvisual editing and mixed-language communication. Executed probes reproduced useful known-method behaviors; they did not earn world-first claims. Some candidates remain reasonable future research, rather than disproven needs.

The selected reserve application is a cross-round researcher-agent synthesis. U's first search supplied the utility need; the second round sharpened the importance of preserving the meaning of a collective question under a representation boundary. Subsequent primary-source checks supplied established private computation as the construction mechanism. The independent synthesis review issued a conditional go, with the prior-art boundary above. It would be incorrect to count this later synthesis as an E victory over U.

The comparison includes two actual U and two actual E hosted completions. Exact backend revision, realized token budgets and billing were unavailable; shared information and query caps were controlled. No human-expert search trial was run. An opportunity absent from these four outputs does not establish that people would miss it. The complete candidate records separate novelty, usefulness, feasibility and commercial plausibility; their ordinal scores are selection judgments, not probabilities. The accompanying discovery record preserves all twelve ideas, source logs, commitments, probes, rejections and attribution. [E1]

What was supplied to the engineering agent

After selection, the root researcher-agent specified the three-party, six-asset, six-demand function and Hall-condition formulation, then authored the independent oracle, generator and controller. A separate implementation context wrote the MPC worker from that specification; another context constructed adversarial cases and reviewed the result. The seeds and acceptance criteria were fixed before confirmation. Correct execution establishes software behavior; it is not evidence that the implementation agent independently discovered the opportunity or invented matching theory. Conventional controls receive the same formulation.

5. An executable confidential instrument

5.1 Exact predicate

Let a scenario contain a set of demand locations D. For every nonempty subset S of D, let N(S) be the set of assets eligible for at least one demand in S and usable in that scenario. Usability is the conjunction of declared availability and absence of asset-specific withholding. The instrument privately checks |N(S)| ≥ |S| for every such subset. Hall's condition is equivalent to the existence of an injective matching in this finite unit-capacity model. It then combines all scenario results before opening a value.

The quantifiers matter: for every declared scenario, there exists an assignment. The instrument does not require one fixed assignment to cover mutually exclusive incidents. Each incident itself must satisfy all simultaneous demands without double assignment. Assets are assumed distinct and truthful; the code does not prove ownership or prevent two organizations from misreporting the same real asset. Withholding is an asset-specific bit, not an arbitrary minimum-reserve quota.

The pair family has 21 scenarios and 51 Hall conditions per world. Adding triples produces 41 scenarios and 191 conditions. The independent cleartext oracle uses recursive assignment enumeration rather than the Hall implementation. A separate bilateral diagnostic asks only whether each individual demand has a neighbor. It is a deliberately limited diagnostic, never a proxy for competent ordinary frontier-model use.

5.2 Execution and security boundary

The study executes three explicitly supervised Python processes using the pinned, published MPyC 0.11 wheel. Its Shamir-based protocols support a passive adversary controlling fewer than half the parties: one of three in this configuration. The secure integer width is eight bits; counts range from zero to six. The security parameter is 40. All loops and circuit shape depend on public dimensions and scenarios. Only the final conjunction per world is opened; no allocation, count, private edge or per-scenario answer is released by the worker. [7,E2]

The processes communicate over plaintext localhost for this research. Each worker opens only its own owner input file, and the instrumentation records byte counts without retaining protocol-message contents. The research supervisor can read every synthetic input to compute the oracle. This is actual multiparty software execution, not independent organizational isolation, authenticated remote deployment, malicious security or a security audit.

Owner-file commitments include a nonce. Reproducible fixture nonces and inputs are deliberately public. A real deployment would require fresh secret nonces and separately controlled endpoints; unsalted hashes of small private vectors would permit dictionary attacks. Integrity hashes bind bytes, not their truth.

5.3 Query authority is distinct from correctness

The wrapper reuses unchanged native AGI ALPHA trust and signed-Journal components. It binds the exact worker, public manifest, input commitments, output shape, scope and epoch to an explicit local controller grant. Before computation, an atomic Journal transaction consumes both a nonce and the scope/epoch slot. A fresh nonce cannot reopen the same slot. Changed scenarios, changed bytes, expiry and revocation require current authority.

Research batches authorize one bit for each independent synthetic world: a 120-world manifest explicitly permits 120 outputs. The prospective customer offering permits one world per authorized epoch. There are no adaptive subset queries within a grant. These are local identity fixtures, not live ENS validation or three real owners' consent. Retained Journal integrity does not provide malicious-host rollback resistance. Ephemeral private keys remain outside the published package; only public signed receipts are exported.

5.4 Prospective confirmation

Development used 32 worlds, balanced across sparse, medium and dense eligibility and correlated owner outages. The generator preserves all scenarios, scenario-specific unavailability and withholding; a correlated event removes both assets of a selected owner. These probabilities define a stress generator, not measured utility failure rates.

Source, dependency and policy hashes were frozen after development and structured challenges, before generating 120 new original-mission worlds and 120 new successor worlds from disjoint seeds. Every world remains in the record. The unchanged ordinary-library control replays the same fixtures with the same information. Local timing is descriptive and is not converted into a broad AI speed or cost advantage. [E2,E3]

The frozen original mission matched the independent oracle on 120/120 worlds: 85 feasible and 35 infeasible. The separately executed successor also matched on 120/120: 84 feasible and 36 infeasible. Together, the two fresh cohorts contain 240 accepted computational outcomes, including 71 correct negative answers. These are synthetic assessment acceptances, not customer acceptances. Both ordinary identical-library replays returned exactly the same answers and public input/scenario hashes.

6. From Insight to an inspectable venture artifact

The study inspects and reuses existing components rather than replacing their roles. Their actual behavior sets the claim ceiling. The existing Insight engine validates a supplied scheduling-world schema; it does not validate the truth of an arbitrary opportunity. Passing this reserve opportunity into that evaluator produces the expected schema refusal, which is preserved. Existing Insight serialization and Discovery parsing can preserve the supplied opportunity object. The independent agent search and construction supply its evidence. [E4]

The same opportunity enters the existing Nova project constructor. A Nova-Seed preserves the hypothesis, provenance, sources, uncertainty, witness, rights and next experiment. The actual existing seal/open functions round-trip the complete public research genome. Its disclosed research passphrase provides reproducibility, not customer confidentiality. The existing FusionPlan compiler and verifier bind executable work specifications, dependencies, acceptance requirements and unfunded encoded bounties. Additional research metadata records budgets, permissions and falsifiers that are not native enforcement semantics of every plan field.

MARK-related workflow checks make the artifacts inspectable within their supported local boundary. The original empty production-qualified profile registry remains unchanged. Unfunded zero-value arithmetic is not a listing, investment, earned income or operational permission. No token was minted, listed or traded, no funds moved, and no live authorization was expanded.

The final source-bound artifact run passes 15/15 checks and binds twenty public provenance files. The native query-authority campaign passes 21/21 controls, including a concurrent-consumer race with exactly one acceptance and refusal of the old grant for the successor. A separate signed local successor.agent.agi.eth fixture verifies the descriptor and worker bytes and passes 5/5 refusal controls. This fixture maps a name to an Ed25519 key with the existing worker role; it does not resolve live ENS. Validator roles and empty operational authority remain intact. Earlier provisional and editorial-correction artifact runs are retained.

In the existing broader implementation, $AGIALPHA can be an encoded payment or bounty denomination where the applicable contracts and permissions support it. This edition exercises no such funded transaction. Its commercial model uses USD service fees, and speculative token appreciation contributes zero modeled productive surplus. Cryptographic provenance can preserve an artifact; it cannot make the opportunity true or profitable.

7. Price the actual information product

7.1 Buyer value and outside options

The buyer is an association or consortium sponsor acting as the three owners’ collective procurement agent. It purchases one complete assessment; member benefits are counted once, not as an additional fourth buyer’s value. All owners must accept the same purpose, output, deployment assumptions and participation costs. A correct negative answer satisfies the contract; incomplete scenarios or unsupported disclosure assumptions do not. The primary arrangement has the sponsor pay the whole-pool fee while owners bear their own input preparation and endpoint costs. Those customer costs reduce participation utility and are not hidden vendor revenue.

No equivalent public service price was found for this exact novel information product. We therefore use a human-led development comparison and a separate value model. Expected pool decision value is modeled as confidence times the sum of avoidable next-stage planning exposure weighted by the probability the bit changes the decision, plus independently avoidable coordination effort. It does not monetize avoided outages, transformer asset value, a hypothetical insurance payout or the mere cost of developing the software.

The central illustrative inputs are $60,000 of otherwise exposed planning expenditure, a 0.35 probability of changing that funding decision, 36 coordination hours saved and a 0.65 confidence/actionability factor. Each owner prepares eight hours of inputs and incurs $100 of runtime expense in that central scenario. All are transparent assumptions. Low/high scenarios, predictable answers and zero actionable value remain in the model.

For each fee and payment policy, participants compare the service with no purchase and, where disclosure is acceptable, a cheaper trusted broker using the same formulation and exact conventional solver. Aggregate surplus cannot override a member whose own participation utility is negative. Sponsor budget, owner consent, procurement delay and finite capacity also constrain uptake. Raising the fee recomputes these choices.

7.2 Tested policy family and financing

The study tests $6,000, $12,000 and $18,000 whole-pool fees; 25%, 50% and 100% refundable deposits; direct launch; conditional launch after sufficient noncash commitments; and no launch. Commitments do not increase buyer values or create cash. Acquisition and screening are paid even when no launch follows. Readiness is staged, uncertain and financed from a finite $60,000 seed; deposits remain restricted refund liabilities until acceptance.

The horizon is 180 days followed by a 120-day settlement tail for the existing cohort. The opportunity pipeline is finite: three, six or nine qualified pools, not an assumed number of completed sales. At most two assessments can run concurrently and two can be booked per fixed thirty-day booking period. Remaining invoices have thirty-day terms and explicit lateness/noncollection. Admission uses ex-ante resource bounds rather than knowledge of future costs or outcomes. The model keeps cash, accounts receivable, revenue, expenses, deposits, obligations, reserves and available funds separate.

7.3 Fresh results: contribution is positive; the business case fails

Development compared nineteen policies on 250 worlds, then froze the chosen direct policy, tied conditional policy and no-launch comparison before opening 1,000 new seeds. No launch wins the stated objective. The best tested direct policy from development is $12,000 with a fully refundable upfront deposit. The same policy remains loss-making in fresh confirmation. Only 31.8% of worlds reach deployment readiness under the staged qualification and modeled $35,000 startup-spend cap; the mean accepted volume is 0.218 assessments.

7.4 Improve the experiment after the conditional tie

All nine conditional policies launched nothing in development and tied on screening loss. The original grid-order choice, $6,000, was uninformative: at an illustrative $3,000 screening spend it needs seventeen conservative commitments, beyond the nine-opportunity bound. The $12,000 and $18,000 policies need seven and four, respectively. These are contingent commitments, not guaranteed accepted sales. The simpler proposal arithmetic of 8/4/3 completed sales is preserved and is not substituted for this stricter rule.

We therefore froze a separate prospective follow-up testing all nine unchanged conditional policies on another 1,000 paired worlds. No value, price, demand, timing, cost or threshold was improved. All nine still launched zero ventures and each incurred $3,510 mean screening loss. At $18,000, the mutually exclusive first bottlenecks were too few qualified pools (260 worlds), too few timely arrivals (149), incomplete procurement before day 62 (577), missing all-owner consent (12), and value/budget/outside-option or screening conditions (2). This establishes where this modeled pipeline stops; it does not prove the real market cannot exist.

The independent controlled test also verifies the positive branch: four already secured, sufficiently valuable $18,000 commitments can satisfy the declared admission rule, whereas three cannot. Those supplied favorable commitments are a condition to validate, not observed demand or a new simulation success rate.

7.5 Cash and obligations

At day 180, the direct-policy mean cash balance is $29,889.53, including the remaining portion of the original $60,000 seed. Its mean support payable is $36.41 and operating reserve is $1,272.00, leaving $28,581.12 available funds. Receivables, unearned deposits and other payables are zero at that snapshot in this particular policy. Available funds have fallen $31,418.88 from seed; the balance is not earned surplus. After scheduled settlement, mean cash is $29,853.12 and the operating reserve is released. Insolvency stresses can retain unpaid accounts payable even after all scheduled tail events; those debts remain deducted from available funds.

No launch preserves seed with zero new expenditure or income. The already incurred research is separately unknown, not erased by this forward decision. Neither simulated receipts nor seed financing is actual revenue.

7.6 Sensitivity and the model-upgrade condition

The frozen direct policy remains loss-making when qualified opportunities fall to three (mean −$29,253), value halves (−$31,831), modeled mission acceptance falls to 0.65 (−$30,842), model tariffs rise fourfold (−$30,558) or repeated unknown expense increases by $5,000 (−$31,488). A $100,000 common-incident stress produces a mean loss of $61,625 and can leave unfunded obligations. The cheaper-broker/zero-privacy segment also removes the service's reason to be purchased. Full paired stresses, including maintenance and reuse, remain in the package.

A labeled post-result analytic bound addresses cheaper models without inventing a performance gain. Holding readiness, participation and fixed costs fixed, serving all 0.837 mean interested pools at $12,000 with zero repeat cost would yield at most $10,044 revenue against $32,179 fixed/startup/acquisition/insurance cost: a loss of at least $22,135. Merely cheaper inference cannot close that conditional gap. Changes in buyer beliefs, readiness, demand or the offered output require a new study; no numerical Operational Alpha is inferred.

The selected policy is best within this finite simulated family and objective. Monte Carlo percentiles describe the specified worlds; they are not confidence intervals for real demand. Price, planning value, privacy preference, consent, mission acceptance and readiness probability remain unmeasured. The current research collected no customer money.
