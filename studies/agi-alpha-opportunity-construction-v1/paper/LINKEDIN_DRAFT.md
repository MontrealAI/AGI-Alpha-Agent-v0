Our new AGI ALPHA FLYWHEEL research edition explores that possibility through confidential reserve-pool feasibility: can the available assets cover every agreed simultaneous-demand scenario?

We built and ran the instrument. Three local secure-computation processes agreed with an independent allocation oracle on 240 of 240 fresh synthetic cases. A separately tasked successor extended the mission from 21 to 41 scenarios, including all triples, with a fresh authorization. The same ordinary reusable library tied exactly.

The opportunity came from a documented search across twelve candidates—not from a supplied final application. Established reserve-sharing programs and secure-computation research are essential prior art. Our contribution is the tested application, its precise acceptance boundary and the connected venture experiment.

The economics sharpened the result. At a modeled $12,000 fee, delivery contributed about $9,324 after variable costs. But readiness, acquisition and sparse uptake produced a complete six-month venture loss of about $30,147 on average. A fresh follow-up testing all nine conditional-commitment policies still found no launch. Under these assumptions, waiting wins.

That is useful intelligence: a working capability, an economic hurdle we can name, and a successor that inherits the instrument without inheriting old authority.
