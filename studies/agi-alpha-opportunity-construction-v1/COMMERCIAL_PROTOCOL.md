# Prospective commercial protocol

## Offering and evidence

The offering is one confidential, pre-emergency reserve-pool assessment for **three named owner/computing parties**, two padded asset slots per party and six public demand locations. It releases one aggregate feasibility bit for the complete authorized scenario family, with public scope, version and release evidence. A correct zero is an accepted assessment. An incomplete or incorrect computation, missing permission or an unsupported deployment is failed delivery. The information product does not reserve equipment, certify physical compatibility, authorize dispatch or promise outage protection.

All dollar figures, customer preferences, consent rates, mission success and consumption are modeled. Actual collections are zero and actual complete construction cost is unknown. The executed local MPC study does not calibrate those commercial assumptions. No profit, demand or numerical efficiency result transfers from the battery study.

`config/commercial_protocol.json` is the exact machine-readable policy. The earlier `evidence/commercial_design_proposal.json` remains preserved as the design proposal, including the human-led research and deployment pathways. Those budgets are not human service prices or evidence of willingness to pay.

## Buyer participation and outside options

The association acts as the **procurement agent for the three-owner coalition**. Its budget and utility represent the coalition's aggregate decision value; it is not a fourth beneficiary whose value is counted again. Each owner must separately accept its share of that value relative to its input preparation, owner-controlled runtime and permitted disclosure burden. The sponsor pays the single whole-pool fee. Customer-side preparation costs are not vendor revenue or silently free automation.

The valuation scenario concerns the next planning decision: discounted avoidable follow-up planning exposure plus distinct coordination effort. It includes a zero-information-value segment. No outage-loss valuation enters it. The central pre-delivery illustration is approximately $16,110 of discounted decision value less $2,823 of three-owner preparation/runtime expense, leaving $13,287 before the fee. The executable buyer choice also applies expected delivery probability, a small accepted disclosure burden and the opportunity cost of a refundable deposit. The $18,000 package therefore needs higher-value pools; price does not leave demand fixed.

A competent trusted broker or in-house team can use the same discovered formulation and exact cleartext solver when every owner permits the required disclosure. Its modeled internal analytical cost is separate from the novel MPC development pathway. Existing reserve programs and postponement remain outside options. The zero-privacy-demand stress gives the broker a particularly strong, inexpensive alternative.

## Policy family and finite opportunity pipeline

The development grid contains nine direct-launch policies and nine conditional-launch policies: whole-pool fees of $6,000, $12,000 or $18,000, each with refundable deposits of 25%, 50% or 100%. A separate no-launch policy spends no new forward funds. Both operating families see the same finite worlds and price-dependent pools.

The six-month horizon contains 3, 6 or 9 hypothetical qualified sponsor opportunities. They are approached during an initial 90-day campaign; procurement takes a modeled 15–90 days. Qualification does not mean consent, purchase or a verified market-size estimate. The capacity limit is two concurrent assessments and **two bookings per fixed 30-day period**, not a rolling thirty-day limit. Some interested pools cannot be served before the horizon; they remain counted as unserved.

The conditional family collects explicit **noncash commitments** at the disclosed fee. Its additional delay reduces value by 5%; there is no assumed increase in demand. It starts readiness only when conservative expected surviving, accepted and collected fees, net of processing, cover startup, six months of fixed cost, insurance, all incurred screening/acquisition and forecast delivery cost for every commitment. The launch deadline is day 62, allowing the maximum 90-day readiness duration and 28-day delivery allowance within day 180.

For illustration, with $3,000 already spent on screening, the conservative commitment threshold is 17 pools at $6,000, seven at $12,000 or four at $18,000. These are conditional commitments rather than guaranteed accepted sales. The $6,000 case cannot meet the threshold within the nine-opportunity bound. The earlier proposal's simpler 8/4/3 accepted-sale arithmetic omitted attrition, conservatism and failure; it is not the commitment rule.

## Startup, complete cost and financing

The company has $60,000 of finite seed capital. Necessary startup costs are distinct from actual unmetered research and from the human-led comparison. The prospective AI productization budget spans $9,547/$25,908/$74,526, including explicitly scoped security, independent operator integration, input-semantics, governance and release verification work. It does not imply that the difference from the human-led pathway is measured Operational Alpha.

The approved startup spend cap is $35,000. A specification gate follows the first 20% of work. The deployment/qualification gate follows another 40%; the remaining 40% is spent only after that gate passes. A cost overrun stops at the authorized cap. All work already incurred is charged, including failed readiness. A pre-development audit caught and corrected an initial implementation that tested the second gate too late; no development or fresh outcomes had been opened when that correction was made.

Repeat execution has a central $1,200 base allowance covering judgment/orchestration, compute, verification, packaging, support, settlement/archive and a $400 explicit unknown-extra allowance. Attempt-dependent categories incur bounded retries. Failed deliveries retain their consumed costs and refund charges. No mandatory human-review hours are imposed on the automated repeat workflow; additional human effort is a separate sensitivity.

The $180 judgment/orchestration envelope is explicitly hypothetical: `gpt-6-astra` at 10 million input and one million billed output tokens costs $150 at documented $10/$50 per-million tariffs; `gpt-6.1-sol` at the same aggregate counts costs $30 at $2/$10. These are aggregate mission envelopes, split into individual requests below long-context surcharge thresholds, with no assumed cache discount. Billed reasoning is included in output. They are not measured consumption or a forecast fitted to the small MPC circuit. Private owner inputs do not enter frontier-model context. Official source URLs and configuration descriptions are recorded in the protocol; 4x and 10x price stresses are declared in source.

Recurring business costs begin after readiness. Acquisition/screening costs apply to all approached opportunities, including failed coalitions and a campaign that never launches. Insurance/underwriting is an unquoted technology-service allowance, not physical-grid insurance. The monetary incident stress is a remediation shock and suspension of new sales; it does not establish a comprehensive bound on confidentiality damages or promise insurance recovery.

Refundable deposits are restricted liabilities. They do not fund readiness or delivery and do not become revenue until acceptance. Admission reserves a declared maximum for delivery, retries, support, fees and recovery instead of inspecting future realized token/cost draws. Accepted but unpaid invoices accrue both payment fees and an expected-credit-loss allowance. Collection or default settles/releases each exactly once. Support is accrued at acceptance and settled later. Tail handling is charged within each delivery.

Report day-180 profit, cash, receivables, credit allowances, unearned deposits, support/processing payables, other unpaid obligations and reserves separately. The 120-day tail completes all scheduled jobs, invoices and support events. In an insolvency stress, accounts payable can remain unpaid; the `settled` label denotes that scheduled tail processing has ended, not that every creditor has been paid. Available funds continue to subtract those unpaid obligations.

## Retention and successor interpretation

The original instrument already supports both pairs and triples. Successor execution transfers unchanged capabilities into a newly authorized mission. It is operational portability, not a newly learned algorithm. An ordinary library with identical content is a tie.

The primary commercial baseline reuses its A-funded product and incurs no second retention bill; C is zero. An optional B assessment is triggered once after the first accepted originating assessment and costs $500 even when rejected. If no origin is accepted, no such assessment is performed. A forced zero-gain retention counterfactual adds $1,500 release, six months of $200 maintenance and $50 per subsequent use. The complete incremental saving required per use is therefore `50 + 3200/N`: $1,116.67 at three uses, $583.33 at six and $316.67 at twelve. These are cost thresholds, not measured savings. Same-model U/E and E+/E mission performance remains unmeasured.

## Selection, confirmation and reproduction

Development uses 250 worlds, seeds 630100–630349. The objective is mean day-180 available operating funds after liabilities/reserves, excluding original seed. Select the best policy within each operating family, retain all candidates, and compare with no-launch. Exact ties retain the declared grid order; they do not establish a price preference. Source, configuration, proposal and development hashes are bound in `results/economics_freeze.json` before opening fresh confirmation seeds 9811000–9811999.

Development selected no-launch overall. The direct-family choice was $12,000 with a 100% refundable deposit, with a mean full-venture loss of $30,005.54 and 0.18 accepted assessments. All nine conditional policies tied at a $3,435.69 screening loss; none launched. Grid order therefore selected $6,000/25%, a non-informative conditional tie rather than a commercial recommendation. Those losses and the no-launch decision are preserved. Conditional-on-readiness service contribution will be reported separately from full startup-inclusive venture profit.

## Fresh confirmation results

The frozen 1,000-world confirmation keeps **no-launch** as the selected decision. The direct-family policy loses $30,146.88 on average over six months, with a 5th–95th percentile interval of −$41,175.70 to −$8,815.51. It accepts 0.218 assessments per world; 31.8% of worlds reach readiness. The frozen conditional policy spends $3,510.67 on screening on average and launches none. All nine conditional policies tied without launches during development; the freshly confirmed $6,000 policy is an uninformative grid-order tie that cannot satisfy the conservative threshold within nine opportunities. This does not establish a confirmed commercial result for higher-fee commitment pools.

At the selected direct fee of $12,000, modeled variable delivery cost per accepted-equivalent assessment is $2,676.15, including failed-booking costs. The resulting $9,323.85 is **delivery contribution**, not operating profit. It excludes startup, acquisition, recurring business and insurance. In the 318 worlds that reach readiness, mean full-venture profit is −$32,364.29; even treating successful startup as sunk leaves a mean operating loss of $6,932.13. The candidate has a useful conditional delivery margin but has not established a profitable complete service business under the declared pipeline.

The optional assessment-and-reject treatment costs an additional $80.50 per world on average, corresponding to $500 in each world with an accepted originating mission. Forced retention with zero capability gain costs an additional $517.90 on average. No newly retained Alpha or frontier Operational Alpha is measured.

`results/economics_plot_data.json` and `results/economics_policy_table.csv` contain compact figure/table data. Rebuild them with `PYTHONDONTWRITEBYTECODE=1 python src/economic_tables.py`. The JSON explains both the per-accepted-equivalent denominator and the conditional-on-readiness calculation. Some high-cost sensitivities show a smaller loss because the funding rule refuses startup and serves no customers; this is avoided exposure, not improved productivity. The next useful commercial test is whether a real coalition can obtain the specified privacy-preserving decision value and secure enough disclosed-fee commitments before readiness spending.

## Separate prospective conditional-policy supplement

The primary grid-order tie left a concrete unresolved question: higher-fee conditional policies were explored, but the frozen fresh policy was structurally unable to launch. A separate protocol, `config/commercial_conditional_supplement.json`, therefore bound the unchanged primary source, configuration and evidence plus a new diagnostic runner **before** generating new seeds 9821000–9821999. It tested all nine existing conditional policies without changing values, demand, timing, costs, thresholds or the primary selection. The primary files were verified unchanged before and after execution.

Every policy again launched zero ventures and lost $3,509.86 on average in screening expenses. The hierarchical first-failed-gate diagnostics are:

| Whole-pool fee | Insufficient total qualified pools | Too few timely arrivals | Too few procurement completions | Insufficient owner consent | Value, budget, outside option or screening threshold |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| $6,000 | 1,000 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| $12,000 | 260 | 516 | 224 | 0 | 0 |
| $18,000 | 260 | 149 | 577 | 12 | 2 |

The counts are the same for all three deposit choices at each fee. They classify the first limiting gate in the declared sequence; they do not establish a unique causal explanation for every failed world. In particular, the sparse early procurement pipeline prevents most worlds from reaching the consent/value gates. These are consequences of the stated hypothetical assumptions, not observed rejection by utilities.

The full compact output is `results/economics_conditional_supplement.json`. The commands are `python -B src/economic_conditional_supplement.py freeze` and then `python -B src/economic_conditional_supplement.py run`. The runner refuses to overwrite its prospective protocol or output. To replay supplied seeds, work in a disposable copy, preserve the committed supplemental protocol and remove only the copied supplemental result before `run`. This reproduces the experiment; it does not create a new confirmation.

## Post-result model-upgrade bound

The request for an explicit cheaper-model/stronger-execution sensitivity arrived after the supplemental freeze. Its protocol remained untouched. `results/economics_upgrade_bound.json` instead records a clearly labeled **post-result analytic upper bound**: retain the original readiness and participation decisions and fixed costs, serve all 0.837 mean interested pools at $12,000, collect every fee, and incur zero repeat variable cost. Maximum mean revenue is $10,044 against $32,179.48 of startup, acquisition, business and insurance expense, leaving at least $22,135.48 mean loss.

That deliberately favorable bound covers more improvement than halving repeat inference tariffs or raising execution success to 0.98 while keeping buyer beliefs fixed. It is neither a new simulation nor measured Operational Alpha. Changed readiness, buyer beliefs, demand or product scope would require a fresh evaluation. At the original booking volume alone, halving inference tariffs can save at most $180.60 per world given the declared per-booking cost/retry bounds; the broader zero-variable-cost bound also allows every originally interested pool to be served.
