# Independent work-role review of the conditional economic experiment

**Reviewer role:** a separate AI-assistant review task, distinct from the study implementation task. This is an internal separation of work roles, not external institutional, human, financial, or audit independence.  
**Review date:** 27 October 2026.  
**Scope:** `study/economics.py`, `protocol/economic_protocol.json`, the measured technical input schema, and focused counterexample tests in `study/test_economics.py`. The reviewer did not edit the economic engine, choose confirmation seeds after seeing outcomes, run confirmation simulations, or conduct customer validation.

## Overall assessment

The commercial layer can support a **conditional comparison of declared operating heuristics** using executed numerical timings, assumed prices and demand, and simulated customer outcomes. It cannot establish earned revenue, actual willingness to pay, complete real-world operating costs, live frontier-model performance, globally optimal investment decisions, or a commercial feasibility crossing caused by specialist learning. Ordinary methods already qualify for the standard numerical offerings. The higher-value conclusion must therefore remain whether a limited measured speed advantage repays the additional assumed retention, sharing, development, and upkeep costs under the declared workload.

The model deliberately keeps a profitable Beta-only enterprise available. Both the ordinary and specialist arms can propose the same finite catalogue, quote the same price grid, reject unsuitable work, use ordinary fallback, and retain cash. Exact equality between the shared executable capability and an ordinary library with identical contents and permissions rules out a graph-specific advantage in this experiment. A private arm that repeats setup and upkeep across four recipients is a **forced duplication ablation**; it is not proof that every competent private enterprise must incur those duplicated costs.

Several substantive accounting and information-flow problems were found before economic confirmation and corrected by the implementation task. Their history is part of the evidence, rather than evidence that the first implementation was already correct.

## Causal and resource comparisons

The policies start with the same seed funds, common research-origin information, total processing capacity, candidate catalogue, commercial prior, ordinary tools, and common customer draws. Additional specialist investments consume each arm's own funds and capacity. The common setup and unpaid origin costs are not credited as Alpha. A separate growth sensitivity purchases capacity only from available surplus rather than interpreting a larger initial budget as compounding.

The shared/library alias is a useful negative control. A matching result means the experiment has measured reuse of an executable artifact under matching permissions; it has not demonstrated value from graph topology, ENS identity, live network authentication, or recipients independently acquiring the service provider's intelligence.

Private and shared forced treatments are informative cost ablations, not optimized enterprises. In particular, a competent private owner might operate one specialist service rather than independently reconstructing four copies. The interpretation of any private/shared difference must name the tested duplication and upkeep assumptions. Forced treatments also retain their specialist routing even when a later regime or hypothetical technology change makes it unattractive. The selective arm offers a bounded alternative; it is not an oracle proof of the best attainable strategy.

## Information boundaries

The tests compare identical observed prefixes followed by different future customer sequences. Prior actions, investment decisions, and balances must remain identical. The engine uses only acquired past lead counts in its demand-rate update. Prototype outcomes are shared latent draws revealed when an attempt is made; using common draws across arms is not an agent's permission to inspect them beforehand.

Before job commitment, policies may use the public offering specification and pre-existing measured timing distributions. They must not use an event's actual transfer success, hidden timing sample, quality outcome, collection default, or future lead suffix. Reserving capacity before realizing execution removes the original free timing lookahead. Future fees, collections, and refunds may be scheduled internally, but must not enter current available funds before their dates.

The commercial prior and buyer-choice functional form are supplied identically to all policies. They are assumed known for the experiment, not inferred from real customer observations. Only the lead-rate estimate updates online. Repeating simulation seeds therefore cannot validate the price distribution, human-service equivalence, quality/trust multipliers, prototype success probability, payment behavior, or acquisition cost.

## Accounting interpretation

For the declared horizon, the engine reports:

- Collections actually due and realized inside the simulated horizon.
- Accrued disjoint costs A, B, and C, with failed attempts and refunds retained.
- Collected-basis operating surplus equal to collections minus all accrued costs.
- Cash equal to seed capital plus collections minus paid costs.
- Unpaid obligations equal to accrued costs minus paid costs.
- Available funds equal to cash minus unpaid obligations minus reserves.

Consequently, available funds also equal seed capital plus the reported collected-basis surplus minus reserves. That identity prevents supplier credit from being treated as free operating capital. Seed funds, reserves, customer savings, token issuance, and speculative option value are not operating revenue.

This reporting convention is deliberately different from accrual accounting revenue: delivered but uncollected jobs do not increase operating surplus. Comparisons with long collection delays partly measure horizon liquidity and collection timing, not only ultimate unit profitability. Receivables must remain outside available funds. Refunds are a cost against the associated gross simulated collection, not a second subtraction from an already net revenue number.

Displaced production consumes opportunities and reduces realized production. The engine records displacement without adding another cash expense equal to hypothetical lost profit. This avoids double counting. Conversely, preparation time and money are different resources: recording both is appropriate when time reduces deliverable capacity and the money represents a separately declared expense.

The 1/3/6 accepted-output weights are declared workload weights, not measured buyer value or the estimated 2/4/8 human-hour equivalents. Total weighted output must be distinguished from productivity divided by a clearly named time or budget denominator. The implemented ratio divides by paid-job numerical plus assumed model-processing hours; it does not include all campaign preparation, unused reserved capacity or operating overhead time, so it is a job-processing ratio rather than a complete enterprise resource-productivity measure.

The costs are complete only **within the stated simulation boundary**. Hosting prices, token workloads, integration budgets, acquisition costs, support overhead, packaging durations, and correction budgets are assumptions. Routine human-review minutes are explicitly zero. Physical commissioning, professional sign-off, customer-specific model validation, legal/tax obligations outside the stated pretax boundary, and live service qualification are not established by this study. The paper should not describe assumed category coverage as measurement of all actual business costs.

## Decision quality and remaining scope limits

### Finite prices and assumed demand

Prices are selected from the same eight-point grid, using a common quadrature approximation of an assumed buyer-value distribution. Buyers can choose AI delivery, the assumed human equivalent, deferral, or no purchase. Neither a discount nor a low model bill guarantees a sale. The reference is estimated human hours multiplied by an assumed USD150/hour billing rate; no exact market quote for this numerical report has been established. Related consulting prices and wage sources are contextual evidence, not validation of that particular price or workload.

Expected compute expense is included in price choice. The calculation remains a myopic grid search with fixed quality and trust assumptions. It does not optimize dynamic prices, learning about willingness to pay, lead targeting, long-term reputation, queues, portfolio interactions, or every future obligation. The word “optimal” should be restricted to the explicit grid objective, not the enterprise as a whole.

### Selective investment is a declared heuristic

The final selective rule takes the larger of confirmation-family 32-case average timing savings and the fresh selection-service saving, then applies that favorable saving to all forecast orders and uses the largest quoted acceptance probability. This is deliberately optimistic for a compute-only repayment screen. It does not fully integrate the actual offered mix, shift-family frequency, transfer failure, capacity value, coming technology change, or all sequential option value. Rejection under that favorable compute forecast is informative within the stated mechanism, but is not a proof against an independently valuable new deadline or a general optimal policy. Decision logs now record a first investment only while the policy still uses Beta.

Commercial productization succeeds or fails through an assumed Bernoulli mechanism separate from the observed numerical prototype. This is a useful way to retain failed costs, but it is not empirical evidence of automated invention. The catalogue has only the predeclared audit, screening, and finite-grid selection products. Their economic activation is not unrestricted opportunity discovery or a new market. The unbuilt continuous-monitoring idea remains a rejected hypothesis.

### Timings and technical qualification

The standard five-second batch contract and two-second finite-grid prototype were already feasible with ordinary methods on the tested numerical families. The failed or timing-sensitive quarter-second interactive tier is not sold in the primary simulation. A different standard service cannot be counted as a specialist-induced crossing of that failed tier.

The economic model resamples a small set of executed technical timings. This creates workload variability but does not supply new numerical tasks, independent tail-latency evidence, a service-level reliability guarantee, or real market data. Circular sampling of 32 cases and reuse of a single measured whole-prototype duration are especially limited latency models. Package acceptance probability is an assumption distinct from residual certification. Full fallback is necessary for shifted tasks; a raw specialist-only failure must not be converted into success merely because its runtime is short.

### Maintenance, shutdown, and growth

Fixed operating overhead can remain an obligation even while voluntary work is declined. Voluntary acquisition and job commitments must respect available funds. The engine reserves the full declared kernel deadline together with assumed model latency and does not reclaim unused reservation. Identical throughput across arms under capacity pressure is therefore partly a consequence of this conservative capacity policy; it is not an independent demonstration that no latency advantage could ever increase capacity. Purchasing leads after capacity is exhausted is not a sophisticated acquisition policy; any such losses belong to the tested heuristic rather than to an inevitable business limitation. Capacity purchases in the growth sensitivity represent an assumed all-in expansion cost, not measured worker provisioning or a proven self-financing growth mechanism.

## Focused verification

`study/test_economics.py` uses small deterministic counterexamples rather than simulation-seed performance tests. It checks seed/revenue separation, accounting identities, defaults, full refunds, delayed invoices, future-prefix invariance, library/shared equivalence, the final prototype schema, maintenance charged once, commitment before timing realization, available-cost admission, and common scaling of measured complete fallback chains. Additional tests check affordable lead acquisition, prohibit future default outcomes from changing the as-of-horizon receivable balance, require numerical acceptance evidence, and reject recorded unsuccessful outputs rather than converting their short runtimes into saleable work. The same-investment test also checks that repeated reassessment does not log additional executed purchases.

The tests do not validate demand assumptions, buyer utility, the token budget, training costs, source-market transferability, physical model validity, or business profitability. They also do not constitute external financial assurance. Confirmation confidence intervals must be labeled Monte Carlo uncertainty conditional on all these assumptions.

## Post-confirmation inspection of the first economic version

This inspection read existing artifacts and did not rerun the simulation. The first version contains exactly **18,432 unique terminal records**, covering all 256 seeds 9000–9255, 12 scenarios and 6 policies, with no missing scenario/seed/policy cell. All 3,072 shared-work/library paired records match exactly on every recorded scalar except the policy label. Their 60-day base-scenario mean paths also match. Full individual paths are not retained for every history, so this check must not be described as direct inspection of every individual daily trajectory.

Selective assessment retained Beta in all 3,072 scenario/seed histories. Every policy had exactly the same order count, accepted output, weighted output and capacity-worker count as its paired Beta history. No forced specialist arm exceeded Beta surplus in any terminal history. Under the base assumptions, Beta mean collected-basis surplus was USD 50,908.25; shared work was lower by USD 60.997858, shared Gym by USD 85.999120, forced private duplication by USD 159.997858, and selective assessment by USD 8. These first-version outcomes are archived development evidence after the correction below, not the final confirmation result.

All terminal cash and available-funds identities matched exactly; the largest floating-point discrepancy in the accrued-cost/profit or obligation identities was USD 2.12e-8. Minimum terminal available funds was USD 1,255.74. The seed 9000 selective exemplar correctly excluded USD 3,135 of future scheduled collections from its USD 72,600 horizon collections. Its native aggregate replay produced 68 valid journal events, with 0 or 1 microUSD reconciliation differences, within the declared 27-microUSD aggregate-rounding tolerance. This validates arithmetic on supplied simulated attestations, not external payment truth.

The first low-willingness-to-pay scenario exposed a concrete limitation: every enterprise declined acquisition and made no sale, but still spent USD 110 on the two optional productizations. Beta lost USD 984.26 including those costs and its fixed operating commitment. The productization rule valued gross contribution without respecting the profitability of the acquisition channel. Calling this an adequate commercial stopping policy would overstate the evidence. In contrast, “no additional specialist adoption” remained true and must not be expanded into “no investment of any kind.”

Other first-version distinctions also matter. The low-demand Beta mean was positive, but 3 of 256 histories were loss-making. Slower collections reduced 60-day collected-basis surplus while leaving deliveries unchanged and increasing gross unpaid invoices; that is materially a horizon/collection effect. The growth scenario acquired exactly 2 capacity increments in every arm and history, so its much larger assumed-market revenue is ordinary scaling rather than an Alpha advantage. Monte Carlo confidence intervals describe uncertainty in the estimated simulation mean; they are not prediction intervals for individual businesses or confidence in market assumptions.
