                                        Abstract

       Can an automated enterprise repeatedly expand the set of work it can profitably deliver
     by learning specialists, transferring their capabilities and discovering adjacent offerings? We
      operationalize this question as a conjunction of usefulness, accuracy, reliability, deadline, customer-
       effort and complete-cost requirements. A faster component is insufficient: a specialist must change
     whether a fixed offering qualifies, or improve the economics of work already feasible.
      We execute a bounded study of stationary disturbance-energy analysis for synthetic dissipative
     dynamical systems. Six originating examples produce a covariance-weighted reduced basis; an
      optional Gym adds four development examples. On 64 protected confirmation cases, rank-48
     work and Gym bases each certify 62 cases, while ordinary adaptive Krylov certifies all 64. All
      learned bases fail certification on 32 stronger-coupling cases, demonstrating the need for refusal
     and fallback. A separate process reuses the digest-verified artifact without training snapshots.
    On a final fresh eight-case numerical workflow, ordinary adaptive delivery takes 0.4662 seconds,
      work-derived reuse 0.1728 seconds and Gym-derived reuse 0.4592 seconds. Every policy returns the
       direct oracle’s discrete-grid optimum. The ordinary baseline already meets the frozen two-second
      numerical contract; no uniquely enabled offering is demonstrated.
      A paired enterprise simulation connects these measured technical costs to explicit pricing, demand,
      capacity, failed commercialization, collections, obligations and optional learning assumptions.
      Existing AGI ALPHA accounting and trust code is exercised without alteration. The central
      contribution is an inspectable investment boundary and a defensible stopping policy: preserve
       profitable ordinary delivery when its economics hold, retain only advantages with a credible
     repayment mechanism, and withhold claims of new markets or sustained compounding until
      additional evidence exists.


1 What this study establishes


The motivating opportunity is substantial in principle: productive experience can leave behind a
reusable capability; that capability can reduce the difficulty of a subsequent mission; new missions
can generate further useful experience. Each arrow, however, has a separate empirical burden. This
study follows those arrows through executable numerical work and an explicit commercial model. It
deliberately permits a competent ordinary enterprise to succeed and to decline poor investments.

The principal measured result is improved delivery of an already feasible service. Work-derived reuse
is approximately 2.70 times faster than the adaptive ordinary comparator on the final fresh design-
selection batch. That ratio is real within the recorded machine, source and synthetic problem family.
Its absolute saving is 0.2934 seconds for an eight-case service. Neither the ratio nor its absolute
value establishes customer willingness to pay, physical-model adequacy or a new commercially feasible
offering.

The optional Gym campaign changes the retained artifact but does not improve protected acceptance
counts. On the downstream selection problem it requires additional direct-solver refinements, removing
most of the work basis’s advantage. This is a useful example of negative transfer at the service
level: a representation with favorable primitive residual behavior can impose more coordination and
verification work on the next task.

2 A productive frontier with meaningful boundaries

Let an offering o specify a beneficiary, useful outcome, deliverable, input contract, required quality qo,
reliability ro, deadline do, maximum customer effort eo, and price po. For policy π, environment z and
workload N, define qualification as

         F(o, π, z, N) = 1{Uo, Qπ,o ≥qo, Rπ,o ≥ro, Tπ,o ≤do, Eπ,o ≤eo, Ππ,o(N) ≥0}.        (1)

Uo represents a useful outcome for the stated beneficiary. The profit term includes a defensible share
of development, validation and lifetime obligations. Each predicate requires evidence; an internal
model score cannot substitute for it. The productive frontier is the set of offerings for which these
joint requirements hold under the declared conditions.

We distinguish four possible outcomes. Improved delivery increases quality or surplus inside an existing
feasible set. Economic enablement makes technically possible work affordable or dependable enough
to offer. Compositional enablement joins capabilities whose combined operation crosses a pre-existing
requirement. Opportunity discovery identifies and validates a useful offering that was not merely
named in advance. These categories are cumulative evidentiary claims, not interchangeable marketing
descriptions.

The comparison is always against the best credible alternative within the declared search. A dense
solve is an important correctness reference, but an enterprise with ordinary adaptive methods is the
relevant commercial control. We therefore report dense-solver threshold misses without interpreting
them as a specialist-created frontier when an ordinary adaptive method already qualifies.

There is also a distinction between a finite research gate and an operational reliability claim. A 62/64
pass rate exceeds a 95% empirical threshold. It does not establish a population reliability of at least
95%. Similarly, two measured batches cannot establish a dependable latency service-level agreement.
The full commercial frontier remains conditional on untested customer effort, physical input adequacy
and market behavior even when the numerical research gate passes.

3.1 The first deliverable

The proposed first service is an offline, reproducible disturbance-energy analysis of already validated
linear models. A customer supplies rights-cleared matrices, units, disturbance assumptions and a
precise question. The output includes validated inputs, a covariance or reproducible representation, an
energy estimate, an explicit residual-derived numerical error bound, refusal messages where assumptions
fail, and a machine-readable evidence package. This is a scientific-computation deliverable. It does not
certify a physical installation, authorize a controller or replace professional engineering obligations.

The selected numerical computation has an established role. Continuous-time Lyapunov equations
underlie stationary covariance and H2 disturbance-response calculations. Existing software already
includes dense and large-scale low-rank methods; M.E.S.S. and integrated Krylov–ADI work are
relevant acquisition alternatives, not missing capabilities that an AI enterprise may assume away
[1, 2, 3, 4].

Two adjacent offerings are considered. A standard batch report compares 32 model variants. A
finite-grid design report selects added damping while preserving the disturbance-energy objective and
numerical requirements. The frozen interactive 0.25-second tier is assessed separately and remains
unqualified as a reliable service. The commercial simulation uses the distinct standard five-second
batch contract already defined in Experiment A; it does not relabel the interactive failure as success.

3.2 What the market evidence can support

No exact matched market quote, customer interview, signed purchase commitment or actual sale was
established for this narrow offline covariance report. Related control consulting is advertised, and
official wage data provide a labor-cost reference, but neither is a price for the tested synthetic artifact.
The BLS source reports electrical-engineering wages; a conversion from annual wages to hourly wages
and then to a consultancy billing rate requires explicit additional assumptions [5, 6].

The economic model uses estimated equivalent human effort of two, four and eight hours at an
assumed USD150 per hour, producing reference anchors of USD300, USD600 and USD1,200. These
are transparent scope-and-rate estimates. They are not documented market prices. Buyer value, trust,
acquisition cost and workload are separate hypotheses. The finite price search may select a substantial
discount, but neither the discount nor a low model bill guarantees a purchase.

A continuous-monitoring service is considered as a genuinely different buyer-value hypothesis without
inventing a human equivalent. It is declined because streaming assurance, a working prototype, demand
evidence and an operational authority boundary are absent. The cost of assessment is retained in the
declared research/production setup budget. A new name does not qualify as a new market.

5 Executed technical experiment


5.1 A useful quantity and a checkable bound

For a real, stable dynamical model with stochastic input, the stationary covariance X satisfies

                     AX + XA⊤+ BB⊤= 0.                                    (2)

The synthetic family has 192 states and A = −D + K, with positive diagonal damping D and
skew-symmetric coupling K. Two localized columns of B describe disturbance channels. Parameters
vary damping, coupling, heterogeneity and input scale. All values are nondimensional research inputs;
no physical plant was fitted.

For dx = Ax dt + B dW with independent unit-intensity white-noise channels, zero direct feedthrough
and C = I, tr(X) is the stationary expected squared state norm and the squared H2 norm. It is not
the H2 norm itself; different disturbance normalization or output weights change the quantity being
sold.

Let sym(A) ⪯−αI for a verified α > 0. For an approximate covariance bX, form the full residual
                      R = AbX + bXA⊤+ BB⊤.                                    (3)

Otherwise the solver refuses a finite relative certificate. For output matrix C, the corresponding energy
error is bounded by ∥C⊤C∥F F. We use C = I and the trace objective. This is a standard consequence

The implementation validates matrix dimensions, finite values, covariance symmetry and the dissi-

evaluated in floating point; no interval-arithmetic enclosure of rounding error is claimed. It certifies
numerical solution error under stated matrix assumptions, not model-form error or physical robustness.


5.2 What is learned

Six originating solutions form an aggregate covariance. Its leading eigenvectors define an orthonormal
reduced state basis of rank 24 or 48. Given basis V , a new query solves

                (V ⊤AV )Y + Y (V ⊤AV )⊤+ (V ⊤B)(V ⊤B)⊤= 0,     bX = V Y V ⊤.               (6)

The resulting array is a data-dependent executable artifact: changing the examples changes the
representation. This is actual numerical learning through work examples. It is not frontier-model
weight training or a claim of invented POD theory.

Optional Gym practice evaluates the rank-48 work basis on 20 development cases, selects the four
largest residual certificates, pays for four full solutions and refits at the same rank. Equal rank isolates
the effect of additional examples from simply allocating more representation capacity. Rank 24 is a
separate compression ablation. No protected confirmation case is used to select training examples.

The ordinary alternatives are SciPy’s Bartels–Stewart solve, adaptive block Krylov with reorthog-
onalization and residual stopping, and a fixed central-instance rank-48 Krylov basis. The adaptive
method builds a query-specific basis and checks its certificate every eight dimensions up to rank 96.
The fixed basis is an ablation, not a representation of every conventional library. Optimally configured
rational/extended Krylov or production M.E.S.S. methods could be stronger still; their availability
makes a broad superiority claim less defensible.


5.3  Freeze, splits and corrections

The initial protocol fixes six origin examples, 20 development cases, 64 protected confirmation cases,
32 shifted cases and 32 early prototype cases. Seeds, parameter boxes, rank, learning budget, error
requirements and deadlines are serialized before confirmation. The original implementation and
all arrays remain byte-identical in an archive. The protected quality gate requires a relative trace
certificate at most 1%, an ex-post trace error at most 1%, and an ex-post Frobenius error at most 2%;
the empirical pass threshold is 95%. Standard and interactive 32-case deadlines are respectively five
seconds and 0.25 seconds.

Initial code trusted a caller-provided damping bound and marked the direct reference accepted without
applying the common certificate gate. Review corrected both boundary defects. Scoped revalidation
preserved every initial acceptance count and all four basis hashes. These revalidated cases are explicitly
exposed cases, not a second fresh confirmation. The final prototype uses a new seed after freezing
both corrected implementations and the unchanged proposal.

Each measured method includes matrix assembly, its numerical solve and full residual certification.
Exact-reference error calculations are ex-post research audits and are excluded from serving time, while
their cost belongs in the study’s validation budget. Production fallback receives only the deployable
certificate, not the hidden oracle. Inspection confirmed that the deployable certificate and the initial
audit gate made the same decision on every initial row.

8 Transformational intelligence as a testable intervention


The transformation considered here changes the representation and work allocation while preserving
the mission. The obstacle is repeated full-state covariance computation. The proposed change is to
reuse an empirically learned subspace, certify the result with an independent residual, and invoke
ordinary computation when the certificate or ranking is inconclusive. The decisive test is a fresh
complete service against an unrestricted ordinary alternative.

Three lessons follow. First, the representation really transfers: a separate process loads a digest-verified
basis and computes fresh outputs without the training corpus. Second, the learned representation
changes which internal computation is needed, but not which tested offering is available to a competent
ordinary enterprise. Third, more practice is not necessarily better preparation for the next mission.
Ranking uncertainty, rather than covariance approximation alone, determines downstream refinement
cost.

The experience can inform a better decision: retain the work basis as a research candidate, avoid
automatic Gym expenditure, require a scoped residual check, and look for a task whose economically
important bottleneck is actually changed. This edition does not measure a general improvement in an
agent’s opportunity judgment. Its economic selector is a finite, inspectable decision rule using the
measured evidence. A live learned opportunity-selection policy requires a separate held-out sequence
of proposals and outcomes.

9 Experiment C: complete conditional enterprise economics


9.1 What is observed, sourced and assumed

The numerical artifacts, accept/refuse outcomes, query timings, fallback calls and transfer tests are
observed executions on synthetic systems. Current model identities and unit prices are sourced from
official documentation. Token workloads, automated preparation latencies, customer demand, trust,
workload, pricing references, acquisition costs and operational expenses are explicit assumptions. Buyer
choices, collections and commercial failures are simulated. Confidence intervals for the simulated
mean measure Monte Carlo estimation uncertainty under those assumptions; they do not validate the
assumptions.

The model covers 60 operating days. Every matched policy receives the same USD2,500 seed capital,
total daily processing capacity, ordinary tools, customer draws, candidate catalogue and price grid.
The seed is capital, never revenue. Origin examples in this edition are unpaid research inputs: their
compute and delivery-equivalent model budget are charged once in C, with zero collections. In a
genuinely paid-origin implementation, the learning necessary for accepted delivery would belong in A
and would not receive another research bill.

Buyers may choose AI delivery, human delivery, deferral or no purchase.  Utilities depend on an
explicitly assumed value distribution, trust-adjusted usefulness and price. The enterprise evaluates
eight prices relative to the estimated human-service anchor; it can refuse incompatible inputs, negative
expected contribution, insufficient funds or unavailable reserved capacity. It reserves capacity before
realizing execution time or failure, preventing perfect foresight about which jobs will be cheap. Failed
acceptance, refunds, collection delays and defaults remain in the records.

9.2 Optional actions and comparable alternatives

The competent ordinary baseline may pursue the same standard batch and finite-grid selection offerings.
It can prototype, price, decline work and hold cash. Private-work, shared-work and shared-Gym
treatments force the specified capability investment to isolate its consequences. Private duplication is
an explicit ablation with repeated integration/assessment, not a universal claim that every private
specialist architecture must pay four times as much. An ordinary-library arm has identical contents,
permissions and costs to shared work; no graph-specific benefit is assumed.

The selective policy pays for assessment and compares retention, Gym and holding cash. Its capability
screen uses an optimistic forecast of the largest measured per-order compute saving, observed demand
information and remaining horizon. Future upkeep affects valuation but is charged only when incurred.
This is a bounded screening rule, not a global solution of enterprise pricing, scheduling and research.
Its optimism makes rejection informative when even the favorable repayment estimate is too small.

Prototype commercialization has an assumed success probability of 0.85, separate from numerical
correctness. Each candidate gets one attempt. Failed prototypes keep their charges and consume
preparation capacity. Integration, Gym and common production setup also displace processing capacity.
Opportunity cost appears as displaced orders and paired enterprise outcomes; it is not subtracted a
second time as a cash expense. A separate growth arm may purchase extra capacity from available
operating surplus, with the same option for every policy. Scale growth by itself is not evidence of
learning-driven compounding.

Preparation is a finite queue of committed work. Unfinished seconds carry into subsequent days.
Setup, specialist access and offering readiness activate only after their required preparation completes;
a successful prototype outcome is not available to the decision policy in advance of completion. Cash
is charged once when committed. Daily capacity is explicitly partitioned into preparation consumed,
customer-job reservations and unused capacity, with any unfinished preparation reported at the horizon.
A new lead is not purchased when no ready offering fits the remaining capacity. Unsupported realized
job overruns cause the bounded simulator to refuse rather than silently create processing time.

9.4 A ledger that does not invent revenue

Costs are disjoint: A contains modeled customer delivery, intake, corrections, refunds and allocated
operations; B contains additional assessment, retention, admission, transfer preparation and upkeep;
C contains unpaid origin research, optional Gym, productization and common setup. The native
replay assigns unique identities to 27 bucket/category aggregates; original activity records remain in
the exemplar. The model assumes USD8 per acquired qualified lead and USD12 per operating day
for allocated monitoring, support and administration. These amounts are hypotheses, not observed
invoices. There are no arbitrary routine human-review minutes; the proposed product’s scope excludes
physical commissioning and professional sign-off.

Let K be seed capital, Rt actual simulated collections received by t, Ct accrued modeled costs, Pt
paid modeled costs, and Zt reserves. The reported quantities are

                   Πt = Rt −Ct,               Casht = K + Rt −Pt,                        (8)
             Obligationst = Ct −Pt,             Availablet = Casht −Obligationst −Zt.          (9)

This is a conservative collected-basis operating surplus, not an assertion of audited accrual-accounting
profit. Gross unpaid invoices are reported separately. Refunds are explicit costs and are paid immedi-
ately when a failed order’s collection arrives. Customer savings, token issuance, speculative opportunity
value and uncollected invoices are not earned revenue. Applicable $AGIALPHA mechanisms are
outside this operating ledger.

The native ledger conformance fixture uses deliberately fictional amounts and is kept separate from
the enterprise results. Unknown costs remain unknown, and native completeness gates prevent a
missing cost from becoming zero. An aggregate replay of a recorded enterprise history checks that
model totals and the unchanged native ledger agree. This proves accounting behavior for supplied
attestations, not the truth of customer or provider payments.

11 Operator decisions: offer, qualify, price and stop


Offer first. Begin with the standard offline numerical audit of already validated, rights-cleared
dissipative models. Require a machine-readable input contract and a named useful decision. Produce
reproducible outputs and numerical-error evidence.  Refuse unsupported dynamics, absent units,
unknown rights or requests for physical safety sign-off. The first live pilot should determine whether
this narrowly scoped deliverable saves a real team’s work.

Price after scope qualification. Use estimated human-equivalent effort as a transparent reference
where the scope matches, then test willingness to pay. In the central assumed market, the finite search
selects USD165, USD330 and USD660 for the audit, standard batch and finite-grid service. Those are
scenario outputs, not validated market-clearing prices or quotes to a customer. Record acquisition,
preparation, corrections, support, obligations and collections before calling an offering profitable.

Choose the capability with a repayment mechanism. The work basis is the better measured
candidate.  It should not receive automatic production admission or extra Gym funding. For the
tested service, its compute saving is too small to pay normal additional integration and upkeep at
the modeled workload. Retain it as inspectable research evidence, and reconsider only when an
independent contract, larger measured workload or different expensive bottleneck provides a credible
mechanism.

Treat the next offering as an experiment. The finite-grid damping-selection prototype works and
transfers, but ordinary tools already deliver it. Its commercialization may still be worthwhile as an
ordinary service. That would validate product development, not specialist-created frontier expansion.
The continuous-monitoring proposal remains rejected until its missing prototype, model assurance and
buyer evidence are supplied.

Stop or redirect. A failed certificate requires ordinary fallback or refusal. Scope shift should bypass
an unsuitable basis. Gym should stop when fresh service-level benefit is absent. Productization should
stop when expected contribution does not cover its cost, and acquisition should stop when the assumed
market cannot cover acquisition and intake. Available funds, not gross receipts or speculative option
value, constrain additional commitments.

12  Limitations and the next decisive experiment


The result has a narrow technical domain. The matrices are synthetic and dissipative by construction;
dimensionality, topology, input locations and parameter ranges are limited. Learning examples and
confirmation instances come from a known generator. The shifted family tests one failure mode, not
all unfamiliar systems. No physical model, laboratory experiment, industrial controller or human
buyer was validated.

The timing sample is small, one host is used, execution order is not a full randomized crossover,
and complete interactive reliability  is not established.  Exact source identity and separate ini-
tial/revalidated/fresh datasets preserve what was observed, but do not create stronger generalization.
The fresh selection cohort contains only eight systems. Measured numerical bounds do not provide
interval-certified floating-point proofs.

The opportunity search is finite and researcher-specified. It does not establish open-ended autonomous
invention or independent demand discovery. Neither does the shared-artifact experiment establish that
every recipient acquires a specialist’s general intelligence. A numerical basis is a compatible reusable
component; a service would instead provide bounded access to computation. Graph topology is not
tested as a source of advantage.

Commercial results are entirely conditional. Token counts, costs, buyer utilities, workload, packaging
risk and capacity are assumed. The model omits some possible strategies and cannot be called a
globally optimal enterprise controller. It assumes a fixed operating horizon with allocated overhead;
shutting down the enterprise or renegotiating obligations may be a better alternative in an adverse
market. No simulation confidence interval validates a price anchor or demand curve. No actual
collections were earned by this study.

The next decisive experiment should begin with a real beneficiary and a demanding fixed contract for
which the strongest ordinary approach is close to an economically important limit. It should include
paid or otherwise independently validated demand, rights-cleared originating experience, a properly
costed specialist campaign, a fresh recipient, and a next-offering comparison that permits ordinary
acquisition alternatives. Its strongest conclusion might again be to decline training. That decision is
productive when it preserves resources for a better-supported opportunity.
