# Commercial experiment: useful construction does not yet fund an enterprise

**Decision: do not launch this offering under the tested demand and operating assumptions.**

This experiment executes a stateful commercial simulator. The code and accounting executed; all organizations, preferences, prices, commitments, transactions, revenues and profits are synthetic. Actual campaign customers and receipts are zero. Complete actual campaign costs and actual profit are unknown. No sales, outreach, contract, purchase, financial transfer or external model call was performed by this economic study.

## The tested offering

The proposed deliverable is an exact, bounded contingency-fiber instrument: a reusable generator for integer tables with stated margins, cell limits and structural zeros, plus counting and rank/unrank operations. Technical scope and novelty are assessed elsewhere. The economic model does not grant a novelty premium or convert synthetic demand into evidence of willingness to pay.

Four original commercial policies were tested: USD149 self-service, USD449 qualified instrument, USD349 renewable monthly subscription for one or two requested outcomes, and USD449 industrial targeting. A zero-investment stop policy is always available. Subscription revenue is earned only when a buyer has a need, chooses the offer, and accepts delivery; dormant subscriptions are not assumed to keep paying. Delivered artifacts are portable, with the modeled support/complaint window and no indefinite hosting promise.

After the initial experiment rejected all launch policies, a fifth commercial policy was added: **a six-month commitment gate at USD449**. This is an actual policy revision followed by fresh evaluation. It does not increase buyer willingness to pay or remove engineering, review, support, maintenance or acquisition costs.

## Evidence and assumptions

The population contains 240 explicitly synthetic organizations: 45% academic methods labs, 35% industrial validation teams, 20% data generation studios. IDs such as `SYN-LAB-001` are invented simulation entities, not real prospects. The population is neither a lead list nor a market-size estimate. Budgets, outcome values, monthly need, exact-scope fit, initial awareness, conversion, quality acceptance, churn and supplier failure rates are authored assumptions.

Source anchors are narrow. The directly opened BLS page on 2026-10-28 reports May2025 median wages of USD135,980 for software developers and USD104,300 for quality-assurance analysts/testers. ## Complete stated operating costs

Opening unrestricted equity is **USD30,000**. The baseline has no credit facility and no assumed outside financing. A bounded credit mechanism exists in the simulator for explicit alternative configurations; no credit is used in the main results and interest is therefore zero.

Launch consumes **USD9,600 cash**:80 engineering replacement hours atUSD95, USD800 administration, and USD1,200 equipment. Engineering and administration are expensed; equipment is depreciated over24months. These are replacement-cost assumptions for the proposed construction/launch, not measured current or historical research costs. A separate40/80/160-hour sensitivity is included.

Monthly core costs are **USD2,510**:18engineering maintenance hours, six administration hours, four sales hours, USD50 hosting and USD100 accounting/insurance. Policy marketing adds USD250–450 per month. Annual requalification costs eight engineering hours. The qualified instrument charges36minutes expert review plus15minutes support per requested outcome, with retries, storage, payment processing, rejected deliveries, later refunds and invoice defaults counted. Small changes in compute cost cannot compensate for this labor and acquisition burden.

The model charges closeout administration while invoices or liabilities remain and for the initial wind-down interval; it runs six additional settlement months after the24-month operating window. The tax assumption is a20% scalar charge on positive cumulative pretax income at settlement checkpoints, with limited loss-offset treatment. It is not a jurisdiction-specific tax model. No tax is due in the loss-producing main histories.

Money is recorded in integer cents. Costs smaller than half a cent may round to zero per attempted delivery; the maximum rounding discrepancy is bounded by0.5c per affected entry. At the executed low order volumes this cannot change the commercial verdict. Costs, acceptance and overhead remain estimates: this is full accounting of the model's declared costs, not a measured complete real-world bill.

## Strategic state and participant choice

Each buyer has persistent resources, values and history. Monthly needs and suitability vary. The buyer compares our expected result value minus price/integration cost against DIY/open software, a competing supplier and abstention. Offers above willingness to pay are refused; a bounded discount may be negotiated. Buyers can churn, decline out-of-scope work, reject delivery and receive refunds. Repeated purchases reduce assumed integration costs, while delivery failure damages trust.

The competitor begins with a lower price, broader distribution and explicit partial coverage. It learns over time. When our preceding share of eligible contested offers exceeds25%, it can lower price, expand coverage and improve quality. This share is a synthetic observability assumption about contested offers, not an observed total-market share. The frozen base worlds actually exercise competitor responses; they are not merely hypothetical branches left unused.

Supplier expert-review capacity is finite. The supplier can inflate prices and lose capacity. The disruption scenario includes complete monthly outages. Orders beyond review capacity or available cash are refused. Retained capabilities incur maintenance and annual requalification. No cash or revenue is assigned to untested successor transfer; transfer costs are outside this enterprise experiment and must be charged in any separate successor business case.

Cash and earnings are separate. A customer advance creates refundable custody. It cannot fund operating expenses. Acceptance releases custody into earned revenue and creates any remaining receivable. Invoices can be delayed or default; refunds reverse earned revenue and return only the money actually owed. An unpaid obligation stays payable and triggers an insolvency flag; the bank is never allowed to go negative. A cash-preserving rule stops intake before exhausting the reserve. Zero insolvency in a stopped company does not mean sustainable operations.

3. The commitment-gated revision used new training seeds141000–141063. Source/config were frozen before evaluating **3000000–3000255**, with256worlds for each of four reported policies in each of seven scenarios: **7,168worlds**. The compared policies are stop, original subscription349, original qualified449, and revised gate449. No confirmation outcome was used to alter the revision.

The precommitted selection rule admits only policies with positive mean after-tax full-project profit, at most25% losing training worlds and at most5% insolvency. Among eligible policies it maximizes tenth-percentile profit, with explicit tie breaks. If none qualify, it selects stop. A diagnostic active policy is evaluated regardless, so choosing stop cannot hide a failing business.

### The revision

The six-month gate charges **USD475 per month** at base rates for marketing, search/triage labor and administration. Conditional synthetic orders expire after three months and may withdraw10% per month. Buyer utility includes a delay penalty. No money is received for these expressions of commitment.

Launch requires the estimated contribution from the current cohort to cover USD9,600 startup, three months of complete fixed/marketing costs, and the USD2,000 reserve. At the stated assumptions this requires **57 simultaneous qualifying commitments**. The unchangedUSD30,000 equity must separately fund construction: customer custody is not spendable. If the gate triggers, committed requests still face capacity, delivery acceptance, refunds and subsequent competition. If it fails, search and closeout costs remain real expenses within the simulation.

In the256base confirmation worlds, the largest active commitment cohort was11; the mean peak was4.32. The gate did not launch in any world. It spentUSD2,850 searching andUSD200 closing out. This improves capital preservation, not enterprise profitability. Conditional-on-launch profit is **null**, not zero, because there were no launches.

## What would need to change?

The separate price–volume arithmetic holds the qualified product's cost structure fixed. It is not a claim that the indicated volume can be purchased at that price. With the stated startup replacement, two annual requalifications and closeout allocated over24months, full monthly burden is aboutUSD3,431.67. The analytic threshold is approximately:

| Nominal instrument price | Expected contribution per net retained order | Required net retained orders/month |
|---|---:|---:|
| USD149 | USD58.48 | 59 |
| USD249 | USD154.26 | 23 |
| USD349 | USD250.03 | 14 |
| USD449 | USD345.81 | 10 |

Nominal review capacity is about47orders/month before outages. This table varies the **qualified instrument** price; it does not use the different self-service policy's lighter review assumption. AtUSD449 and ten retained orders/month, the static grid produces only aboutUSD634 pretax project profit over24months, before strategic erosion and stochastic volume. The full dynamic campaign fails to obtain this volume; acquisition, competition, scope and price remain joint constraints. The table is a threshold for future evidence, not demand evidence.

The independent40/80/160replacement-hour sensitivity uses32new worlds per policy/cell, seeds4000000–4000031, and changes no buyer values. All original active policies still lose; the revised gate still refuses launch. See `economics/replacement_cost_sensitivity.json`. A separate6000-fully-aware-organization control exercises the positive-profit and cohort-launch code branches; it is an **arithmetic stress fixture only**, not a market scenario, lead pool or commercialization result.

## Audits and preserved corrections

The money ledger enforces the balance-sheet identity after every event and prevents spending customer custody. A separately written Decimal audit reads the exported monthly CSV and reconstructs balances and cumulative equity/profit. It also passes under optimized Python, so audit checks do not disappear under`-O`.

The original audit counter incorrectly counted a prior-month continuity check for month0. The underlying equalities and numerical outcomes were correct. Original source/results remain immutable. `economics/AUDIT_COUNT_CORRECTION.json` records the original and corrected counts, source hashes and an AST check showing that only the audit counter changed after confirmation. **Correct performed checks:1,756,160for v1 and3,512,320for v2**. Do not cite the larger original counters as executed checks. The corrected v2 receipt is `economics/corrected_decimal_audit_v2.json`.

The unchanged canonical `flywheel_arrangements.assess_arrangement` independently reproduces terminal cash and cash contribution for all21non-stop reference paths. That adapter groups cash flows by month and checks only the aggregate cash bridge; it does not establish accrual classification, intramonth liquidity, demand or native commercial admission. It grants no authority and records no actual collections. The detailed new ledger supplies the event-level custody/accrual checks.

Scoped Ruff and strict Mypy pass on the new simulator. Black is unavailable; Ruff formatter was used. Full repository release qualification is separate and is not inferred from these research checks.

## Reproduce

From the repository root, choose an output directory that does not exist:

```bash
python studies/agi-alpha-profitable-possibility-v1/economy.py --config studies/agi-alpha-profitable-possibility-v1/economics_config_revision.json --output /tmp/agi-alpha-economy-reproduction
python studies/agi-alpha-profitable-possibility-v1/economy.py --audit /tmp/agi-alpha-economy-reproduction/monthly_ledger.csv
PYTHONPATH=. python studies/agi-alpha-profitable-possibility-v1/economy.py --native-audit /tmp/agi-alpha-economy-reproduction
python studies/agi-alpha-profitable-possibility-v1/economy.py --price-volume
```

The simulator, Decimal audit and analytic grid use standard Python only. The optional native check requires this repository's runtime dependencies. The frozen original source and input are also inside each experiment directory. Re-running current corrected source reproduces numerical world/monthly results; the audit count and source hash differ from the preserved original for the documented counter-only correction, and the freeze timestamp naturally differs. Output directories are never overwritten.

Raw artifacts include selection records, all world summaries, all monthly ledgers, one complete event/decision reference path per non-stop policy/scenario, byte manifests, exact frozen configs/source and exposed development results. The findings establish a rejected commercial design and a better loss-limiting search policy. They do not establish a profitable enterprise, realized sales or a financeable successor.
