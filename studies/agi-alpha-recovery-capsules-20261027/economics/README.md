# Recovery Capsule: complete conditional economics

Run `python economics/run_economics.py` from the study directory, or run the script by its absolute path. Python 3.10+ and its standard library are sufficient. No network, credentials, paid calls or third-party package is needed. `inputs.json` is the frozen scenario source; each result records its SHA-256. Outputs include annual accounting, daily cash ledgers through warranty runoff, price/volume/acceptance grids, payment arrangements, acquisition and model-cost sensitivities, a conventional shared-arrangement comparison and buyer-participation thresholds.

## Main result and evidence boundary

At **$900 per finally accepted capsule**, the central scenario has 40 qualified inquiries per month, 20% purchase conversion, eight attempted orders, and 85% final acceptance after correction and refund. Over twelve months it produces expected accepted revenue of **$73,440**, direct contribution of **$63,109.01**, operating profit of **$40,309.01**, and full modeled project return of **$36,309.01** after $4,000 additional setup. These are conditional modeled values. Expected counts can be fractional. The current study has **zero buyers and $0 actual collections**; actual complete study cost is unknown because platform billing and token usage are not fully exposed.

The first useful technical capsules and their independent checks are separate evidence. They do not estimate paid demand, 85% customer acceptance, acquisition cost or a two-business-day service level. The handbook examples have a free newer edition and are unsuitable evidence of willingness to pay for recovery. Paid targeting is conditional on a customer-specific analysis lacking an equivalent free substitute.

The downside combines three attempts per month, 50% final acceptance, a $600 price, heavier model/retry consumption and $8,200 setup. It loses **$28,943.02** over the full modeled project. Low uptake loses **$13,855.69**; zero buyers loses **$14,800** if the service keeps operating for twelve months. A funded campaign of forty qualified inquiries monthly with zero conversions instead loses **$26,800**, including $12,000 unsuccessful acquisition. These losses remain in the results. The favorable case is a sensitivity, not a sales forecast.

## Scope-matched human reference

The same notebook/CLI, environment, hashes, original/after evidence, independent checks, diff, README and correction responsibility are assigned estimated low/central/high totals of 4/8/16 human hours. Rates are $84/$149/$249 per hour, giving **$336/$1,192/$3,984**. The central eight hours divide into one hour triage, two repair, two independent verification, one packaging, one expected support and one coordination/handoff. The $3,984 high value uses ParaTools' unloaded rate and is a resource reference, not its loaded selling-price reference. Sixteen hours at its published **$412.97 loaded rate is $6,607.52**, separately reported. Public provider rates are anchors, not quotes for this exact offer; hour estimates are not measured human trials. Source details and caveats are in `inputs.json`.

At $900, the offer is 24.5% below the central human resource-price reference, but substantially above the low reference. Easy notebooks and free maintained alternatives can defeat the commercial case. A $600 scenario is approximately half the central reference and still returns **$12,563.98** under unchanged central delivery and demand assumptions. $1,200 is only a higher-price sensitivity: faster service or higher willingness to pay has not been demonstrated.

## Complete cost, charged once

The central baseline API envelope is 2 million aggregate input tokens and 400,000 aggregate output tokens across the full multi-call workflow. At documented standard short-context GPT-6 Astra rates this is $40. All input is uncached; output includes reasoning. Outcome-dependent correction/rejection multipliers produce $53.80 expected model cost per attempted job. This is an explicit deployment-budget assumption, not token metering from the present session.

Expected tools, artifact retention, human support and genuine exception resolution bring direct cost to **$84.3825 per attempted job**, including final rejections. Clean jobs require no imposed manual quality review. Expected human support is 0.1925 hours per attempt; exceptions add 0.11 hours. Qualified-lead cost is $25, including 0.1 human hour valued at $95 and $15.50 in modeled channel cost. At 20% conversion this is $125 per attempted order, or $147.06 per final accepted order. No separate acquisition bill is added elsewhere.

Monthly fixed expense is $900: four maintenance hours and four administration hours at $95, $60 hosting/software and $80 professional business services. Human attention totals **14.42 hours monthly** at eight orders, including acquisition, support and exceptions. The $95 labor opportunity-cost estimate is based on published BLS wage/benefit data, not a contracted rate. A fractional shared operation is assumed; a full dedicated salary is not hidden outside this model.

Setup is 40 additional hours at $95 plus $200 tooling. Delivery work is charged to its job once. There is no extra invented bill for knowledge learned on that job, and no retained-skill or Gym investment. The full modeled commercial project includes this setup; it is not an actual-cost statement for the present research session.

## Acceptance, refunds and participation

Central outcome shares are 60% clean acceptance, 25% corrected acceptance, 12% rejection before payment and 3% later full refund. Thus initial collection occurs for 88%, but retained accepted revenue for 85%. Original processing fees on refunded payments remain a cost. The same case type jointly changes cost, correction, support and acceptance. Parameter values are uncalibrated commercial assumptions; technical test pass counts are never used as customer-acceptance probabilities.

At the central $1,192 human reference and $900 capsule price, a buyer who uses human fallback after capsule rejection has expected gross savings of `0.85 × (1192 − 900) = $248.20`. Incremental setup, delay, verification and trust costs must fit within that amount. This is a participation threshold, not a demand estimate. The low human reference gives a negative gap, so price alone offers that buyer no reason to switch. `participation_thresholds.csv` preserves all price/reference combinations.

## Break-even and capacity

At central cost and quality, the operating break-even price is **$390.73** and the full-project price is **$441.26** at eight attempts per month. At $900 with acquisition proportional to orders, operating/full-project break-even is **1.69/2.32 attempted orders per month**, or **1.44/1.97 final accepted orders**. Those are steady expected rates, not integer monthly commitments. If a fixed campaign of 40 inquiries is purchased regardless of conversion, use the separate conversion thresholds in `summary.json`; do not combine cheap proportional acquisition with low conversion from the same unchanged campaign.

The acceptance threshold recomputes rejected-job work and correction cost when the mix changes: 40.58% operating and 44.99% full-project at the central volume/price. The nominal capacity envelope is 39.88 attempts monthly from 40 available human hours and 160 agent hours; this is proposed scheduling capacity, not verified API throughput or SLA. All selected 3–15 order scenarios fit this envelope. Actual account rate limits and task-duration distribution remain unmeasured.

## Cash and financing

The default contract collects after initial acceptance. All open-warranty receipts remain restricted until the 14-day window closes. Development, acquisition, execution, fees and support are paid; reserves are not a second expense. Expected-case required opening cash is **$6,760.92**. A deterministic first cohort in which all eight initially paid orders are refunded requires **$10,298.12**, assuming the following eleven months recover to central conditions. Recovery is conditional. These are modeled equity requirements, not a financing commitment or a probabilistic solvency guarantee.

The fully restricted 50% deposit alternative does not fund delivery. Its small timing difference comes from when payment fees and earned balances occur; refund liabilities remain fully covered. Collection only after the warranty window avoids fees on later-refunded initial payments but increases collection delay. All alternatives retain the same capsule and final-refund responsibility. No arbitrary seed-capital ceiling is used to erase an otherwise profitable case.

## Complementarity and the correction retained

`coordination_comparison.csv` compares human and frontier-assisted delivery, each with and without ordinary shared work, for cohorts of 4, 8 and 16. It gives both the same 15% reusable-work assumption, $380 cohort setup, 0.5 hour plus $20 coordination, and 5% common rework exposure. Reuse requires customer permission. For four jobs, coordination makes both arrangements worse; at sixteen jobs ordinary human sharing saves modeled resource cost, while AI sharing still costs more than it saves. This rejects a claim that optional sharing must improve the primary service. It is an illustrative threshold exercise, not observed retained Alpha.

The first auxiliary coordination table incorrectly used the $149 provider selling-price anchor as an in-house production-cost input. That table is preserved as `coordination_before_correction.csv` and is superseded. The corrected comparison uses eight hours at the same $95 loaded resource proxy as other labor. This correction does not change the main service economics.

## Stochastic sensitivity and reproducibility

The 1,000 seeded histories use an explicit, uncalibrated 10% monthly common-incident assumption. Incident months reduce volume from eight to six, final acceptance to 70%, and increase API costs 1.5-fold. Reported quantiles describe that conditional experiment; they are not forecast confidence intervals, real-world profitability probabilities or evidence of demand. The deterministic 1,001-cell grid is similarly a parameter-space map, not a probability distribution.

Assertions reconcile gross collections less refunds to accepted revenue, every operating-cost category, final cash to full modeled project return, nonnegative restricted balances and zero outstanding modeled warranty obligations after runoff. Price, demand, acceptance, acquisition, development, API budgets and payment fees are independently inspectable. No new model access, spending, customer communication or financial transaction is performed.

## Independent-review amendments (v1.1)

The complete prior frozen inputs, code, outputs, verification and interpretation are preserved in `prior/economic-v1-before-review-amendments.zip`. The original input SHA-256 is `c5f811c54e9f2c6d2c4d44972e19e03120141c07405c726df5d60761f14bd464`. The amendments add adverse cases and clarify resource limits without changing the central $36,309.01 modeled return.

Every aggregate API budget is now partitioned into prospective calls no larger than 250,000 input and 100,000 output tokens, below the cited 272,000-input short-context threshold and 128,000-output limit. Central initial delivery uses eight budgeted calls of 250,000 input and 50,000 total output tokens. The partitions account for all tokens, including reasoning, and do not claim those calls occurred.

Explicit final-acceptance challenges at **40.1%, 44.9% and 45.0%** produce full modeled returns of **−$4,434.29, −$78.66 and +$12.09**, respectively. Rates reported by broader notebook-recovery studies concern different inputs and mechanical recovery; they do not calibrate this screened offer or paid customer acceptance. These stress points show how easily a small improvement near break-even changes the sign.

Base scenarios assume 100% collection of finally accepted invoices, no bad debt and no post-warranty chargebacks. Separate sensitivities deduct 1%, 2% or 5% accepted-invoice loss, or 0.5%/2% chargebacks plus a $15 received-dispute fee. They are expense challenges, not measured default rates or full collections simulations. Additional dispute-countering labor would reduce returns further.

The cash schedule now also tests collection 35 days after initial acceptance. When collection follows warranty closure, failed invoices are canceled before capture and only finally accepted invoices are paid; it never refunds nonexistent cash or creates a negative reserve. Financing assumes cash-paid fractional labor at the $95 resource proxy and an equity injection, with no debt interest. Deferring owner compensation changes cash timing, not the economic cost or obligation. The five-calendar-day delivery input is a conservative liquidity buffer and does not measure the proposed two-business-day SLA.
