\section{The opportunity and offer}
Research and R\&D teams can inherit useful custom analyses whose original author or environment is unavailable. A Recovery Capsule restores one authorized analytical Python notebook and supplies the runnable notebook/CLI, frozen inputs, source attribution, change records, independent checks and reproduction instructions. This makes analytical work usable without first establishing a software-maintenance function.

Scope is bounded to 100 code cells, 5 MB of available data and a 120-second execution envelope on the supported CPU stack. The proposed offer is two-business-day delivery after eligible inputs, one correction cycle during 14 days, and a full refund if agreed acceptance remains unmet. Turnaround is proposed, not measured. The handoff verifies computational behavior; it does not certify a scientific conclusion.

\section{What the evidence supports}
The cases are real MIT-licensed first-edition \emph{Python Data Science Handbook} code with actual removed interfaces. They are educational engineering analogues, and a free updated edition exists. They demonstrate construction and checking; they do not establish willingness to pay for these examples or performance across customer research.

The proposed paying population is customer-specific legacy analysis without an equivalent replacement. Notebook repair already has capable human and AI alternatives. Scientific novelty, Operational Alpha, retained skills and Gym training are separate possibilities, not prerequisites for this commercial foundation.

\section{Equivalent human scope and customer value}
The same complete handoff is estimated at four, eight or sixteen human hours. Public provider-rate anchors give a low reference of \$336 and a central \$1,192; a specialized loaded high reference is \$6,607.52. These are resource estimates, not exact quotations or measured demand. The \$900 price is 24.5\% below the central reference, but higher than the low reference. Free and inexpensive adequate alternatives can defeat the offer.

\section{Conditions that change the answer}
At central volume and costs, the full-project break-even price is \$441.26 and final acceptance must exceed approximately 44.99\%. With acquisition proportional to orders, full-project break-even is 2.32 attempts monthly. For a fixed campaign of 40 qualified inquiries per month, at least 8.49\% must convert to attempted orders; the central assumption is 20\%.

Current broad-corpus MLEModernizer recovery rates of 40.1\% and 44.9\% are different measures on different inputs, not estimates of this screened offer. Used only as stress points, they give full-project results of $-\$4,434.29$ and $-\$78.66$. The combined downside loses \$28,943.02. Forty monthly funded inquiries yielding no sales lose \$26,800. At \$600, with all other central assumptions unchanged, the model still returns \$12,563.98.

The human fallback comparison leaves a central buyer \$248.20 in expected gross savings to cover extra setup, delay and trust costs. The important unknowns are therefore paid demand, final acceptance, complete resource use and collection. Four successful engineering cases do not measure those variables.

\section{Cash and expansion}
Expected opening equity is \$6,760.92 with all open-warranty receipts restricted. Refunding the entire first eight-order cohort raises the requirement to \$10,298.12, assuming later recovery. A 35-day collection delay requires \$9,003.82. Refundable customer funds are never treated as unrestricted financing.

A credible expansion begins with recurring customer-specific recovery and metered accepted delivery. Shared adapters deserve additional investment only when fresh-job benefits exceed validation, maintenance and coordination costs. The current contribution is a complete digital-delivery study joined to auditable conditional economics, with the failure conditions preserved.
