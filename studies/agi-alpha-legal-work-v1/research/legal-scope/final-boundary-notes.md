# Candidate selection and the professional boundary

## Finding to lead with

The first prototype did not establish a dependable complete playbook-review packet. The execution lead reported complete engineering acceptance on 2 of 24 primary cases for B, C1 and C, and 0 of 24 for the independently constructed conventional comparator D. C improved five of the 192 rule-status decisions relative to B, without improving the number of completely accepted packets. Exact source-span and changed-text coverage checks passed across all 160 first-prototype predictions, including the transfer and stress arms. These are synthetic mechanical results; every professional legal acceptance remains unassessed.

The corresponding immutable records are `protocol/corpus-freeze.json`, `protocol/prediction-seal.json` and `protocol/key-release.json`. The publication should cite the final evaluator tables alongside those identities. Do not convert an exact quotation into evidence that its interpretation was correct, or count an escalated packet as a completed legal review.

These findings support a narrower second prototype: **exact version reconciliation for counsel**. Its proposed deliverable is an auditable comparison preserving original and revised text, valid source offsets, reconstructable changes and the full text of separately supplied amendments. It makes no claim that a revision is safe, commercially acceptable, enforceable or equivalent in intended meaning. It does not automatically consolidate amendments into an authoritative contract.

## Why this narrower offering is worth a bounded confirmation

Exact version reconciliation isolates the function the first run performed consistently. A counsel user can inspect what changed, find the original and revised passages, and retain an audit record before applying professional judgment. That is a useful support outcome in principle, although willingness to pay and actual labor savings remain unmeasured. A fresh second cohort was specified before its generation, covering edits, insertions/deletions, movement/renumbering, separate amendments, Unicode/formatting changes and nested references. Its acceptance is entirely mechanical and does not inherit substantive acceptance from the first prototype.

The second confirmation is now complete: both ordinary comparison methods passed exact-conservation acceptance on all 12 fresh cases. Each method preserved the four distinct supplied amendment documents. The independent audit verified 95 frozen source files and 75 sealed prediction files without changes. These results are recorded in `evidence/reconciliation-independent-audit.json`; the second corpus, prediction seal and key-release receipt preserve the generation order. Different highlighting volumes are descriptive and do not establish superior usability or saved professional time.

There is substantial conventional competition. [Microsoft Word's legal blackline](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/word/compare-document-differences-using-the-legal-blackline-option) already compares two document versions and presents their differences. It belongs in the commercial baseline. The study's independently authored Python comparator is an executed research arm; it is not an execution of Word, Spellbook or LegalOn. No results for those commercial products may be inferred from D.

For the broader review candidate, [Spellbook's documented redline-aware playbooks](https://help.spellbook.legal/en/articles/13875622-first-party-playbooks-redline-review), [LegalOn's contract playbooks](https://www.legalontech.com/contract-playbooks) and [oneNDA's Word add-in](https://www.onenda.org/playbook) demonstrate that reusable playbook review is already an available product category. A saved playbook, source citation or skills graph alone does not establish competitive differentiation. Superior useful outcomes, lower complete cost, easier integration or stronger evidence would need to be measured.

## Ontario scope and remaining responsibilities

The intended professional user is an Ontario-qualified commercial lawyer or a legal team operating under appropriate supervision. The current [Law Society Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90l08), including sections 1(5)–(6) and 26.1, is the relevant starting authority for the definition and restriction of legal services. Authorized exceptions and permitted licence scope matter. Calling an offering “support” is not itself a legal determination that every proposed operation is permitted.

The LSO's [Generative AI: Your professional obligations](https://lawsocietyontario-dwd0dscmayfwh7bj.a01.azurefd.net/media/lso/media/lawyers/practice-supports-resources/generative-ai-your-professional-obligations.pdf), dated 10 April 2024 and retrieved for this study, places responsibility on the licensee. Its competence guidance calls for independent human verification of relied-upon AI information. The associated [LSO white paper](https://lawsocietyontario-dwd0dscmayfwh7bj.a01.azurefd.net/media/lso/media/lawyers/practice-supports-resources/white-paper-on-licensee-use-of-generative-artificial-intelligence-en.pdf) preserves expert independent judgment and responsibility for delegated work. Confidentiality, supervision, client communication and fair billing remain relevant to any later real service. This research is not an exhaustive deployment opinion.

No real client information was accepted. Inputs are original synthetic research documents. No client advice, filing, negotiation, external communication, signature or binding decision was performed. The research playbook expresses experimental business preferences rather than Ontario-law requirements or counsel-approved standards. The French demonstration must preserve those distinctions with the same prominence as the English version.

## Claims the completed evidence can and cannot support

Technical confirmation can establish exact text conservation on the frozen cases and reproducibility in the inspected execution environment. It cannot establish comprehensive legal issue detection, human-equivalent accepted legal delivery, real customer demand, professional time saved or superiority over commercial legal software. The external [lawyering study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.1177/2755323X261427048) does not remove those gaps.

A prospective commercial model may show conditions under which the narrow support packet pays for itself. Its price, demand, residual review and integration assumptions must remain visible. Professional review belongs in the cost comparison even if the customer performs it. Avoided staff time is not automatically a cash saving, and neither is operating surplus unless receipts exceed delivery costs, obligations and reserves.

The first unsuccessful candidate, second construction cost and full research history remain in the ledger. A positive second technical result would establish a scoped support capability. It would not, by itself, establish that retained skills improved accepted work, that a graph adds value, or that ordinary operating surplus financed a worthwhile next capability. Those are separate measured propositions.
