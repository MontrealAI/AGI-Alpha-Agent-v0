# Reconciliation-only confirmation: technical conservation without established commercial advantage

## Finding and relation to the initial offering

The narrower version-reconciliation prototype produced 24 mechanically accepted packets: all 12 fresh synthetic document pairs passed the frozen exact-conservation check with each of two ordinary comparison methods, `characters` and `lines`. This demonstrates the tested text-reconciliation mechanics. It does not establish a useful professional redline, reduced human work, customer acceptance, a competitive advantage, operating earnings, or a new retained capability.

This is a separately frozen adaptation after the initial playbook-support offering failed on fresh work. It does not replace, pool with, or relabel the primary results: B, C1 and C each produced 2/24 completely accepted technical packets on the same two cases, while D produced 0/24. The primary economic campaign remains unchanged and negative; see `economics/findings.md`. Only two actual methods are represented here. They have not been duplicated into fictitious B/C efficacy arms.

The frozen secondary economic model was executed once on the independent evaluator's actual 24-record input. It provides 45 customer value thresholds, 48 partial provider-cost thresholds, and four hypothetical no-benefit investment controls. It does not simulate customers or receipts for this second offering.

## What was produced and measured

The intended professional user receives a source-bound packet showing the two supplied document versions and their recorded differences. Exact reconstruction of the source text and preservation of supplied amendments are the tested acceptance boundary. There are no legal conclusions, playbook-compliance judgments, proposed negotiations, filings or binding decisions in this variant.

The two methods passed the same narrow gate but did not necessarily present equally useful differences. On RECON-10, the line method marked the entire original and revised documents as changed while still satisfying conservation. This illustrates why a complete source reconstruction cannot establish useful localization. The character method preserved more unchanged context in aggregate, but context preservation has not been validated as a measure of professional review time, usability or quality. No monetary benefit is assigned to it. [Evidence: `evidence/reconciliation-independent-audit.json`, including `localization_assessed: false`.]

The cases are a synthetic research cohort, not recurring customer work. The study did not ingest confidential client documents, recruit professional reviewers, collect customer payments or establish a commercial acceptance rate. Twelve successful synthetic pairs per method are not a population reliability estimate for real contracts and file formats.

## Equivalent competent workflow and customer value threshold

Competent human-only delivery already has ordinary non-AI document-comparison tools. Microsoft documents Word's legal-blackline comparison, which produces a third comparison document by default. Therefore, the relevant comparator is a professional using existing comparison tools and checking the result, rather than manually comparing every character. No Microsoft workflow was executed or timed in this experiment. [Microsoft Support: https://support.microsoft.com/en-au/word/compare-document-differences-using-the-legal-blackline-option.]

The wage anchor is the Government of Canada Job Bank's Ontario median employee wage for NOC 41101, CAD 65.21/hour, with a 2023–2024 reference period and November 19, 2025 update. A 1.3 employment-burden multiplier is an explicit assumption, giving CAD 84.773/hour, or CAD 1.41288333 per minute of professional capacity. This is neither a law-firm invoice rate nor observed avoidable payroll expenditure. It is not evidence about task duration or willingness to pay. [Job Bank: https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/occupation/15815; https://www.jobbank.gc.ca/wagereport/location/on. Source notes: `economics/sources.json`.]

The frozen sensitivity coordinates use ordinary review times of 1, 3, 5, 10 and 20 minutes, and assign the proposed service exactly the same review time. These times are assumptions, not sourced task estimates or measured durations. The existing ordinary tool's incremental license cost is assumed to be zero only for an already licensed user. No claim is made that the software is free to purchase or maintain.

With offered fee P, professional capacity value w per minute, and extra buyer integration time i, the necessary time-saving threshold is:

`required saved minutes = P / w + i`.

At zero demonstrated review advantage, the fee plus any additional integration time makes the proposed service more expensive in this opportunity-cost comparison. The fee would need to be justified by measured time savings or another verified buyer benefit. The calculation does not predict adoption or imply that a provider can capture all saved capacity as revenue.

| Assumed fee, CAD | Required saved minutes, no extra integration | With 1 extra minute | With 5 extra minutes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5 | 3.539 | 4.539 | 8.539 |
| 10 | 7.078 | 8.078 | 12.078 |
| 20 | 14.155 | 15.155 | 19.155 |

For example, an assumed CAD 10 fee requires 7.078 saved professional minutes even before extra integration. If ordinary review took five minutes, eliminating all five minutes could not justify that price solely through this time-saving mechanism. If ordinary review took ten minutes, the threshold could in principle be met without extra integration, but this experiment has not shown that any of those minutes can be removed. Adding five integration minutes raises the threshold to 12.078 minutes, above the assumed ten-minute starting workflow. The threshold is a condition for value under supplied assumptions, not evidence that reducing necessary review is appropriate.

The table assigns full loaded opportunity value to saved capacity. A buyer who can use only part of any released capacity, or who values it below that rate, would require larger time savings for the same fee. No conversion of released capacity into cash payroll savings has been observed. The primary campaign's separate assumed 50% capacity-realization rule has not been imported into this secondary threshold table.

## Provider costs: marginal subtotal, recurrence and full allocation

The frozen model assigns a shadow compute rate of CAD 0.25/hour of measured production, a hypothetical tool charge of CAD 0.25/attempt, provider labor at CAD 40/hour, zero to five operator minutes per packet, and zero or one support minute. Every one of these rates and effort coordinates is an assumption. There is no observed platform bill or invoice establishing the assigned rates.

For the complete 12-packet character cohort, the measured runtime receives a shadow compute allocation of approximately CAD 0.00003849; the line cohort receives approximately CAD 0.00002784. These very small figures describe only an allocated runtime component. They are not accepted-deliverable costs and are not compared with a lawyer's complete invoice.

The difference between the two recorded machine runtimes has no established customer value. Both use ordinary standard-library comparison. No new capability, professional-time saving, price premium or long-run compute scalability result is inferred from the difference.

| Provider cost category | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Recorded generation/native runtime | Measured subtotal; monetized only at an assumed shadow rate |
| Tool use | Assumed CAD 0.25 per recorded attempt |
| Operator and support effort | Sensitivity only; no human measurement |
| Provider onboarding | Assumed 60 minutes at CAD 40/hour, amortized over assumed 12, 50 or 200 jobs |
| Buyer review and extra integration | Kept in customer value thresholds; actual time unknown |
| Acquisition, maintenance, professional review if required, business overhead | Incomplete; not represented as zero full costs |
| Historical research and adaptation | Full cost unknown; not written off or claimed recovered |
| Complete recurring cost | Unknown |
| Fully allocated cost | Unknown |

For illustration, at three operator minutes and one support minute per technically accepted packet, the marginal subtotal is approximately CAD 2.91667 for either method. The assumed CAD 40 onboarding cost contributes CAD 3.33333 at 12 lifetime jobs, CAD 0.80 at 50 jobs, or CAD 0.20 at 200 jobs. The resulting prices needed just to cover those components are approximately CAD 6.25, CAD 3.72 and CAD 3.12 respectively. They exclude the unresolved cost categories in the table and do not establish a viable offer.

At zero operator and support time, the model's marginal subtotal is approximately CAD 0.25. That sensitivity endpoint does not establish fully unattended delivery: it simply shows the assumed floor for the components included. Human handling, usability assessment and necessary professional responsibilities remain unmeasured.

A business cannot infer available surplus from the gap between an assumed fee and these partial subtotals. Actual paid demand, complete costs, reserves and obligations are required. The canonical ledger therefore retains observed collections at zero and full cost, margin, reserves, unpaid obligations, available surplus and eligible reinvestment as unknown.

## Reinvestment control, maintenance and lifetime

This adaptation did not add a new retained skill, so there is no earned-capability efficacy comparison or claim of succession. A hypothetical investment is included solely as a no-benefit control using the unchanged canonical `calculateInvestment` function: CAD 400 construction, CAD 0.25 maintenance per reuse and zero asserted saving per reuse.

| Assumed remaining uses | Hypothetical lifetime cost, CAD | Assumed gross saving, CAD | Conditional net benefit, CAD | Payback within horizon |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| 12 | 403.00 | 0 | −403.00 | No |
| 50 | 412.50 | 0 | −412.50 | No |
| 100 | 425.00 | 0 | −425.00 | No |
| 250 | 462.50 | 0 | −462.50 | No |

Break-even reuse and repayment time are non-finite under that zero-benefit condition. The machine-readable break-even field is null, not zero. Additional reuse increases the hypothetical upkeep cost without creating savings. A useful lifetime or positive repayment time cannot be estimated from these data; neither demand recurrence nor a performance improvement was observed. These figures are not construction spending actually incurred or an estimate of the prototype's full historical cost. No investment was executed, and no construction cost was charged to both the primary campaign and this hypothetical control.

The control does not establish that every future reconciliation improvement is worthless. It establishes that an investment cannot be justified by assuming a benefit absent from the evidence. A new proposal would need independently assessed usefulness, matched ordinary-tool review time, a measured improvement on fresh work, realistic uptake and complete incremental costs before a financing claim could be evaluated.
