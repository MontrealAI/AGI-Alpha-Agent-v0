# A question that now has a checkable answer

**An unbounded search has become a finite, reusable proof.** This study constructs an exact mathematical certificate that rules out a proposed family of low-heft congruence partitions for every interval length. A reader can check the result with ordinary Python, without a language model, a numerical optimizer, a wallet or a trusted prediction about future capability. The artifact is the useful outcome: a precise answer that subsequent researchers can inspect, challenge and build upon.

The problem is simple to state. Partition the integers from $1$ to $n$ into complete residue classes, with each class containing at least two integers of that interval. A class with modulus $m$ contributes $1/m$ to the partition's **heft**. Gerry Myerson asks whether, with distinct moduli, such a partition can have heft below $0.98$ [@myerson2026; @myerson2009]. We establish the following computer-assisted theorem:

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle h=\sum_i\frac{1}{m_i}>
I:=\frac{1891871140656049}{1929687500000000}
=0.9804028583156853\ldots>0.98.}
$$

The proof covers every positive integer $n$ admitting a partition and even permits repeated moduli. It preserves complete residue classes, exact coverage and at least two hits per class. It does not apply to arbitrary selected arithmetic progressions, singleton classes or two-dimensional coverings. The inspected prior general lower bound is $\sqrt8-2\approx0.828427$, from Emanuel's dissertation, Appendix C, Theorem C.1 [@emanuel2011]. The constant above is certified, not claimed optimal. A bounded literature audit did not locate this result; external mathematical review and a fuller priority assessment remain pending.

The enterprise conclusion is narrower than the scientific result. No customer bought the work; actual revenue and receipts are zero, and complete actual authoring cost is unknown. An explicit planning model for review and integration of this existing artifact is conditionally profitable with four compatible sponsors, but demand is unvalidated and financing is required. A competent conventional shared arrangement has the same economics. No measured Operational Alpha, superhuman specialist, profitable skill compounding or general discovery engine is established.

**Abstract.** We investigate whether a bounded, independently challenged discovery process can produce a new useful result rather than an architectural proposal or an attractive scenario. A diverse opportunity screen rejects generic novelty claims already addressed by established methods. A first exact-code construction lane fails to improve the strongest located comparators. A subsequent finite-cover investigation discovers a rational degree-20 dual weight whose class inequalities are certified on 78 continuous patches, with an analytic tail and an exact all-length objective identity. Independently implemented integer/rational checkers reproduce the computational proof. Fresh tests examine 512 additional residue classes, four additional interval lengths, 19 scope decisions and malformed or altered inputs. Failed numerical evidence is retained. Existing AGI ALPHA provenance and execution components are exercised locally without minting, live identity claims or financial activity. Complete conditional accounting separates operating surplus, full-project profit, funding needs, restricted deposits and service obligations. The result demonstrates a portable research capability and a concrete mathematical advance relative to inspected prior bounds. It does not demonstrate an exclusive algorithmic advantage or a commercially validated enterprise.

# The construction and its verification

## A weight that every class must respect

Let $T_r$ denote the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. The construction is the exact symmetric polynomial

$$
f(x)=\frac{2499}{2500\cdot10^{10}}
\sum_{j=0}^{10}b_jT_{2j}(2x-1),\qquad 0\le x\le1.
$$

The eleven integers $b_j$ appear in Appendix A and in the frozen coefficient file. Assign the $i$th integer weight $f((i-1/2)/n)/n$. We prove two facts: every allowed residue class receives total weight at most its reciprocal modulus, and the total weight of the interval is strictly greater than $I$. Summing the class inequalities proves the theorem. The weights need not be nonnegative because every integer is counted exactly once.

![The exact degree-20 witness, plotted for explanation. Sampled values in this figure are not the acceptance test; the proof uses rational coefficients and inequalities over entire continuous domains.](figures/witness.pdf){width=95%}

For a class with $k$ hits, normalized spacing $d=m/n$ and first normalized midpoint $a$, it suffices to show

$$
d\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}f(a+jd)\le1,
\qquad
\max(0,1-kd)\le a\le\min(d,1-(k-1)d).
$$

Symmetry reduces the short-class domain to two parameterized squares for each $k=2,\ldots,40$. On each square the inequality becomes nonnegativity of a rational polynomial of bidegree at most $(21,20)$. Bernstein coefficients and exact midpoint subdivision certify all 78 squares. For $k\ge41$, a derivative-variation bound controls every shifted grid. An exact polynomial identity in $1/n^2$ then proves that the finite midpoint average exceeds $I$ for every $n\ge2$. The case $n=1$ is vacuous. Appendix A supplies the complete argument.

# Appendix A. Complete mathematical argument

## A.1 Definition and exact witness

Let a complete residue class modulo a positive integer $m$ be restricted to $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. Suppose finitely many such restrictions partition that interval and each contains at least two points. Repeated moduli are allowed in this proof. Let $h$ be the sum of reciprocal moduli.

The coefficient vector, in increasing even Chebyshev order, is

$$
\begin{aligned}
(b_0,\ldots,b_{10})={}&(7061527976,-8616261687,2079655784,\\
&-480883959,30533879,8370806,32646497,\\
&-16295510,3707761,-15109523,30943868).
\end{aligned}
$$

Set $f(x)=2499(2500\cdot10^{10})^{-1}\sum b_jT_{2j}(2x-1)$. It satisfies $f(1-x)=f(x)$. Integration of each Chebyshev term gives $\int_0^1T_{2j}(2x-1)\,dx=(1-4j^2)^{-1}$, so its exact integral is $I$ as stated in the theorem.

## A.2 The class domain and the dual argument

A class has interval hits $r,r+m,\ldots,r+(k-1)m$, where $k\ge2$. Put $d=m/n$ and $a=(r-1/2)/n$. The previous and next members lie outside the interval. Consequently

$$
\max(0,1-kd)\le a\le\min(d,1-(k-1)d),
\qquad \frac1{k+1}\le d\le\frac1{k-1}.
$$

These closed inequalities relax the strict exterior conditions of actual integer classes and thus enlarge the checked domain. Define the class constraint

$$d\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}f(a+jd)\le1.\tag{A1}$$

If (A1) holds, the class's assigned weight is at most $1/m$. Exact partitioning then yields

$$h\ge M_n:=\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^n f((r-1/2)/n).\tag{A2}$$

There is no sign restriction on individual weights in this summation argument. We next establish (A1) for every class and $M_n>I$ for every $n\ge2$.

## A.3 Every short class: 78 exact continuous certificates

Write $F(z)=f((z+1)/2)$ and $u=2a+(k-1)d-1$. Reflection of the class allows $u\ge0$ because $F$ is even. The admissible domain becomes

$$0\le u\le\min((k+1)d-1,1-(k-1)d).$$

For $(s,v)\in[0,1]^2$, its two halves are parameterized by

$$
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{lower spacing:}&d=(k+s)/(k(k+1)),&u=vs/k,\\
\text{upper spacing:}&d=(k-1+s)/(k(k-1)),&u=v(1-s)/k.
\end{array}
$$

For each $k=2,\ldots,40$ and each half, the polynomial

$$P_k(s,v)=1-d\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}F\big(u+(2j-k+1)d\big)$$

has bidegree at most $(21,20)$. Clear its positive rational denominator and transform to the tensor Bernstein basis. A polynomial in that basis is a convex combination of its coefficients on the unit square. Nonnegative coefficients therefore prove $P_k\ge0$ on that square. Exact de Casteljau midpoint subdivision applies the same test on smaller rectangles whose union is the original square.

The primary checker uses the identity

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(u+(2j-k+1)d)^\ell
=\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose r}u^{\ell-r}d^r
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(2j-k+1)^r.
$$

Symmetry eliminates the odd terms. After clearing the coefficient denominator, a common denominator contains $[k(k\pm1)]^{21}$ and is positive. All 78 patches pass: 106 visited nodes, 92 accepted leaves and maximum subdivision depth six. The independent sparse-Horner implementation checks the same polynomials with a different conversion and subdivision sequence: 95 accepted leaves and the same maximum depth. Exact leaf rectangles and bounds are retained. Different trees do not change the covered domain.

## A.4 Every larger class: an analytic tail

Define $g=f-f(0)$ on $[0,1]$ and extend it by zero to the real line. Symmetry makes this extension continuous. Exact univariate Bernstein bounds on 64 rational subintervals give

$$|f(0)|<3/250,\qquad \|f''\|_\infty<486,
\qquad \operatorname{TV}(g')<24.$$

The variation includes both endpoint jumps of the extended derivative. Its tighter computed upper bound is approximately 23.05544856893; the second-derivative bound is approximately 485.76248165584. Both arise from rational inequalities, not rounded acceptance thresholds.

For a continuous compactly supported function whose derivative has bounded variation, the shifted-grid trapezoid bound is

$$
\left|d\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}g(a+jd)-\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)\,dx\right|
\le\frac{d^2}{8}\operatorname{TV}(g').
$$

To obtain the constant, integrate the piecewise-linear interpolation error on each grid cell against the distributional second derivative. The corresponding kernel has magnitude at most $d^2/8$; summing the variation over cells gives the bound. The integral of the interpolant equals the composite trapezoid sum. Zero extension and continuity account for cells crossing the endpoints.

For a complete class, $|kd-1|\le d$. Adding back $f(0)$ gives

$$
d\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}f(a+jd)
\le I+|f(0)|d+\frac{d^2}{8}\operatorname{TV}(g').
$$

For $k\ge41$, $d\le1/40$, so the right side is at most

$$I+\frac3{10000}+\frac3{1600}=I+0.002175<1.$$

Thus (A1) holds for every larger class as well. The independent tighter rational bound is approximately 0.98250103613, also strictly below one.

## A.5 Every interval length: the strict objective

Let $t=1/n^2$, $z_i=(2i-1-n)/n$ and $A_r(t)=n^{-1}\sum_i z_i^{2r}$. The identity

$$\sum_{i=1}^n\big((z_i+1/n)^{2r+1}-(z_i-1/n)^{2r+1}\big)=2$$

telescopes. Binomial expansion gives

$$
A_0(t)=1,\qquad
A_r(t)=\frac{1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}{2r+1\choose2\ell+1}t^\ell A_{r-\ell}(t)}{2r+1}.
$$

Writing $F(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{10}c_rz^{2r}$, this yields the exact identity

$$M_n=\sum_{r=0}^{10}c_rA_r(t)=I+tQ(t),$$

where $Q$ has degree nine. The coefficient list agrees exactly with a separate Euler--Maclaurin derivation. The independent checker uses an explicit Chebyshev coefficient formula and the telescoping recurrence, without importing the first derivation.

All Bernstein coefficients of $Q$ are strictly positive on each of the 16 intervals $[j/256,(j+1)/256]$, $j=0,\ldots,15$. Hence $Q(t)>0$ on $[0,1/16]$, which covers every $n\ge4$. The two remaining exact differences are

$$M_2-I=\frac{184569613348897}{61750000000000000}>0,$$

$$M_3-I=\frac{1033236506971259179273363}{53827234190437500000000000}>0.$$

Therefore $M_n>I$ for every $n\ge2$. Together with (A2), this proves $h>I$. The JSON evidence contains every rational coefficient and each positive interval bound.

A redundant weaker completion also survives: the midpoint-remainder inequality gives $M_n>I-486/(24n^2)>0.98$ for $n\ge225$, with smaller $n$ checked exactly. This overlap provides an additional check on the original threshold conclusion; it is not needed for the stronger constant.
