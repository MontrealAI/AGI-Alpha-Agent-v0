# A question that now has a checkable answer

**An unbounded search has become a finite, reusable proof.** This study constructs an exact mathematical certificate that rules out a proposed family of low-heft congruence partitions for every interval length. A reader can check the result with ordinary Python, without a language model, a numerical optimizer, a wallet or a trusted prediction about future capability. The artifact is the useful outcome: a precise answer that subsequent researchers can inspect, challenge and build upon.

The problem is simple to state. Partition the integers from $1$ to $n$ into complete residue classes, with each class containing at least two integers of that interval. A class with modulus $m$ contributes $1/m$ to the partition's **heft**. Gerry Myerson asks whether, with distinct moduli, such a partition can have heft below $0.98$ [@myerson2026; @myerson2009]. We establish the following computer-assisted theorem:

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle h=\sum_i\frac{1}{m_i}\ge
J:=\frac{17902560}{18258529}
=0.9805039606421744\ldots>0.98.}
$$

The strongest certificate was discovered by the fresh conventional challenge and strengthened by exact normalization in independent review. The proof covers every positive integer $n$ admitting a partition and even permits repeated moduli. It preserves complete residue classes, exact coverage and at least two hits per class. It does not apply to arbitrary selected arithmetic progressions, singleton classes or two-dimensional coverings. The inspected prior general lower bound is $\sqrt8-2\approx0.828427$, from Emanuel's dissertation, Appendix C, Theorem C.1 [@emanuel2011]. The constant above is certified, not claimed optimal. A bounded literature audit did not locate this result; external mathematical review and a fuller priority assessment remain pending.

The enterprise conclusion is narrower than the scientific result. No customer bought the work; actual revenue and receipts are zero, and complete actual authoring cost is unknown. An explicit planning model for review and integration of this existing artifact is conditionally profitable with four compatible sponsors, but demand is unvalidated and financing is required. A competent conventional shared arrangement has the same economics. No measured Operational Alpha, superhuman specialist, profitable skill compounding or general discovery engine is established.

**Abstract.** We investigate whether a bounded, independently challenged discovery process can produce a new useful result rather than an architectural proposal or an attractive scenario. A diverse opportunity screen rejects generic novelty claims already addressed by established methods. A first exact-code construction lane fails to improve the strongest located comparators. A subsequent finite-cover investigation first constructs an exact degree-20 dual weight. A fresh conventional challenge independently discovers a simpler piecewise-linear weight; normalization by its exactly checked maximum gives the stronger final bound. Two separately implemented line-arrangement checkers and an analytic tail certify every allowed class, while cell integrals transfer the result to every finite interval length. Fresh tests examine additional classes, scope decisions and malformed or altered inputs. The earlier polynomial proof remains independently verified and reproducible. Failed numerical evidence is retained. Existing AGI ALPHA provenance and execution components are exercised locally without minting, live identity claims or financial activity. Complete conditional accounting separates operating surplus, full-project profit, funding needs, restricted deposits and service obligations. The result demonstrates a portable research capability and a concrete mathematical advance relative to inspected prior bounds. It does not demonstrate an exclusive algorithmic advantage or a commercially validated enterprise.

# The construction and its verification

## A weight that every class must respect

Let $W$ be the continuous piecewise-linear function with knots $r/20$, $r=0,\ldots,20$. Its first eleven heights are

$$0,159,316,493,721,981,1240,1500,1658,1818,1858,$$

and the rest are their reflection about the midpoint. Extend it by zero outside the interval. Its exact integral is $A=1963/2$. The construction proves that every complete progression with spacing $d$ and at least two points in $[0,1]$ obeys

$$d\sum_j W(a+jd)\le M:=\frac{18258529}{18240}.$$

Assign integer $i$ the **cell integral** $w_i=\int_{(i-1)/n}^{i/n}W(x)\,dx$. Integrating the progression inequality over the phase within each cell proves $m\sum_{i\in C}w_i\le M$ for every complete restricted class $C$ of modulus $m$. Since the classes partition all integers exactly once, $A\le Mh$. Thus $h\ge A/M=J$. This uses actual finite cells; it introduces no limiting approximation or exception for small lengths.

![The final piecewise-linear weight divided by its exact class maximum, with the earlier polynomial shown for comparison. These curves explain the construction; sampled plotting values do not certify it.](figures/witness.pdf){width=95%}

For a class with $k$ points, spacing $d$ and first point $a$, the complete-class parameter domain is the quadrilateral

$$0\le a\le d,\qquad a+(k-1)d\le1\le a+kd.$$

Knot-crossing lines partition that quadrilateral into cells. Within a cell, the class sum has the form $A_1ad+B_1d^2+C_1d$. A maximum occurs on a cell boundary: a nonzero mixed coefficient makes the Hessian indefinite; when it is zero, the value is independent of $a$ and extends to a boundary. On each boundary segment, exact quadratic maximization checks endpoints and any interior stationary point. All knot lines and all four exterior boundaries are included.

The primary conventional checker examines 1,967 segments for $k=2,\ldots,20$ and obtains exact maximum $M$. A separately written checker reconstructs the clipping from polygon-edge intersections and uses direct interpolation to fit the quadratic on every segment. It agrees. For $k\ge21$, the total variation of the derivative of the zero extension is 21,200, yielding the universal tail bound $7905/8<M$. Appendix A gives the full proof.

# Appendix A. A universal piecewise-linear weight certificate

## A.1 Statement and exact coefficients

Let finitely many complete residue classes, restricted to the integers $1,\ldots,n$, partition that interval. Suppose each restriction contains at least two integers. If their moduli are $m_1,\ldots,m_r$, then

$$
\boxed{\displaystyle h=\sum_{i=1}^r\frac1{m_i}
\ge J:=\frac{17902560}{18258529}
=0.9805039606421744\ldots>\frac{49}{50}.}
$$

The proof permits repeated moduli and therefore answers the original distinct-modulus question. It retains complete restrictions, exact partitioning and the minimum of two interval elements per class. No optimality assertion accompanies the constant $J$.

Define $W$ as the continuous linear interpolant at the knots $j/20$, for $0\le j\le20$, with $W(1-x)=W(x)$ and the following integer coefficients. Extend $W$ by zero outside $[0,1]$.

| $j$ | $W(j/20)$ |
|--:|--:|
| 0 | 0 |
| 1 | 159 |
| 2 | 316 |
| 3 | 493 |
| 4 | 721 |
| 5 | 981 |
| 6 | 1240 |
| 7 | 1500 |
| 8 | 1658 |
| 9 | 1818 |
| 10 | 1858 |

Reflection supplies the remaining ten coefficients. Exact trapezoid integration and summation of derivative jumps give

$$
I_W:=\int_0^1W(x)\,dx=\frac{1963}{2},
\qquad V:=\operatorname{TV}(W')=21200.
$$

Here $W'$ denotes the derivative of the zero extension. Its variation includes the jumps from zero derivative at both support endpoints. The normalization used below is

$$M=\frac{18258529}{18240},\qquad J=\frac{I_W}{M}.$$

## A.2 The complete progression domain

For $k\ge2$, consider the sum

$$S_k(a,d)=d\sum_{\ell=0}^{k-1}W(a+\ell d),\qquad d>0.$$

The closed domain

$$
D_k=\{(a,d):0\le a\le d,\quad a+(k-1)d\le1\le a+kd\}
$$

contains every complete $k$-point progression in the open unit interval. The inequalities allow its predecessor or successor to lie at an endpoint; this enlargement is harmless because $W(0)=W(1)=0$. In particular,

$$\frac1{k+1}\le d\le\frac1{k-1}.$$

The domain is the compact quadrilateral with consecutive vertices

$$
\left(\frac1{k+1},\frac1{k+1}\right),\quad
\left(\frac1k,\frac1k\right),\quad
\left(0,\frac1{k-1}\right),\quad
\left(0,\frac1k\right).
$$

We prove $S_k\le M$ throughout $D_k$ for every $k\ge2$.

## A.3 Exact maximization for short progressions

For each $2\le k\le20$, subdivide $D_k$ by all knot-crossing lines

$$a+jd=\frac r{20},\qquad 0\le j<k,\quad 0\le r\le20.$$

On each resulting polygonal cell, the arguments of $W$ stay in fixed linear pieces. Consequently

$$S_k(a,d)=Aad+Bd^2+Cd$$

for constant rational coefficients. If $A\ne0$, its Hessian has determinant $-A^2<0$, excluding an interior local maximum. Equivalently, its derivative in $a$ is the nonzero quantity $Ad$. If $A=0$, the objective is independent of $a$: a maximizing section at fixed $d$ reaches the cell boundary with the same value. Degenerate cells already belong to boundaries. Thus a global maximum occurs on an enumerated boundary, including possible stationary ridges.

Every boundary is a knot-crossing line or one of the four edges of $D_k$. The lines with $(j,r)=(0,0)$ and $(k-1,20)$ cover $a=0$ and $a+(k-1)d=1$. The remaining edges are included explicitly as $(j,r)=(-1,0)$ and $(k,20)$, representing $a=d$ and $a+kd=1$.

On a fixed line $a+jd=r/20$, every crossing with another knot line occurs at

$$d=\frac{s-r}{20(\ell-j)},\qquad \ell\ne j.$$

The domain endpoints and these crossings divide the line into finitely many rational segments. On each, $S_k=\alpha d^2+\beta d$. Its maximum occurs at an endpoint or, when $\alpha<0$, at the stationary point $-\beta/(2\alpha)$ if that point belongs to the segment. Degenerate point segments are also evaluated.

The standard-library checker `fresh_conventional/verify_weight.py` implements this enumeration using exact integers and fractions. All nineteen cardinalities pass, and their largest value is precisely

$$M=\frac{18258529}{18240},$$

attained at $k=2$, $a=7/20$ and $d=4273/9120$. The original enumeration checks 1,967 segments, including point cases and repeated descriptions.

The independent checker `lineage/audit_conventional_weight.py` reconstructs line segments by intersecting polygon edges, rather than by the first checker's inequality-clipping formulas. It interpolates $W$ directly, reconstructs each quadratic from exact endpoint values, checks its midpoint identity and evaluates stationary points directly. It reproduces every per-cardinality maximum. Its different bookkeeping gives 1,863 positive-length segments and 360 interior stationary evaluations, with degenerate points checked separately. The audit receipt is `confirmation/conventional_independent/INDEPENDENT_WEIGHT_AUDIT.json`. These are separate implementations within one study, not external peer review or proof-assistant verification.

## A.4 An analytic bound for all remaining cardinalities

For a continuous compactly supported piecewise-linear function, the composite trapezoid estimate on a lattice of spacing $d$ is

$$
\left|d\sum_{z\in\mathbb Z}W(a+zd)-\int_{\mathbb R}W(x)\,dx\right|
\le\frac{d^2}{8}\operatorname{TV}(W').
$$

Indeed, on a lattice cell $[u,u+d]$, the trapezoid error is the integral of $(x-u)(u+d-x)/2$ against the signed measure of derivative jumps. This kernel has magnitude at most $d^2/8$. Summing over cells and using the measure's total variation proves the estimate. Endpoint jumps of the zero extension are included, so cells crossing the support boundary require no exception.

For $(a,d)\in D_k$, the listed progression contains every nonzero contribution of the lattice. When $k\ge21$, the bound $d\le1/20$ therefore gives

$$
S_k(a,d)\le\frac{1963}{2}+\frac{21200}{8\cdot20^2}
=\frac{7905}{8}<M.
$$

Together with the finite exact computation, this proves $S_k\le M$ for every admissible progression, without a cardinality cutoff.

## A.5 Exact transfer to every integer interval

For an arbitrary partition of $\{1,\ldots,n\}$, define cell-integral weights

$$w_j=\int_{(j-1)/n}^{j/n}W(x)\,dx.$$

Consider a complete restricted residue class $C$ modulo $m$. For each $0<t<1$, its points

$$x_j(t)=\frac{j-1+t}{n},\qquad j\in C,$$

form a progression of spacing $d=m/n$ with at least two members. Its predecessor integer is at most zero, so its normalized predecessor is at most $(-1+t)/n<0$. Its successor integer is at least $n+1$, so its normalized successor is at least $1+t/n>1$. The progression thus belongs to $D_{|C|}$.

Integrating its certified inequality in $t$ yields

$$
\frac mn\sum_{j\in C}\int_0^1W\!\left(\frac{j-1+t}{n}\right)dt
=m\sum_{j\in C}w_j\le M.
$$

Sum over the partition. Every integer cell occurs once, giving $I_W\le Mh$ and hence $h\ge J$. This applies directly to every admissible $n$; no limiting argument, midpoint correction or unverified small-length case remains. For $n=1$ the hypotheses are impossible.

## A.6 Normalization and contribution

The discovery report used the convenient safe scale $T=2003/2$, obtaining $h\ge1963/2003>0.98$. Its computation already established the smaller exact maximum $M$. Normalizing by $M$ gives the stronger bound $J$ without changing the knots, domain or proof obligations. The theorem states $h\ge J$; it does not assert strictness at $J$.

Discovery used standard linear and mixed-integer optimization, followed by exact rational geometry and a classical quadrature estimate. The contribution is the explicit certificate and resulting universal bound. It establishes neither a new general solver nor an exclusive AI capability or controlled discovery advantage.


# Appendix B. The earlier polynomial proof

## B.1 Definition and exact witness

Let a complete residue class modulo a positive integer $m$ be restricted to $\{1,\ldots,n\}$. Suppose finitely many such restrictions partition that interval and each contains at least two points. Repeated moduli are allowed in this proof. Let $h$ be the sum of reciprocal moduli.

The coefficient vector, in increasing even Chebyshev order, is

$$
\begin{aligned}
(b_0,\ldots,b_{10})={}&(7061527976,-8616261687,2079655784,\\
&-480883959,30533879,8370806,32646497,\\
&-16295510,3707761,-15109523,30943868).
\end{aligned}
$$

Let $T_r$ denote the Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind. Define

$$f(x)=\frac{2499}{2500\cdot10^{10}}\sum_{j=0}^{10} b_jT_{2j}(2x-1).$$

It satisfies $f(1-x)=f(x)$. Each basis element has integral $(1-4j^2)^{-1}$ on $[0,1]$, so the exact witness integral is

$$I=\frac{1891871140656049}{1929687500000000}=0.9804028583156853\ldots.$$

## B.2 The class domain and the dual argument

A class has interval hits $r,r+m,\ldots,r+(k-1)m$, where $k\ge2$. Put $d=m/n$ and $a=(r-1/2)/n$. The previous and next members lie outside the interval. Consequently

$$
\max(0,1-kd)\le a\le\min(d,1-(k-1)d),
\qquad \frac1{k+1}\le d\le\frac1{k-1}.
$$

These closed inequalities relax the strict exterior conditions of actual integer classes and thus enlarge the checked domain. Define the class constraint

$$d\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}f(a+jd)\le1.\tag{B1}$$

Assign integer $i$ the weight $f((i-1/2)/n)/n$. If (B1) holds, the class's assigned weight is at most $1/m$. Exact partitioning then yields

$$h\ge M_n:=\frac1n\sum_{r=1}^n f((r-1/2)/n).\tag{B2}$$

There is no sign restriction on individual weights in this summation argument. We next establish (B1) for every class and $M_n>I$ for every $n\ge2$.

## B.3 Every short class: 78 exact continuous certificates

Write $F(z)=f((z+1)/2)$ and $u=2a+(k-1)d-1$. Reflection of the class allows $u\ge0$ because $F$ is even. The admissible domain becomes

$$0\le u\le\min((k+1)d-1,1-(k-1)d).$$

For $(s,v)\in[0,1]^2$, its two halves are parameterized by

$$
\begin{array}{lll}
\text{lower spacing:}&d=(k+s)/(k(k+1)),&u=vs/k,\\
\text{upper spacing:}&d=(k-1+s)/(k(k-1)),&u=v(1-s)/k.
\end{array}
$$

For each $k=2,\ldots,40$ and each half, the polynomial

$$P_k(s,v)=1-d\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}F\big(u+(2j-k+1)d\big)$$

has bidegree at most $(21,20)$. Clear its positive rational denominator and transform to the tensor Bernstein basis. A polynomial in that basis is a convex combination of its coefficients on the unit square. Nonnegative coefficients therefore prove $P_k\ge0$ on that square. Exact de Casteljau midpoint subdivision applies the same test on smaller rectangles whose union is the original square.

The primary checker uses the identity

$$
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(u+(2j-k+1)d)^\ell
=\sum_{r=0}^{\ell}{\ell\choose r}u^{\ell-r}d^r
\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(2j-k+1)^r.
$$

Symmetry eliminates the odd terms. After clearing the coefficient denominator, a common denominator contains $[k(k\pm1)]^{21}$ and is positive. All 78 patches pass: 106 visited nodes, 92 accepted leaves and maximum subdivision depth six. The independent sparse-Horner implementation checks the same polynomials with a different conversion and subdivision sequence: 95 accepted leaves and the same maximum depth. Exact leaf rectangles and bounds are retained. Different trees do not change the covered domain.

## B.4 Every larger class: an analytic tail

Define $g=f-f(0)$ on $[0,1]$ and extend it by zero to the real line. Symmetry makes this extension continuous. Exact univariate Bernstein bounds on 64 rational subintervals give

$$|f(0)|<3/250,\qquad \|f''\|_\infty<486,
\qquad \operatorname{TV}(g')<24.$$

The variation includes both endpoint jumps of the extended derivative. Its tighter computed upper bound is approximately 23.05544856893; the second-derivative bound is approximately 485.76248165584. Both arise from rational inequalities, not rounded acceptance thresholds.

For a continuous compactly supported function whose derivative has bounded variation, the shifted-grid trapezoid bound is

$$
\left|d\sum_{j\in\mathbb Z}g(a+jd)-\int_{\mathbb R}g(x)\,dx\right|
\le\frac{d^2}{8}\operatorname{TV}(g').
$$

To obtain the constant, integrate the piecewise-linear interpolation error on each grid cell against the distributional second derivative. The corresponding kernel has magnitude at most $d^2/8$; summing the variation over cells gives the bound. The integral of the interpolant equals the composite trapezoid sum. Zero extension and continuity account for cells crossing the endpoints.

For a complete class, $|kd-1|\le d$. Adding back $f(0)$ gives

$$
d\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}f(a+jd)
\le I+|f(0)|d+\frac{d^2}{8}\operatorname{TV}(g').
$$

For $k\ge41$, $d\le1/40$, so the right side is at most

$$I+\frac3{10000}+\frac3{1600}=I+0.002175<1.$$

Thus (B1) holds for every larger class as well. The independent tighter rational bound is approximately 0.98250103613, also strictly below one.

## B.5 Every interval length: the strict objective

Let $t=1/n^2$, $z_i=(2i-1-n)/n$ and $A_r(t)=n^{-1}\sum_i z_i^{2r}$. The identity

$$\sum_{i=1}^n\big((z_i+1/n)^{2r+1}-(z_i-1/n)^{2r+1}\big)=2$$

telescopes. Binomial expansion gives

$$
A_0(t)=1,\qquad
A_r(t)=\frac{1-\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}{2r+1\choose2\ell+1}t^\ell A_{r-\ell}(t)}{2r+1}.
$$

Writing $F(z)=\sum_{r=0}^{10}c_rz^{2r}$, this yields the exact identity

$$M_n=\sum_{r=0}^{10}c_rA_r(t)=I+tQ(t),$$

where $Q$ has degree nine. The coefficient list agrees exactly with a separate Euler--Maclaurin derivation. The independent checker uses an explicit Chebyshev coefficient formula and the telescoping recurrence, without importing the first derivation.

All Bernstein coefficients of $Q$ are strictly positive on each of the 16 intervals $[j/256,(j+1)/256]$, $j=0,\ldots,15$. Hence $Q(t)>0$ on $[0,1/16]$, which covers every $n\ge4$. The two remaining exact differences are

$$M_2-I=\frac{184569613348897}{61750000000000000}>0,$$

$$M_3-I=\frac{1033236506971259179273363}{53827234190437500000000000}>0.$$

Therefore $M_n>I$ for every $n\ge2$. Together with (B2), this proves $h>I$. The JSON evidence contains every rational coefficient and each positive interval bound.

A redundant weaker completion also survives: the midpoint-remainder inequality gives $M_n>I-486/(24n^2)>0.98$ for $n\ge225$, with smaller $n$ checked exactly. This overlap provides an additional check on the original threshold conclusion; it is not needed for the stronger constant.
