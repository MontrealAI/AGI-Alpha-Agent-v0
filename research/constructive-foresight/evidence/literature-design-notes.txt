MISSION SELECTION BEFORE OUTCOMES

Scientific computing / repeated sparse inverse actions: recommended. Observable setup, solve, verification, restoration, and error; public matrices; bounded computation; direct factorization is a strong already-known capability. Beneficiary is a prospective numerical-analysis user, not an enrolled customer. Internal benchmark acceptance must be called internal.
Energy system design / public power-network matrices: include as numerical transfer case only. Real utility/market adoption, planning constraints, and full AC feasibility are outside a linear benchmark; not an operational energy claim.
Structural engineering / stiffness matrices: include as representative real-origin numerical work family. Do not represent solutions as a safety assessment of the named structure. Synthetic RHS must be labeled synthetic.
Industrial instrumentation / new sensing method: reject for this edition because calibration, physical sensor access, independent acceptance, and real acquisition cost cannot be established locally; also avoids repeating earlier thermal/spectroscopy papers.
Software verification / open-source build validation: feasible but reject as deep mission because repository held-runtime/review dependencies and rapidly changing external CI make a short prospective study less controlled. Keep useful integration tests separate.

FAIR DESIGN AND FALSIFICATION

Lock matrix IDs, calibration/holdout RHS seeds, method grid, budgets, stopping rules, and fallback/exclusion logic before benchmarking. Keep every failure. Do not drop ill-conditioned matrices after CG loses. Freeze timestamped forecasts after permitted calibration and BEFORE local holdout runs. Prospective local event: numerical qualification AND delivery within a fixed monotonic deadline. This is prospective delivery evidence, not prospective real-world adoption.
Compare cached splu against repeated spsolve, static expert choosing the fastest calibrated solver and allowed caching, CG+Jacobi (and a credible supported preconditioner if finite budget allows), and adaptive rent-or-build. Forecast-only is an ablation. Every competitor may construct the same capability. Do not give constructive foresight free engineering, probes, prewarming, or future knowledge.
Use randomized method order within repeated blocks, fixed BLAS thread count, explicit wall/CPU timers, and comparable residual gates. Include setup, policy computation, validation, and restoration. SuperLU objects are not automatically serializable; if restore refactors, report and cost that. A manifest alone does not restore an already-ready numerical factor.
Acceptance: frozen normwise residual/backward-error threshold, finite solution, shape compatibility, matrix/content digest, explicit supported problem class. For known manufactured solutions additionally measure relative forward error; do not mistake it for physical-world correctness.
Use 0.5,1,2 times a predeclared calibration time statistic as sensitivity deadlines if needed; identify one primary deadline in advance. These are hardware-specific computational deadlines, not beneficiary promises. Include cold setup and hot reuse targets separately.
Avoid pseudoreplication: numerous RHS on four matrices are four matrix contexts, not hundreds of independent sectors. Give per-matrix results and paired timing uncertainty. Calibration bins have finite-sample intervals; a low Brier score alone does not prove calibration.
Readiness construction adds value only when qualified lead time and repeated demand amortize setup. The strong threshold is N*=ceil(B/(c_uncached-c_cached)) when denominator positive, with comparable quality. If an adaptive cache baseline matches, report no distinct foresight advantage.

SIMULATION PROTOCOL PROPOSAL (NOT RESULTS)

Use measured setup/solve/verify/restore distributions as runtime primitives. Keep demand, adoption, competitor arrival, imitation, and capture assumptions in a separate config and label all resulting utility model-dependent. Do not silently rescale millisecond measurements to days. If day-scale scenarios are desired, introduce an explicitly assumed engineering-delay distribution separate from measured numerical setup.
Freeze finite test seeds and six regimes: nominal calibrated signal; uninformative signal; costly premature construction; delayed/absent demand; fast imitation/competitor catching up; shifted signal-demand relation. Give all policies the same contemporaneous signals/calibration records and same construction actions. A perfect-future policy is an oracle upper bound only.
Policy set: forecast-only/no construction; constructive forecast-guided build-or-wait; fixed wait-and-act; static expert (same construction allowed); adaptive break-even rent-or-build; a strong same-information dynamic/adaptive competitor. No method-specific latent variables. Allow strong competitor to infer from the same signals, or its inferiority is merely access asymmetry.
Use common exogenous scenario draws for all policies; paired replay estimates simulator effects conditional on the model, not real causal adoption effects. If simulating intervention on adoption, state structural equation and sensitivity; prefer exogenous adoption and endogenous qualified delivery to minimize arbitrary feedback assumptions.
VOI=E_Z[max_a E(U|I,Z)]-max_a E(U|I)-acquisition cost, with acquisition delay cost included. Better Brier can coexist with negative VOI, since information may not change the optimal action or can arrive after the readiness deadline.
Crucial: imitation does not erase a beneficiary's CPU savings. It changes the strongest alternative and thus incremental advantage, or affects a separate assumed private-capture metric. Price compression must not be presented as loss of scientifically useful output. Report accepted deliveries, total runtime, and latency first; economic conversion is sensitivity only.
Full cost ledger must distinguish observed cash (possibly zero incremental paid spend), unmetered assistant inference, engineering/review labor estimates, paid compute assumptions, failed attempts, recurring maintenance, numerical runtime, and policy overhead. Do not call unobserved or bundled inference free. Do not double count agent time as human labor and measured CPU time unless they are distinct cost bases.
