# One provider. One useful improvement. One earned next experiment.

**Status: proposed, uncommissioned.** This is an operational research checklist, not a legal contract, financing offer, provider instruction, or spending authorization.

## The deal to investigate

A willing computing provider offers a public-input workload and a credible baseline. Alpha investigates a removable restriction. An independent evaluator accepts or rejects the resulting method under prespecified outcome and cost rules. If the agreed benefit is established, a capped portion is compensated as a precisely described future service. A separate mission runs through that actual delivery.

The provider keeps the negotiated majority of the benefit. The network receives neither ownership of the provider nor an unlimited claim to its hardware. A 25% share is an illustration; commercial terms must be negotiated using alternatives and full costs.

## Before work starts

Select a provider who confirms that the workload actually matters and that the best readily available method has not already removed the opportunity. Prefer a public numerical/software workload with stable, independently checkable outputs. Reject confidential data, customer credentials, unbounded production changes, and workloads whose success cannot be measured credibly.

Name the owner/controller, provider representative, construction operator, and evaluator. Distinct agents or addresses are not evidence of organizational independence. Establish who controls tests and metering and disclose conflicts. Pay evaluators for valid negative assessments as well as favorable ones.

Freeze the descendant's useful outcome before earning the allocation. State whether uninterrupted execution is mandatory or whether checkpointing is genuinely permitted. Do not silently weaken the task to match a convenient reward.

## The minimum agreement

The parties must complete: primary workload and quality; baseline and strong alternative; rights and licensing; seed exposure; source/environment identity; metering and attribution; treatment of overhead and failures; share and cap; service-unit conversion; exact hardware/software and memory; delivery windows and priority; exclusivity and concurrency; expiry, interruptions and remedies; descendant acceptance; public evidence permissions; and review/approval identities.

Historical saved CPU time is not storable. A later service credit creates a provider obligation that requires a later reservation. Record it as earned-but-undelivered until fulfillment. Do not recognize the face value as cash or profit.

## Epoch A: construction and evidence

1. Reproduce the real incumbent and the strongest relevant readily available alternative.
2. Let the configured agent investigate alternative formulations within its authorized tools. Record what the investigator supplied; do not give the winning method and claim autonomous discovery.
3. Keep unsuccessful attempts, actual model/tool resources, and human time.
4. Freeze the candidate and fresh confirmation design. Randomize matched-run order in an isolated resettable workload; shared services need carryover/interference-aware design.
5. Assess every required output and the full resource vector. Choose fixed-work or fixed-resource-window accounting as primary; do not collect twice on one gain.
6. The evaluator and provider accept or reject the qualified gain. Close the performance tranche. Only then mark performance-based credit earned under its agreed terms.

A strong library implementation is allowed to win. If it already supplies the provider's desired outcome, there may be no incremental commercial opportunity. That is a valid result, not a reason to weaken the baseline.

## Epoch B: deliver and use capacity

Obtain a provider-authoritative, exclusive reservation for the named descendant. Bind image, hardware class, CPU/accelerator limits, memory, data movement, region, time, priority, expiry, and current source.

Run the actual installed recipient through that provider allocation. An offline simulation, a command that prints a quota, or a dashboard screenshot cannot establish delivery. Independently observe resource use and preserved provider service. Debit consumed resources even if the mission fails. Reconcile unused, expired and disputed balances.

Inspect and independently check the descendant artifact. Record whether it is useful to the intended beneficiary, not just whether execution exited successfully.

## Final report

Report five statuses separately: technical improvement; net release; earned entitlement; actual delivery; descendant outcome. Then report the seed resources, provider opportunity cost, auxiliary resources, unpaid liabilities, support, taxes/reserves where relevant, and both participants' alternatives. Unknowns remain unknown.

A positive exchange-shaped result does not establish seed recovery. A resource-positive exchange does not automatically establish monetary profitability. One pilot does not demonstrate improving discovery skill across missions or token appreciation.

## Gate decisions

- No credible provider workload or primary checker: do not start the commercial pilot.
- Better ordinary method already adequate: retain/adopt it and decline unjustified discovery spending.
- Quality regression or unconfirmed gain: no performance-based earned credit.
- Real gain but missing agreement: technical success only, not resource acquisition.
- Accepted credit but no suitable calendar: owed but undelivered; renegotiate or expire under agreed terms.
- Delivery but failed descendant: record consumed resources and failed mission, not useful capacity growth.
- Passed descendant but seed costs unrecovered: mechanism evidence, not self-financing.
- New public product or native settlement: separate commissioning and actual receipt evidence.

## Public completion statement to work toward

“Here was the provider's original restriction. Here is what Alpha actually discovered and built. Here is the independent outcome and resource comparison. Here is the service the provider owed and actually delivered. Here is the separate mission completed using that delivery. Here is the full cost, what each party retained, and where the method should not be reused.”

Do not fill this statement with proposed, simulated, or forecast events and present it as completed.
