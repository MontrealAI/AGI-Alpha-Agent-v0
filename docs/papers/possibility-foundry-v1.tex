\section*{Abstract}
Can an enterprise construct an enabling capability that makes a family of previously unaffordable investigations feasible? We study a bounded version of this question in an executable, synthetic spectroscopy laboratory. The enterprise receives fixed scientific requests, an initial 64-channel scanner, and a disclosed construction grammar. It acquires 24 noisy reference spectra, evaluates 60 combinations of measurement design, observation count, and calibration, then emits and executes a reusable instrument. The selected capability combines 16 data-dependent point measurements, affine calibration, and a learned linear decoder. Its 20-exposure operating cost becomes 26.056 exposures per single-session mission after allocating all 15,504 discovery and verification exposures across the frozen campaign.

At the declared budgets, the capability makes 1,678 of 2,048 missions feasible, compared with zero for the original scanner. Total campaign exposures fall from 174,080 to 66,704, a 61.68\% reduction. Transfer is partial: the unchanged instrument passes 204 of 512 withheld paired-change investigations. An independently authored construction policy and an expert-derived analytical software comparator discover the identical instrument with lower search costs; the proposed exhaustive policy therefore does not win the builder comparison. Calibration removal reduces feasible missions to 518; a mismatched decoder reduces them to zero. Unseen narrow spectral lines defeat the instrument on all 128 stress cases.

The emitted first capability also acquires the observations used to construct a temporal rate-identification capability. Search selects a three-time-point, offset-exponential estimator, which passes all 128 fresh temporal tasks. Those tasks are affordable conditional on an existing parent capability, but not when the entire parent discovery cost is charged to this small successor campaign. The experiment executes through the released AGI ALPHA workbench and an unchanged Jobs draft compiler. Native spectroscopy admission through Manager/Fleet, the held newer Harness integration, contract execution, physical validation, external human-expert comparison, and the \(\$100\rightarrow\$10{,}000\) commercial target remain unestablished. The contribution is an auditable construction-and-evaluation mechanism and a measured change in a defined resource frontier, not a demonstration of open-ended invention or realized profit.

\begin{claimbox}[The result to remember]
\textbf{A constructed instrument changes the affordable work set. A competent alternative builder can construct the same instrument more cheaply.} Both findings are part of the result.
\end{claimbox}

\section{The productive object is a possibility}

An enterprise ordinarily receives a task and searches for an answer. Here, it receives a portfolio for which its existing means of investigation are too expensive. The decision variable changes: it must choose what reusable means to construct before returning to the portfolio.

The central question is therefore:
\begin{quote}\large
Can an enterprise discover what it needs to become, construct that missing capability, and make an entire family of previously impractical missions worth pursuing?
\end{quote}
This paper gives an executed, deliberately narrow answer. Within a supplied digital laboratory and construction grammar, a program selects and constructs a lower-cost measurement interface. It then demonstrates which fixed requests cross an affordability threshold, which remain outside it, and what happens when the constructed capability is removed.

The experiment separates three propositions that are easily conflated. First, a system can select a capability rather than receive its final design. Second, that capability can change a portfolio's feasible work set. Third, the particular search policy can outperform other capable builders. Our evidence supports the first two within the declared scope and rejects the third against the comparators used here.

\subsection{Relation to the preceding research}

Writer \#4's \emph{Transformational Autonomous Enterprises} examines supplied-capability inheritance with fresh checks for different missions \citep{writer4}. The new decision here is the instrument to construct: its measurement locations, associated subspace dimension, calibration mode, and decoder are selected from observations and comparative evaluation. The final instrument and temporal successor are not entered as winning blueprints.

\emph{Invent the Economy} treats the joint technical and commercial conditions that make a new economic possibility viable \citep{inventeconomy}. This experiment holds task requirements and resource rules fixed. It does not construct buyers, agreements, demand, prices, or financing. Its measured output is a resource-constrained investigation frontier. The scientific subtitle names the broader question of economic opportunity; the demonstrated quantity is not a market valuation.

\subsection{Contributions and claim limits}

The deliverable contains a reproducible capability-construction mechanism, emitted programs, a complete proposal history, fixed evaluation partitions, causal interventions, a second construction, and an execution record from released project code. It also contains the full failure evidence.

The distinctive contribution is the combination of those elements around a portfolio-level frontier test. Principal-component reconstruction, regularization, greedy experimental design, affine calibration, exponential fitting, and program emission are established methods. We do not claim a new sensing theorem, an exhaustive priority claim, a general scientific inventor, or a production-admitted autonomous enterprise.

\section{Scientific foundations}

Autonomous scientific experimentation has substantial prior art. Robot Scientist Adam combined hypothesis generation, experimental execution, and formalized records \citep{king2009automation}. A mobile robotic chemist demonstrated autonomous experimental search using laboratory equipment \citep{burger2020robotic}. Coscientist combined language-model planning, documentation, code, and experimental automation \citep{boiko2023coscientist}. These systems establish that automated scientific loops are not new. Our focal variable is the reusable measurement capability selected before a portfolio is evaluated.

Program-synthesis and library-learning research already studies reusable computational structure. DreamCoder learns libraries and search mechanisms across tasks \citep{ellis2021dreamcoder}; Stitch synthesizes abstractions from supplied primitives \citep{bowers2023stitch}. That distinction matters here: autonomous composition inside a declared grammar does not imply that the grammar, physics, or fundamental algorithms were invented autonomously.

Measurement choice can be expressed through explicit objectives, as in experimental-design research \citep{chaloner1995design}. Reduced measurements can also support signal reconstruction under suitable structural conditions \citep{candes2006stable}. The present decoder uses an empirical low-rank model and ridge inversion. It is not the sparse-recovery procedure in that theorem, and its performance does not inherit the theorem's guarantee.

Physical sensing further depends on illumination, throughput, saturation, and signal-dependent noise. Multiplexing advantages can change or disappear under those constraints \citep{ratner2007multiplex}. We therefore use a transparent digital exposure model, bound measurement-row norms, charge complementary signed readings separately, and include a signal-dependent-noise stress condition. Those precautions delimit the simulation; they do not establish an optical advantage.

\section{A measurable opportunity frontier}

\subsection{Tasks, capabilities, and resource limits}

Let a fixed portfolio be \(\mathcal T=\{T_i\}_{i=1}^{N}\). Task \(T_i\) specifies an input distribution, a required output, a quality predicate \(Q_i\), and a resource allowance \(b_i\). A capability \(c\) is an executable acquisition-and-reconstruction program together with its fitted parameters and a source identity. It does not include a transferable permission to execute a new consequential action.

The first campaign contains \(N=2{,}048\) missions. Three static families contain 512 missions each; a withheld paired-change family contains another 512. A static mission requires one sample session; a paired mission requires two. With \(v_i\in\{1,2\}\),
\[
S=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_i=2{,}560.
\]
Single-session tasks have a 28-exposure allowance; paired tasks have a 56-exposure allowance. Those limits and the quality requirements are fixed before scored evaluation.

Let \(D(c)\) be the complete counted discovery charge and \(u(c)\) the operating exposures per sample session. The declared equal-per-session allocation gives
\begin{equation}
K_i(c)=v_i\left(u(c)+\frac{D(c)}{S}\right).
\label{eq:allocation}
\end{equation}
The observed affordable frontier is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal F(c;\mathcal T)=
\left\{i:Q_i\!\left(c,T_i\right)=1\ \land\ K_i(c)\leq b_i\right\}.
\label{eq:frontier}
\end{equation}
This is a finite observed set. A failed attempt is not a theorem that the task is impossible, and membership on one sample is not a distribution-wide quality guarantee.

\subsection{Conservation of the shared charge}

The allocation has a simple audit property:
\begin{equation}
\sum_iK_i(c)=D(c)+S\,u(c).
\label{eq:conservation}
\end{equation}
Every failed proposal, reference acquisition, and current verification charged to \(D(c)\) appears once in the campaign total. Paired missions receive twice the fixed-cost allocation because they use the instrument twice. No hypothetical future customer base is used: all 2,560 sample sessions are actually evaluated.

We also report the operating-only frontier, which asks what is affordable after construction is treated as sunk. Keeping it separate prevents a cheap operating number from concealing an expensive discovery process.

\subsection{Break-even conditions}

Against an initial instrument with cost \(u_0\), the constructed instrument reduces campaign exposure use when
\[
D(c)+S\,u(c)<S\,u_0.
\]
For the observed \(D=15{,}504\), \(u=20\), and \(u_0=68\), resource parity occurs at 323 sample reuses. Meeting the stricter 28-exposure fully loaded per-session allowance requires at least 1,938 sample reuses under the declared allocation. These are exposure-accounting thresholds, not predictions of sales, demand, or dollars.

At small reuse counts the fixed cost dominates. A framework that reports only mature steady-state use would miss precisely the construction burden this research is meant to test.

\section{The digital discovery laboratory}

\subsection{Signal and measurement model}

The laboratory represents a spectrum \(x\in\mathbb R^{64}\) on a normalized wavelength grid. Development spectra contain a nonnegative baseline and three smooth Gaussian components. Their centers, amplitudes, and widths vary within the frozen ranges in the executable generator. The system must reconstruct the full spectrum, not merely return one scalar.

For a nonnegative measurement row \(a\), a specimen exposure has the form
\begin{equation}
y=g\,a^\mathsf Tx+b+\epsilon,
\qquad
\epsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2),
\label{eq:measurement}
\end{equation}
where \(g\sim U[0.85,1.15]\), \(b\sim U[-0.04,0.04]\), and \(\sigma=0.006\). Gain and offset are fixed within a sample session and independently redrawn for another session. A two-exposure signed row measures its nonnegative positive and negative parts separately and subtracts them. Both noise draws and both exposures are retained.

Rows satisfy \(\|a\|_2=1\). This prevents free algebraic amplification. It does \emph{not} equalize photon dose, optical throughput, or illumination time. In particular, equal \(L_2\) norms need not imply equal \(L_1\) throughput. An exposure is a unit of this digital detector model.

Calibration is also constructed. The grammar allows no calibration, two dark readings, or affine calibration using two dark and two known-standard readings. Standards are developer-supplied measurement primitives. The constructor selects their use; it does not discover metrological traceability. The affine mode costs four exposures in every new session. Calibration observations are not inherited from unrelated sessions.

\subsection{Reference acquisition and learned reconstruction}

The constructor acquires 24 reference spectra through the same noisy affine full scanner. Each reference costs \(64+4=68\) exposures, for a total of 1,632. The constructor receives those measured reconstructions rather than simulator truth. Ground truth remains available to the synthetic evaluator for scoring.

The mean \(\mu\) and principal basis \(B\) are fitted to the noisy reference library. For an \(m\)-row measurement matrix \(A\), the associated rank is \(r=\min(m,16)\). This rank is selected indirectly through the chosen measurement count; it is not a separately invented representation dimension. The emitted decoder is
\begin{equation}
\hat x=\mu+B\left((AB)^\mathsf T AB+\lambda I\right)^{-1}
(AB)^\mathsf T\left(\tilde y-A\mu\right),
\qquad \lambda=10^{-4},
\label{eq:decoder}
\end{equation}
where \(\tilde y\) is the corrected observation vector.

The empirical reference library places 99\% of its centered variance in ten principal components. This is a diagnostic about the sampled library, not a guarantee for every future spectrum. Narrow-line stress cases deliberately violate that useful representation.

\section{What is autonomous, and what is supplied}

The strongest supported autonomy claim is \emph{bounded executable capability composition}. After the source/protocol freeze, the program acquires references, diagnoses the declared resource conflict, constructs alternatives, selects an instrument from validation evidence, emits it, executes it on new requests, and uses it in a second construction without a developer selecting the winning candidate.

The developer supplies the scientific setting, distributions, primitive algorithms, grammar, budgets, standards, scientific objectives, and evaluators. The system does not invent the meaning of spectroscopy, propose a new physical law, or decide which human goals are valuable. It discovers a design choice and fitted implementation within those boundaries.

\section{Protocol and capable comparisons}

\subsection{Frozen requirements and procedural separation}

The protocol and all five laboratory source files were frozen at 19:48:32 UTC on 23 October 2026, before the first scored campaign. The protocol SHA-256 is:
\begin{quote}\small\ttfamily
\seqsplit{312cff24f05a8ab5744850e83cc611e290c079a75ea01144b41446797aa92162}
\end{quote}
The freeze is an internal content commitment, not external preregistration. Developers know the generator and seeds. Builder functions receive the permitted references and validation cases; code inspection verifies that held-out cases are generated and evaluated only after the selected plan and emitted program are frozen. This is procedural separation rather than a security sandbox or externally blinded trial.

The selection portfolio contains four cases for each static family, twelve total. A candidate qualifies with at least eleven passes and at least three in every family. Among qualifying candidates, the policy minimizes operating exposure cost, then validation reconstruction error, then canonical candidate identifier. If none qualifies, the program emits a clearly unqualified fallback. Twelve fresh static checks are then charged separately. All three selected builders pass those twelve checks.

Every static mission requires a full reconstructed spectrum with relative \(L_2\) error at most 0.08, plus a family-specific requirement:

The paired-change family introduces a different inference objective and a new component whose location is not restricted to the three customary centers. It receives no bespoke instrument rebuild. It remains within the same simulated spectroscopy environment; this is not a claim of transfer across unrelated physical domains.

\subsection{Builders, not deliberately weak solvers}

The original scanner is the initial apparatus, not the only baseline. Two additional builders receive the same measured library, validation access, construction grammar, shared-infrastructure rights, and 25,000-exposure cap.

The \textbf{independent agent builder} is a deterministic policy authored by a separate AI agent before results were revealed. It searches candidates by cost, completes the least-cost tier containing a qualifying design, and selects by validation error. This is an actual separately authored builder exercise; it is not a general-purpose online LLM-agent benchmark.

The \textbf{expert-derived analytical comparator} orders the same candidate space by cost and a first-order error approximation derived from the reference library, then stops at the first qualified design. It is a software proxy. No human spectroscopy expert participated, and the requested strong human-expert contest therefore remains unperformed.

Both comparators can construct and reuse the same kinds of infrastructure as the foundry. Their selected artifacts happen to be identical to the foundry's artifact. This coincidence is informative: the main advantage lies in acquiring a reusable instrument, not in a unique search intelligence demonstrated by the exhaustive policy.

\subsection{Controls and stress}

The apparatus-removal contrast returns to the original scanner and assigns it zero discovery overhead, favoring the control. It does not forbid alternative reconstruction strategies in the capable-builder comparisons.

Two component interventions retain the selected design: remove affine calibration, or circularly mismatch the decoder columns with the unchanged measurement rows. The latter keeps detector costs identical while breaking the learned measurement-to-spectrum correspondence.

Two predefined stress conditions test the model boundary. One adds previously unseen narrow spectral lines. The other changes both specimen and bright-standard noise variance by adding \(0.0025\) times the relevant signal. This is a synthetic heteroscedastic Gaussian stress, not literal photon-count validation.

\[
D(\COne)=1{,}632+816+12{,}816+240=15{,}504.
\]
Every rejected candidate remains included. The same final instrument is found after 42 candidate evaluations by the independent builder and after 39 by the analytical comparator.

\section{A successor that constructs different work}

\subsection{From a spectrum interface to a temporal instrument}

The second capability estimates a temporal decay rate. A controlled trace has
\begin{equation}
x(t)=x_{\mathrm{background}}+\alpha e^{-kt}m,
\label{eq:temporal}
\end{equation}
where \(m\) is a supplied smooth spectral marker and the static projected background is unknown to the estimator. The parent instrument supplies reconstructed spectra at proposed times. A fixed marker projection converts those spectra into temporal readouts.

The developer supplies the generator, marker, rate range, and estimator primitives. The system constructs a new executable composition by choosing times and an estimator from observations. This is different work from static spectral reconstruction; it is still bounded composition in a specified scientific family.

Construction first acquires six controlled traces at seven allowed times:
\[
0,\ 0.5,\ 1,\ 2,\ 3.5,\ 5,\ 8.
\]
The actual emitted parent module performs these 42 acquisitions. At 20 exposures per snapshot, construction data cost 840 exposures. Six time subsets are combined with two estimator primitives: a zero-background log-linear fit and a grid fit of an exponential with nonnegative amplitude and unknown nonnegative background.

All twelve candidates reuse the same acquired traces, so they add computation but no new detector exposures. Their 41,436 grid-point evaluations and 36 log-linear fits are separately recorded. Known controlled training rates are provided as calibration labels. Fresh evaluation rates are never supplied to the estimator.

\subsection{Selection, execution, and current evidence}

Six of twelve candidates fail the all-six-traces construction rule. The selected program uses times \(\{0,2,8\}\) and the offset-exponential grid estimator. The emitted temporal program is executed on 128 fresh traces. All 128 meet the fixed 15\% relative-rate-error requirement.

The child artifact names the parent program's content hash. Its own source, plan, acquired traces, candidate outcomes, and fresh task results are recorded separately. Historical knowledge and source identity transfer; a parent's evidence is not relabeled as a current child check, and no execution authority is inherited.

\subsection{The ancestor cost changes the answer}

The successor operates with three parent acquisitions per mission, costing 60 exposures. Its construction overhead is \(840/128=6.5625\), making the marginal fully loaded cost 66.5625 per task, below the 120-exposure allowance.

If the parent must be built from scratch for this small successor campaign, the correct charge is
\[
60+\frac{15{,}504+840}{128}=187.6875,
\]
which exceeds the same allowance. The marginally affordable count is \(128/128\); the de-novo count is \(0/128\). Ancestor costs do not disappear simply because the artifact is called a successor.

For a combined history that already executed the full first campaign, the parent discovery is recorded once: 66,704 first-campaign exposures plus 840 child-construction and 7,680 child-operation exposures gives 75,224. This combined total is a different accounting question from a new 128-task business starting from nothing.

\subsection{A causal construction control with a precise limit}

The same seven-time-point constructor, using the original full scanner, stops after 1,768 exposures before exceeding the 1,800 construction cap. It has only three complete traces and a partial fourth; the next 68-exposure snapshot would exceed the cap. The record says \emph{construction budget exhausted}, not \emph{no solution exists}.

The intended 42 full-scanner snapshots would cost 2,856 exposures, compared with the parent's 840: a 70.59\% reduction in this fixed data-acquisition routine. However, an alternative constructor that directly chose three times for six traces would cost \(6\times3\times68=1{,}224\) and fit the construction cap. Our ablation does not exclude that alternative. The three-snapshot full-scanner operating cost would still be 204 per temporal task, above the 120-exposure allowance.

This distinction prevents a truncated routine from being mistaken for a lower bound. The evidence establishes a useful construction dependency and lower data-acquisition cost in the specified routine, not the necessity of this exact inheritance mechanism for all temporal science.

The predefined target is at most \(\$100\) in total attributable cost producing at least \(\$10{,}000\) in genuinely realizable incremental value. This package establishes neither a verified executable offer nor a settled payment. Its recorded proceeds and offers are zero, and its complete monetary cost is unknown. The existing instrument can make the marginal successor campaign affordable because a productive asset is already available. That is a legitimate conditional result. A new venture without that asset must include the ancestor construction bill, and fails the stated successor allowance in this experiment.

\section{Limitations and falsifiable next evidence}

\textbf{Finite construction space.} The system selects and instantiates supplied methods. Broader capability classes, new primitive discovery, and open-ended scientific invention are outside the evidence.

\textbf{One discovery realization.} Fresh mission cases are numerous, but only one reference-library/selection-seed pipeline is evaluated. The replay uses the same seeds. Independent runs with new construction libraries and new validation portfolios are needed before generalizing search reliability.

\textbf{Partial transfer.} The withheld paired-change family has 39.84\% success. The narrow-line stress has zero success. A future capability must improve those outcomes on a newly frozen evaluation bank; tuning to these disclosed failures would be exploratory.

\textbf{Comparison strength.} Both software builders are allowed shared infrastructure and beat exhaustive discovery cost. However, no independent human domain expert or unrestricted contemporary tool-using agent has contested the design. A stronger builder could discover a cheaper or more accurate interface beyond this grammar.

\textbf{Synthetic measurement resources.} A detector exposure is not a physical unit of energy, photon dose, elapsed laboratory time, or money. Additive Gaussian noise and the affine session model omit drift, wavelength-dependent gain, actuation costs, detector saturation, spectral throughput, and calibration-traceability burdens.

\textbf{Specific scientific outputs.} Full-spectrum reconstruction is mandatory here. A mission requiring only a scalar could admit a cheaper direct estimator. The frontier cannot be transferred to such a different specification without a fresh comparison.

\textbf{Small development bank.} Twelve validation tasks and twelve fresh checks do not certify population-level reliability. The candidate-history transparency permits scrutiny of selection overfitting, but does not eliminate it.

\textbf{Construction-control scope.} The successor ablation fixes a seven-snapshot data-acquisition routine. It does not compare every possible temporal constructor. An explicit cheaper three-snapshot alternative fits the construction cap, although its full-scanner per-mission operating cost remains too high.

\textbf{Execution and authority.} Released Alpha orchestration and real draft compilation are executed. Native spectroscopy semantics for Manager/Fleet, installed execution, the held newer Harness lane, contract calls, and applicable token use are not established by those executions. Artifact identity is not scientific truth or legal authority.

\textbf{Commercial validity.} Demand, willingness to pay, accepted deliverables, rights to physical designs, realized proceeds, and complete attributable dollars remain unmeasured. The language of an enterprise does not remove those empirical obligations.

\begin{claimbox}[The next decisive physical experiment]
Freeze a bench instrument's true exposure time, photon/dose constraints, wavelength-dependent noise and calibration process. Give an independent domain expert and a conventional tool-using agent the same construction rights. Acquire new calibration specimens, construct the chosen design, then test untouched scientific requests and an untouched temporal family. Record all human and provider costs and evaluate value only against verified demand. Success or failure on that experiment would carry a meaning this digital study cannot supply.
\end{claimbox}

\section{Conclusion}

The Possibility Foundry turns a supplied construction space into an executed choice of means. The selected instrument makes 1,678 fixed missions affordable, preserves the cost of every rejected proposal, and supports construction of a different temporal capability. It also loses to capable alternative builders on discovery efficiency, fails many withheld investigations, fails an entire narrow-line stress set, and cannot make its small successor campaign affordable from scratch.

Those boundaries make the result useful. A productive capability is valuable only relative to the work it enables, the resources its construction consumes, the failures it retains, and the evidence each new mission earns. The experimental product is a changed, inspectable set of possibilities.
