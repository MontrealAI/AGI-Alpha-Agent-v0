The contribution

A changed action space.
A comparison that can fail.

APERTURE constructs a measurement operator from a bounded mathematical language, selects observations, fits a declared model, and quarantines compromised measurement groups when permitted evidence arrives. A new task can change the representation, likelihood, and execution structure.

The study freezes its implementation and protocol before revealing a different scenario family. It compares eight methods across 96 cases, with acquisition, verification, recovery, waiting, and compute kept in their own units. The strongest conventional construction control has the same information, primitives, and operational budgets.

The result is an inspectable implementation and an informative negative comparison. Neither autonomous invention nor an advantage over all unknown futures is demonstrated. The conventional control is a supplied analytical program, not an observed human-expert trial.

Costs remain explicit

Instrument units, scored candidates, solver calls, CPU time, and assumed switching tariffs are distinct. Removed observations stay charged. Human engineering effort, provider compute, long-term maintenance, and monetary prices are unknown.

“Alpha” denotes a stated task-level advantage. The primary qualified-coverage advantage here is zero. No revenue, token price, investment return, or commercial adoption follows from these experiments.
