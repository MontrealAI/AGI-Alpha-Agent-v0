\section{From digital capability to useful physical capacity}
\label{sec:physical-progression}
The longer-term objective is a succession of productive capabilities: digital methods help construct scientific instruments; qualified instruments help manufacture dependable modules; manufactured modules support operating infrastructure; that infrastructure delivers useful energy and other services. Each transition requires a new physical construction and a new acceptance argument. A software certificate cannot supply missing materials, establish an instrument's calibration, finance a factory or prove an energy balance.

The USD~100 total attributable production-cost / more-than-USD~10,000 realizable incremental-value challenge remains explicit and \textbf{UNPROVEN}. It applies first to the bounded digital deliverable and its documented realization mechanism. It is not a claim that a USD~100 software run builds industrial infrastructure. Later projects need their own complete budgets, financing, lawful rights, independent counterparties and delivery windows. Estimated future energy savings cannot retrospectively establish today's commercial result.

\subsection{Three layers of claim}
\textbf{Demonstrated now:} the finite compiler constructs and uses synthetic measurement programs, exposes conventional ties and losses, and checks changed inheritance. Its resource evidence concerns declared acquisition units, logical work and observed software execution.

\textbf{Testable next:} a small, separately authorized low-voltage electrical test fixture could replace one supplied synthetic channel model. A concrete first specimen is a switched resistance/contact network with reference loads, a calibrated meter or acquisition instrument and controlled fault insertions. Before testing, fix the required fault decisions, environmental range, error budget, allowed probes and comparison methods. Physically assemble the fixture, record its bill of materials and labor, and evaluate blinded fault cases using an independent reference measurement. Failure of the assumed drift/error envelope must reject or rebuild the protocol. Calibration traceability requires a documented connection to reference measurements and an uncertainty account; traceability alone does not establish fitness for the decision \citep{nisttraceability}.

\textbf{Long-term ambition:} extend accepted measurement and production methods into manufacturing, infrastructure and useful energy service only when the next row's construction, resources, validation and economics have been established. The proposed sequence in Table~\ref{tab:physical-path} is one concrete development path, not a forecast or an already operating business.

\subsection{Physical production and commercial capacity must agree}
A useful inheritance might reduce repeated calibration design, inspection programming or process qualification. It cannot eliminate the next project's material flow, machine occupancy, energy input, labor or delivery bottleneck. Supply-chain readiness therefore has to be evaluated at the relevant production steps, including subcomponents and end-of-life requirements \citep{doesupply}. For a fixed time window, translate each constraint into complete deliverable units before comparing capacity:
\[
 Q_{\mathrm{accepted}}\leq
 \min\{Q_{\mathrm{materials}},Q_{\mathrm{tooling}},
 Q_{\mathrm{labor}},Q_{\mathrm{validation}},Q_{\mathrm{delivery}}\}.
\]
Realizable commercial quantity is further limited by independent executable demand. The same purchase offer, manufacturing slot or shared instrument hour cannot support several simultaneously promised deliveries. A successor can spend only resources actually available under its authority; prospective revenue is not collected financing.

Useful energy must be defined at the beneficiary's required boundary and quality. Electricity at an accepted voltage/frequency and heat at a required temperature are different services and must not be combined into an undifferentiated value total. Delivered energy is measured; avoided consumption is counterfactual. For the latter, compare baseline and post-change energy under comparable production, weather and operating conditions, as measurement-and-verification practice requires \citep{doemv}. Include auxiliary consumption, storage losses, downtime, upkeep and embodied construction costs in the appropriate physical and economic accounts, without subtracting losses twice from a quantity already measured at delivery.

The next physical decision is therefore modest and falsifiable: specify and cost the one instrument fixture, obtain the necessary permission and reference access, and test whether its independently verified decisions justify its full cost relative to existing equipment. Passing that test would establish one additional enabling capability. Manufacturing, infrastructure and energy service would each remain a further construction to earn.
